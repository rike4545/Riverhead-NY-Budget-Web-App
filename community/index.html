Community & tax base

Who and what the Town taxes: population sets the scale of demand for services, and the tax base — the assessed value of all taxable property — is what the levy is spread across.

Independent project·Updated September 8, 2026·● Current·Check data freshness →
💡In plain English
Who and what the Town of Riverhead taxes. Population sets the scale of demand for services; the tax base — the assessed value of all taxable property — is what the levy is spread across. A few large commercial properties carry an outsized share, which is why grievance settlements on them ripple onto everyone else's bill.
  • ›Population: roughly 36,000 residents — the number of people the Town’s services and budget must cover.
  • ›Tax base: the total value of taxable property. A few big commercial parcels carry an outsized share.
  • ›Why it matters: when a large taxpayer wins an assessment reduction, the same levy is re-spread onto everyone else — so the tax base’s health is a resident’s concern.

The tax base, explained

  • Implied from the audited debt-limit disclosure: the statutory debt limit is 7% of the Town's five-year-average full (market) valuation, and that limit is $495,782,621 — so the five-year-average full valuation is about $7.08 billion.
  • Riverhead assesses property at roughly 8% of market value — an assessed value of about $50,040 corresponds to a ~$600,000 market-value home (near the Town median).
  • Total assessed value barely moved in 2023 — up $380,869, or 0.05%, from 2022. The town-wide tax rate rose $2.409 per $1,000 of assessed value in 2023.
  • The Town’s outstanding general-obligation debt uses only 4.83% of its legal debt limit (about $23,950,000 of a $495,782,621 ceiling) — it carries a Moody’s Aa2 (Moody's Investors Service) rating.
  • Since a 1998 voter referendum, Riverhead levies a 2% real-estate transfer tax (the Community Preservation Fund) on sales above $150,000 (developed) / $75,000 (undeveloped).

Riverhead's assessment system is under chronic strain.

  • More than 300 tax-grievance lawsuits are filed against the Town every year, and settlements shift the burden onto other taxpayers.
  • A decades-old assessment error tied to the Friar's Head golf property produced tax spikes of up to ~160% for some residents in 2026.
  • Because assessed values sit at ~8% of market and are not regularly reassessed town-wide, small percentage swings move large dollar amounts.
