The Optimum cable franchise and Verizon

What Riverhead’s cable franchise with Optimum provides, what it brings the Town, when it is due for renewal, and whether Verizon or another provider could compete, as of November 10, 2026.

Independent, unofficial project·Data last refreshed November 11, 2026·Recently refreshed·See data update details →

In plain English

Optimum (formerly Cablevision) serves Riverhead under a ten-year franchise the Town Board approved on April 19, 2016. It pays the Town 5 percent of its cable revenue. The General Fund’s franchise revenue, which includes that fee, was $814,730 in 2025, down 11 percent since 2018. The franchise is not exclusive, so another provider such as Verizon could apply for its own, but nothing in the records shows one has.
  • Cable franchise: the Town’s agreement letting a cable company use Town roads and rights-of-way, in return for fees and services.
  • Franchise fee: a share of the company’s cable revenue paid to the Town. Federal law caps it at 5 percent.
  • PEG channels: public, educational and government access channels, such as the channel that airs Town Board meetings.

What the 2016 franchise provides

The Board held a public hearing on April 5, 2016 and adopted Resolution #279 on April 19, 2016, 5 to 0, authorizing the Supervisor to sign the renewal with CSC Acquisition-NY, Inc. (Cablevision), now Optimum, owned by Altice USA. At the hearing the company said: The proposed agreement before you is a renewal of cablevisions franchise for a term of ten (10) years. The agreement becomes effective upon confirmation by the New York Public Service Commission.

Franchise fee
“franchise fees at five percent on a broad basis of gross revenue definition. Right now it’s somewhere around eight hundred and sixty thousand dollars” (Cablevision’s Director of Government Affairs, hearing minutes)
Grants to the Town
“an upfront Grant of one hundred thousand dollars in support of paid access programing payable within sixty days of the effective date; a public education and government equipment purchase and installment, the purchase and installation of playback equipment and mounted cameras in this room at a value of fifty thousand dollars; a technical Grant of five thousand dollars” (Cablevision’s Director of Government Affairs, hearing minutes)
Senior discount
“fifteen percent off expanded basic and equivalent level of service plus fifteen percent off installs” (Cablevision’s Director of Government Affairs, hearing minutes)
Free service
“free basic cable service to at this point we’re still finalizing the final list. There’s around thirty-four locations between schools, libraries, fire department, police department and municipal buildings” (Cablevision’s Director of Government Affairs, hearing minutes)
Access channels
“the continuation of the airing of your two access channels. So you have here channel 20 which is the regional public access channel and then channel 22” (Cablevision’s Director of Government Affairs, hearing minutes)
Not exclusive
“Any cable company can come in as long as they have a franchise agreement with us can come in and give us service. So it’s not exclusive to them” (Councilman John Dunleavy, hearing minutes)

Sources: minutes of April 5, 2016 (hearing) and minutes of April 19, 2016 (Resolution #279). The minutes are the Town’s transcript; the agreement itself is not attached to them.

When it comes up for renewal

The minutes say the ten years start when the State Public Service Commission confirms the agreement. This site has not found the confirmation order, so it cannot give the end date. A term confirmed in 2016 or 2017 would end in 2026 or 2027: ask the Town for the date.

Federal law gives the Town two paths. In a formal renewal it can open a public proceeding in the window before the franchise ends:

“A franchising authority may, on its own initiative during the 6-month period which begins with the 36th month before the franchise expiration, commence a proceeding which affords the public in the franchise area appropriate notice and participation for the purpose of (A) identifying the future cable-related community needs and interests, and (B) reviewing the performance of the cable operator under the franchise during the then current franchise term.” 47 U.S.C. §546(a)

Or the two sides can negotiate informally, as they did last time: a cable operator may submit a proposal for the renewal of a franchise pursuant to this subsection at any time (47 U.S.C. §546(h)). News reports at the time said the last renewal took about three years of talks after the previous franchise expired in 2012.

This site found no renewal proceeding or vote on the franchise in the Town Board's 2025 and 2026 meeting records, through November 5, 2026.

Could Verizon come in?

The Town can let a second company in, but it cannot make one build. Federal law says a franchising authority may not grant an exclusive franchise and may not unreasonably refuse to award an additional competitive franchise (47 U.S.C. §541(a)(1)). A Board member said at the 2016 hearing that the franchise is not exclusive.

  • Asked about bringing FiOS to the East End, a Verizon spokesperson said, “It’s a simple no,” and the paper reported the company “isn’t planning to invest in any new build-outs.” The paper also reported that Riverhead’s earlier franchise with Cablevision included “a non-exclusivity deal allowing the towns to allow other cable companies to operate.” News report: Riverhead News-Review, November 4, 2014
  • A union and elected officials, including Riverhead’s Supervisor, asked the State Public Service Commission to order Verizon to bring FiOS to Suffolk. News report: RiverheadLOCAL, November 10, 2014

This site found no Town Board resolution, hearing or application in 2025 or 2026 about a Verizon cable franchise in Riverhead, and no current statement from Verizon about building FiOS here. Commercial availability websites give conflicting figures, so the page does not use them.

The 2025 channel move

Optimum moved the Town’s government channel from 22 to 1310 and the public access channels 20 and 115 to 1300 and 1302. The Town said the change was “initiated without prior notice.” Meetings remain available on the Town’s website.

News report: RiverheadLOCAL, August 26, 2025

The 2016 franchise covered “the continuation of the airing of your two access channels,” but the minutes do not say whether it fixes their channel numbers. A renewal is the Town’s chance to set that.

Questions residents may want to ask

  • When did the State Public Service Commission confirm the 2016 franchise, and when does it end?
  • Has the Town started the renewal review federal law allows, with public notice, to identify residents’ cable needs and review Optimum’s performance?
  • Will a renewal keep the 15 percent senior discount, the free service to Town buildings and schools, and channel placement the Town agrees to in advance?
  • Has the Town asked Verizon, or any other provider, whether it would apply for a competing franchise? Federal law lets the Town grant one but not require anyone to build.
  • Franchise revenue fell from $915,483 in 2018 to $814,730 in 2025. What does the Town expect as more residents drop cable, and does it plan for that in the budget?
