Covers activity through November 26, 2026; due November 30, 2026.

Not due yet as of October 28, 2026.

Contributions are Schedules A to C; transfers in, G; other in, D, E, I and L (in-kind gifts, other receipts, loans and refunds); spent, F; transfers out, H. Owed is Schedule N, the outstanding loans and bills each report repeats, so it is a balance, not new money. The State's open data holds itemized transactions only: unitemized contributions are not in these totals, and a committee missing from a report may have filed it with nothing to itemize. Contributions or loans over $1,000 received after October 19 and before the election are reported separately within 24 hours (Election Law §14-108) and are not in these reports. Source: Campaign Finance Disclosure Reports Data, data.ny.gov.

Town employee donor matches

A separate name-matching tool compares employee payroll records with disclosed individual donors. A name match is a lead for review, not proof of identity, influence, or wrongdoing.

Town Employee Donors

Cross-references Town payroll employees against individual campaign donors to the committees above. Matched by name only — this is disclosure context, not an accusation. Modest personal donations from Town employees to sitting or former officials are common and legal. A 2011 RiverheadLOCAL investigation found that a Town finance administrator was using town offices and equipment to conduct outside private business — a case that illustrates why understanding the financial relationships between Town employees and the officials who oversee their employment is a matter of public transparency.

What contribution limits apply?

Riverhead Town race — reference figures, not a current compliance determination

New York Election Law § 14-114 sets rules that depend on the race, donor relationship, and applicable election cycle. The Business Council of New York State published the following Riverhead reference figures as of 2022:

General election
Individual: $1,109.30
Family: $5,546.50
Democratic primary
Individual: $1,000.00
Family: $1,538.00
Republican primary
Individual: $1,000.00
Family: $2,000.75

Voter counts and limits can change. Do not use these historic reference amounts alone to allege a violation; check the current Suffolk County Board of Elections Comprehensive Limits Report and the underlying filing first. Source: Business Council of New York State, "NYS Campaign Contribution Limits".

  • Most individual donors: The ordinary limit is generally based on registered voters in the district × $0.05, subject to a $1,000 minimum.
  • Qualifying family donors: Certain close family members may be subject to a separate higher limit based on registered voters × $0.25, subject to a $1,250 minimum.
  • Candidate's own money: A candidate's personal contributions are generally not subject to the ordinary contribution cap for local town races. Different rules can apply under the State public campaign-financing program.
Sources, coverage & how to read this page

The page uses New York State's open campaign-finance data from data.ny.gov, with a local snapshot for initial display and live refresh controls for updated records. Coverage shown here is 2005–2026.

  • Candidate registrations and committee/filer records are separate records and may have different statuses.
  • Where historical committees are combined, the page notes the scope so a committee total is not mistaken for a single election total.
  • Missing activity in a snapshot does not necessarily mean no contribution occurred; check live data and source filings.
  • Employee-donor matches rely on names and can produce false positives or miss name variations. They are not findings of misconduct.
  • Contribution-limit figures are dated references. Current compliance requires the current limits and complete filing context.
