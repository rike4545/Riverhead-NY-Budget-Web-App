and receive information, comments and other evidence for the Town Board members to 
consider in deliberating upon the applications submitted.   
Pursuant to Riverhead Town Code § 301-312, with respect to a Special Permit, the Town 
Board may consider, material factors and matters including but not limited to suitability 
of the site and proposed use, sufficiency of lot size for the use, characteristics of the 
proposed use together with surrounding uses, adequacy of accessibility,  provision for 
emergencies, parking, design features to protect adjacent land uses,  provision for 
stormwater, sanitary and waste collection or dispos al, adequacy of existing municipal 
services, impact of construction activities, intensity of use within the zoning use district.  
Note that no single factor is to be considered dispositive and the foregoing factors are 
not exhaustive for the Town Board’s consideration.  
Pursuant to Riverhead Town Code § 301 -305, with respect to a Site Plan application 
which includes legal data, natural features of the site for development, existing 
structures and utilities, and details relative to the proposed development, the applicant 
shall present the site plan to the Town Board and the Town Board shall review the site 
plan application, the Planning Department written report and comments from any other 
agencies and receive comments from the public. The Town Board shall receive written 
comments for 10 days following the close of the public hearing.  Upon review of the 
application, recommendations from Planning staff and other agencies and departments 
and comments from the public, the Town Board may accept, deny or accept wit h 
modifications the plan presented.

ADDITIONAL COST TO OWNER.

NO COST TO OWNER.

Instructions for Completing Part 1 
Part 1 is to be completed by the applicant or project sponsor.  Responses become part of the application for approval or funding, 
are subject to public review, and may be subject to further verification. 
Complete Part 1 based on information currently available.  If additional research or investigation would be needed to fully respond to 
any item, please answer as thoroughly as possible based on current information; indicate whether missing information does not exist, 
or is not reasonably available to the sponsor; and, when possible, generally describe work or studies which would be necessary to 
update or fully develop that information. 
Applicants/sponsors must complete all items in Sections A & B.  In Sections C, D & E, most items contain an initial question that 
must be answered either “Yes” or “No”. If the answer to the initial question is “Yes”, complete the sub-questions that follow.  If the 
answer to the initial question is “No”, proceed to the next question. Section F allows the project sponsor to identify and attach any 
additional information.  Section G requires the name and signature of the applicant or project sponsor to verify that the information 
contained in Part 1is accurate and complete.

Introduction 
As part of the Riverhead Downtown Revitalization Projects, J. Petrocelli Riverhead Town Square, LLC (the 
“Applicant”),1 which was designated by the Town of Riverhead Town Board as the Master Developer for 
the Riverhead Town Square project,2 is proposing to construct a five-story, 94-key boutique hotel with 
amenity space, retail spaces/restaurants and hotel common spaces on the first floor, with parking 
underneath the building, to be known as the Peconic River Hotel (the “proposed Project”). The Peconic 
River Hotel is proposed to be located adjacent to and east of the proposed Riverhead Town Square along 
the south side of Main Street at 117-127 E. Main Street, in the hamlet of Riverhead, Town of Riverhead, 
Suffolk County, New York (the “Subject Property”). According to the Town, Riverhead Town Square is 
centrally located on the south side of Main Street, providing an anchor for the other identified projects set 
forth in the Riverhead Downtown Revitalization Projects. A hearing regarding a proposed tax lot line 
modification to consolidate a number of tax lots to better align the properties within the Town Square 
with their proposed future uses was held by the Town Planning Board at its meeting of February 19, 2026. 
The hearing was closed and no public comments were received. The proposed lot line modification was 
approved at the Planning Board meeting of March 5, 2026 (see further details below).  
In accordance with the State Environmental Quality Review Act (SEQRA) process conducted by the Town 
of Riverhead Town Board, a Part 1 – Environmental Assessment Form (EAF) has been prepared for the 
proposed Project and this document contains an analysis of the proposed Project’s consistency with the 
Town of Riverhead’s EAF Narrative and Determination of Non-Significance (“Negative Declaration”) for the 
proposed Riverhead Downtown Revitalization Projects, which is a multi-phased development that includes 
the proposed hotel as part of the Phase 1 development.

SEQRA Compliance History 
According to the Part 1 – EAF Narrative, the Town has spent several decades attempting to revitalize the 
downtown, which has been experiencing economic decline. While some renewal has begun to occur 
within the downtown, in March 2023, the Town of Riverhead adopted Article XLIVA – Downtown Riverfront 
Overlay District, which includes the Subject Property. This overlay district was created to help implement 
previous efforts, including the Comprehensive Master Plan, Downtown Riverhead Pattern Book and 
Downtown Riverfront Activation Plan, and “promote waterfront development to enhance recreation and 
tourism, and manage land uses to develop the district as a civic area that is people-friendly with active 
uses, additional entertainment facilities and events, specialty retail and restaurants.” Subsequent to the

SEQRA Consistency Analysis 
The subject application, The Peconic River Hotel, was considered and reviewed as one of the 
component parts of the “Riverhead Downtown Revitalization Pro jects” with the overall 
redevelopment plan encompassing more than ten (10) acres that will experience various degrees 
of disturbance. Accordingly, the project was considered a Type I action pursuant to SEQRA. The 
Town Square will be developed as an Upper a nd Lower Town Square, terraced with landscaped 
public space from Main Street and sloping down toward the Peconic River. The proposed 
developments serve to improve public access to the Peconic River and stimulate cultural, 
recreational, and commercial devel opment in t he Downtown . The proposed developments are 
needed to improve the Main Street area through increased public awareness of the Riverfront, 
improved public safety, flood attenuation, storm resiliency, historic building preservation and 
educational opportunities within an Urban Renewal Area, with development to be implemented 
as a three (3) phase project.  
 
The Town Board retained Jeffrey L. Seeman, CEP, to act as its environmental consultant to assist 
in reviewing the Downtown Redevelopment Projects pursuant to , and in compliance with 
SEQRA. The most critical issues that were identified by the Town Board during the SEQRA 
process were as follows: 
• Flooding and Proposed Mitigation  
• Water Use & Wastewater Capacities

The proposed development is located at Suffolk County Tax Map  numbers 600-129-1-13-15 and 
600-128-6-86.1, on the south side of East Main Street, also known as 117-127 East Main Street. The 
site is approximately 0.42 acres, adjacent to the Town Square , which is located to the west and 
East End Arts facility located immediately east of the proposed hotel site. 
The project  is described as a five -story, 69,738 sf mixed use building containing a first- floor 
restaurant/retail space and hotel common space, with four floors above for use as 94 hotel rooms. 
The lower level beneath the building will be utilized for nine (9) parking stalls reserved for hotel 
employees, laundry facilities , general storage, trash receptacles,  and mechanical spaces. Hotel 
guest parking will be provided at offsite locations through an agreement with the Town to utilize 
reserved grade level parking spaces north of Main Street (behind the Suffolk Theater) and once 
completed, also at the First Street Parking Garage.  
The earlier proposed development plan, subject to the Town of Riverhead’s “Downtown 
Revitalization” SEQRA review included a five -story mixed use building, with first floor  
retail/dining space and management offices for 76 hotel room s on floors 2 , 3 , and 4, with 12 
condominium units on floor 5. Parking to accommodate the condominiums was provided onsite 
beneath the building with hotel guest parking provided for offsite using hotel valet services. 
The revised Peconic River Hotel has a main entrance from Main Street, a first floor comprised  of 
a +/- 5,100 sf, 116-seat restaurant (including 20 bar seats) with a terrace, located at the building’s 
south end (riverfront) , a +/ - 800 sf/+ - 20-seat coffee shop, approximately 2,861 sf of publicly 
accessible retail space , with additional entrances from the Town Square (west)  to access  the 
common areas, hotel lobby, lounge/bar, kitchen and internal gym.  
The hotel rooms are described as having 26 rooms on the second floor, 28 rooms on the third floor, 
26 rooms on the fourth floor and 14 suites on the fifth floor. The fourth floor has some units with 
terraces that overlook Main Street and the fifth-floor suites each contain a balcony or terrace.   
An analysis of the changes between the earlier 88-unit hotel/condominium development and the 
current 94-unit hotel without condominiums was evaluated by the Town’s environmental 
consultant and included reviews of the Site Plan, architect’s drawings, the applicant’s May 2026 
“Consistency Analysis for the Proposed Peconic River Hotel at Riverhead Town Square ” and its 
attendant FEAF, last dated May 1, 2026, prepared by Christiana Kastalek, of VHB. 
As was originally decided by the Town Board acting as  SEQRA Lead Agency, the multiple of 
Downtown Revitalization Projects was collectively classified as a Type 1 SEQRA Action.  
The Downtown SEQRA process determined the best way forward was to intentionally segment 
the environmental review as permitted by SEQRA under Part 617.3 General Rules subsection (g)

(1),  because of the planned phasing of  projects, quantity and diversity of projects involved, each

For example, if a project was shovel  ready but had no funding its potential for environmental 
impacts might be de minimis or if that same project was co- dependent with other funded and 
shovel ready development then the environmental impacts might be greater. The SEQRA reviews 
found that riverfront projects were co -dependent because  the cornerstone of the SEQRA 
Findings’ negative declaration  determined that the Whole Action’s  flood mitigation was a key 
socio-economic benefit. Therefore, a segmented review supported the socio-economic benefits of 
flood mitigation that are the result  of enacting the Phase 1 developments  along the south side of 
Main Street , where elevations  would be raised and stormwater control infrastructure will 
mitigate the potential for flooding, outweighing less significant potential impacts.  
In February of 2025, the Town completed SEQRA for the Downtown Area improvements, which 
represented the review of each individual project and then reviewed the potential cumulative 
impacts generated by all the projects and evaluated what mitigation is needed . The Town also 
required each project be subjected to additional SEQRA assessment once site plans were prepared 
and submitted to the Planning Department. This second review is necessary to confirm 
consistency with the previously issued negative declaration, and/or if additional potential impacts 
warranted further mitigation.

The VHB Consistency Analysis for the Proposed Peconic River Hotel at Riverhead Town Square provides more 
detailed information for the proposed project’s SEQRA compl iance.  In summary the proposed 
Peconic River Hotel is determined to be consistent with the prior Riverhead  Downtown 
Revitalization Projects SEQRA assessment for the following reasons: 
(a) Flooding and Proposed Mitigation: The elevation of the first-floor level of the finished area 
is 13.5-ft. and combined with the proposed roof and site drainage system  designed for 
onsite containment for a 2-inch rainfall using 25 leaching galleys protects the building and 
surrounding area from flooding to the maximum extent possible. 
(b) Water Use & Wastewater Capacities : the earlier SEQRA assessment allotted 
approximately 35,000 GPD of water and wastewater for the 76 -unit hotel room and 12 -
unit condominium project. The revised 94 -unit hotel is estimated to require 
approximately 20,000 GPD of potable water and discharge approximately 16,568 GPD of 
wastewater, which is consistent with the Suffolk County Department of Health Services 
design requirements. Letters of  Availability for  confirmation are required from the 
Riverhead Water District and Riverhead Sewer District, prior to Site Plan approval.
