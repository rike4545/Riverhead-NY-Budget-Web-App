 Dear Honorable Supervisor Aguiar and Members of the Town Board:  
 
  
For more information visit our website at www.townofriverheadny.gov  
 
I am a certified environmental profession and have conducted SEQRA studies in Riverhead 
for more than twenty years. I have no professional interest in the proposed Nextera 
development an d if requested by the Town of Riverhead or the applicant to become 
professionally involved I shall respectfully decline. However, as a certified environment 
professional, I am bound by the Academy of Board Certified Environmental Professionals’ 
Standards of Professional Practice and Code of Ethics and therefore obligated to bring these 
concerns to your attention as well for the benefit of the two new members of the Lead 
Agency who may be unaware of the existing SEQRA record and review procedures under 
6NYCRR Part 617. 
 
A SEQRA Determination of Significance memorializes the potential for environmental 
impacts generated by an action. As intended under the law, the determination is a non -
political decision, free of prejudice and impartial. The consideration of tabled Resolution 
2019-827 by the Town Board as SEQRA Lead Agency may adversely influence the quality 
and integrity of SEQRA reviews conducted by the Riverhead Planning Board. Issuing a 
Negative Declaration of significance on Nextera, a Type I Action, will  cause a gap in the 
Planning Board’s SEQRA record and render previous SEQRA reviews incomplete, 
inconsistent and vulnerable to challenge. 
 
A negative declaration may damage both he Special permit issued for S -Power-1 as well as 
the validity and significanc e of the Town Board’s written comments to the New York State 
Public Service Commission prepared by Town Building and Planning Administrator 
Jefferson V. Murphree, AICP for the S-Power-2 solar project. 
 
On behalf of the Town of Riverhead, I have been profes sionally engaged for conduction 
SEQRA reviews on several solar projects. This includes the SEQRA review of the draft and 
final environmental impact statement and the Findings Statement of S -Power-1, a twenty 
MW solar facility in Calverton, preparation of t he Supplemental EIS and Supplemental 
Findings Statement for S -Power-1 on behalf of the Planning Board. On behalf of the Town 
Board, I have provided extensive review and comments to the Preliminary Scoping 
Statement for S -Power-2, a 36 MW commercial solar f acility planned for Calverton under 
review by the Public Service Commission pursuant to Article 10 procedures. 
 
In the aforementioned studies, there was no specific mention or identification of the Nextera 
22.9 MW project. Unbeknownst to the Planning Board  and the undersigned, on September 4, 
2018, the Nextera EAF Part I and Site Plan Application was filed with the town. The 
Planning Board’s adopted March 1, 2019 Supplemental EIS for S -Power-1, which required 
all proposed solar facilities in Riverhead be id entified, and the attendant Supplemental 
Findings were adopted without the inclusion of Nextera. The October 2018 written 
comments on S -Power-2 submitted to the NYSPC also lacked a reference to Nextera. The 
significance of these omissions is explained below.  
 
During the environmental review of S -Power-1, potential adverse impacts were identified, 
specifically growth inducing impacts such that the Planning Board imposed a covenant upon 
the gen-tie line and its easement restricting its use to the -Power-1 project only 20 MW as 
  
For more information visit our website at www.townofriverheadny.gov  
 
mitigation. There would be o opportunity for additional solar facilities to share the gen -tie 
easement for connection to the Edwards Avenue substation thus controlling the proliferation 
of solar facilities on the west side of Edwards Av enue in the 11933 -zip code until further 
projects were further analyzed under SEQRA. The Town Board cannot and must not issue a 
Negative Declaration on Nextera to nullify these mitigating measures.  
 
The Planning Board’s SEQRA review of the EIS for S -Power-1 required identification of all 
potential proposed solar developments in the Calverton area as a method to evaluate growth 
inducement and cumulative impacts. When the applicant’s EIS failed to include its own S -
Power-2 36 MW facility, the Planning Board as Lead Agency required preparation of a 
Supplemental EIS to correct the SEQRA record. Again, there was no specific identification 
of the Nextera project and thus the Planning Board having not included Nextera in its review, 
has no mention of Nextera in it s adopted Supplemental EIS for S -Power-1 resulting in an 
incomplete and flawed SEQRA record. 
 
The S -Power-1 Positive Declaration of Significance set a precedent for a determination of 
significance. When a Lead Agency is reviewing projects of similar type, location and 
magnitude such as Nextera, it must be consistent with prior determinations. 
 
The S -Power-1 SEQRA review cannot be used as replacement for the Nextera SEQRA 
review nor can it be used as a Generic Environmental Impact Statement under SEQRA, as i t 
was specific to the S -Power-1 project. In addition to the inadequacy of cumulative impacts 
assessment, there is also a serious concern for segmentation under SEQRA procedures. 
 
The Lead Agency is responsible for reviewing the application as a whole proje ct, on behalf 
of the involved agencies. The applicant must demonstrate its ability to comply with all 
relevant permits/approval requirements from the agencies. Each of these permit requirements 
and any special conditions imposed could alter the project or its impact potential. The EIS 
process would allow the public and other agencies to be more directly involved, offer 
comments on the record and identify how the project may or may not work as a whole 
project.  
 
In the case of Nextera, these additional agenc y reviews include the New York State DEC, 
Part 666 Wild and Scenic Recreational Rivers permit which requires the applicant to 
demonstrate alternatives tht remove the development from within the Wild Scenic 
Recreational Rivers jurisdictional boundary to the  greatest extent practicable, the Suffolk 
County Department of Health Services for closure of the sanitary disposal systems at the 
Maintenance Barn and Clubhouse and New York State DEC compliance with the 
decommissioning of the irrigation well located on t he easterly parcel. These approvals were 
not all listed on the Environmental Assessment form and thus the coordinated review is 
incomplete. Has the site been evaluated for presence of other environmental quality concerns 
including storage presence of pesticides, fertilizer and fuels associated with the site’s past use 
as a golf course? Has coordinated review been completed by the Central Pine Barrens 
Commission or New York State Department of Agriculture and Markets who commented on 
the regional loss of agriculture generated by this type of development? How much C&D will 
  
For more information visit our website at www.townofriverheadny.gov  
 
be generated by demolishing the clubhouse, maintenance barn, golf course irrigation system 
and other existing infrastructure? When will these development issues be assessed? How can 
the Lead  Agency come to a conclusion that there are no potential adverse environmental 
impacts without completely evaluating the proposed action? 
 
Based on the aforementioned information and history of SEQRA reviews and with my 
extensive experience in and knowledge of these matters, it is my professional opinion that the 
SEQRA determination of significance for the Nextera application for Town Board Special 
Permit and Planning Board Site Plan must result in a Positive Declaration of Significance 
necessitating the preparation of an EIS. This EIS would serve to address the aforementioned 
SEQRA deficiencies including segmentation and maintain an accurate, complete and 
defensible SEQRA record of decision. Thank you for your consideration, Jeffrey Seeman, 
CGCA/CEP

INVESTMENT POLICY  
FOR THE  
TOWN OF RIVERHEAD 
 
I SCOPE 
 
This investment policy applies to all moneys and other financial resources available for 
investment on its behalf or on behalf of any other entity or individual. 
 
II OBJECTIVES 
 
The primary objectives of the local government’s investment activities are, in primary 
order: 
 
- to conform with all applicable federal, state and other legal requirements 
- to adequately safeguard principal  
- to provide sufficient liquidity to meet all operating requirements; and 
- to obtain a reasonable rate of return  
 
III DELEGATION OF AUTHORITY 
 
The Town Board’s responsibility for administration of the investment program is delegated 
to the Town Supervisor who shall establish written procedures for the operation of the  
investment program consistent with these investment guidelines.  Such procedures shall include 
an adequate internal control structure to provide a satisfactory level of accountability based on 
a database or records incorporating description and amounts of investments, transaction dates 
and other relevant information and regulate the activities of subordinate employees. 
 
IV PRUDENCE 
 
All participants in the investment process shall seek to act responsibly as custodians of 
the public trust and shall avoid any transaction that might impair public confidence in the Town 
of Riverhead to govern effectively. 
 
Investments shall be made with judgement and care, under circumstances then 
prevailing, which persons of prudence, discretion and intelligence exercise in the management 
of their own affairs, not for speculation, but for investment, considering the safety of the 
principal as well as the probable income to be derived. 
   
All participants involved in the investment process shall refrain from personal business 
activity that could conflict with proper execution of the investment program, or which could 
impair their ability to make impartial investment decisions. 
 
V DIVERSIFICATION 
 
It is the policy of the Town of Riverhead to diversify its deposits and investment s by 
financial institution, by investment instrument, and by maturity scheduling.

VI INTERNAL CONTROLS 
 
It is the policy of the Town of Riverhead for all moneys collected by any officer or 
employee of the government to transfer those funds to the Town Supervisor within ten days of 
deposit, or within the time period specified in law, whichever is shorter. 
 
The Town Supervisor as chief fiscal officer is responsible for establishing and maintaining 
an internal control structure to provide reasonable, but no t absolute, assurance that deposits 
and investments are safeguarded against loss from unauthorized use or disposition, that 
transactions are executed in accordance with management’s authorization and recorded 
properly, and are managed in compliance with applicable laws and regulations. 
 
The Town Board, in order to implement the investment policy, authori zes the Financial 
Administrator or his deputy, under the control and direction of the Financial Administrator, to 
undertake the necessary ministerial day to  day functions regarding the temporary investments  
of monies and to further carry out the cash management for town funds and related collateral 
management matters subject to and consistent with General Municipal Law, Section 11.

VIII COLLATERALIZATION OF DEPOSITS 
 
In accordance with the provisions of General Municipal Law, Section 10, all deposits of the Town 
of Riverhead, including certificates of deposits and special time deposits, in excess of the amount 
insured under the provisions of the Federal Deposit Insurance Act shall be secured pursuant to 
and consistent with the provisions of General Municipal Law, Section 10 (3) (a) through (d): 
 
1) By a pledge of “eligible securities” with an aggregate “market value”, or provided 
by General Municipal Law, Section 10, equal to the 102% of the aggregate amount 
of deposits from the categories designated Appendix A to this policy.  
 
2) By an eligible “irrevocable letter of credit” issued by a qualified bank other than 
the bank with the deposits in favor of the government for a term not to exceed 90 
days with an aggregate value equal to 140% of the aggregate amount of deposits 
and the agreed upon interest, if any.  A qualified bank is one whose commercial 
paper and other unsecured short -term debt obligations are rated in one of the 
three highest rating categories by at least one nationally recognized statistical 
rating organization or by a bank that is in compliance with applicable federal 
minimum risk-based capital requirements.

IX SAFEKEEPING AND COLLATERALIZATION 
 
Eligible securities used for collateralizing deposits shall be held by the depository and/or 
a third party bank or trust company subject to security and custodial agreements. The Supervisor 
shall be authorized to execute all and any necessary security, custodian and collateral 
agreements on behalf of the Town of Riverhead. 
 
The security agreement shall provide that eligible securities are being pledged to secure 
the Town’s deposits together with agreed upon interest, if any, and any costs or expenses arising 
out of the collections of such deposits upon default.  It shall also provide the conditions under 
which the securities may be sold, presented for payment, substituted or released and the events 
which will enable the Town of Riverhead to exercise it rights against the pledged securities.  In 
the event that the securities are not registered or i nscribed in the name of th e Town of 
Riverhead, such securities shall be delivered in a form suitable for transfer or with an assignment 
in blank to the Town of Riverhead or its custodial bank. 
 
The custodial agreement shall provide that securities held by the bank or trust company, 
or agent of and custodian for the Town of Riverhead, will be kept separate and apart from the 
general assets of the custodian bank or trust company and will not, in any circumstances, be 
commingled with or become part of the backing for any other deposit or other liabilities.  The 
agreement should also describe that the custodian shall confirm the receipt, substitution or 
release of the securities.  The agreement shall provide from the frequency of revaluation of 
eligible securities and for the substitute of securities when a change in the rating of a security 
may cause in-eligibility.

All investment obligations shall be payable or redeemable at the option of the Town of 
Riverhead within such times as the p roceeds will be needed to meet expenditures for 
purposes for which the moneys were provided and, in the case of obligations purchased 
with the proceeds of bonds or notes, shall be payable or redeemable at the option of the 
Town of Riverhead within two years of the date of purchase. 
 
XI AUTHORIZED FINANACIAL INSTITUTES AND DEALERS 
 
The Town of Riverhead shall maintain a list of financial institutions and dealers 
approved for investment purposes and establish appropriate limits to the amount of 
investments which can be made with each financial institution or dealer.  All financial 
institutions with which the Town conducts business must be credit worthy.  Banks shall 
provide their most recent Consolidated Report of Condition at the request of the Town.  
Security dealers not affiliated with a bank shall be required to be classified as reporting 
dealers affiliated with the New York Federal Reserve Bank, as primary dealers.  The chief 
fiscal officer is responsible for evaluating the financial position and maintaini ng a listing 
of proposed depositories, trading partners and custodians. Such listing shall be evaluated 
at least annually.

deposit or other liabilities.  The agreement shall describe how the custodian shall confirm 
the receipt and release of the securities.  Such agreement shall include all provisions 
necessary to provide the Town a perfected interest in the securities.
