Fringe benefits: the deferred comp, annuity and life insurance allowance

Every elected official in Riverhead, and a handful of appointed managers, is paid a yearly sum on top of salary to put toward retirement savings, a life insurance policy or an annuity. It rises with inflation every year, and no budget line shows it.

Independent project·Updated October 5, 2026·● Current·Check data freshness →
💡In plain English
In 2025 the Town paid $5,762.28 to each of 12 elected officials, $9,603.80 to the Supervisor, and $6,649.69 to each of 5 appointed managers: $111,999.61 in all. Each person chooses where it goes: a deferred compensation account (Roth or regular), an insurance policy, or an annuity. The amount goes up every year by the previous year's New York-area inflation, whether or not salaries do.
  • ›Deferred compensation: a retirement savings account (a “457” plan). Riverhead uses the New York State Deferred Compensation Plan.
  • ›Roth 457: the after-tax version: tax is paid now, and withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. The Town opted in by Resolution 2017-495.
  • ›Annuity: an insurance contract that pays out later, often for life.

How the amount is set: inflation, every year

The contracts that carry the benefit say how it grows. The Police Chief's, approved in December 2023:

“The cost of these policies to the Town may not exceed $2,500.00. […] The cost will be adjusted annually based on the Consumer Price Index for New York and Northeastern New Jersey area for all Urban Consumers […] The Base Year to be used will be 1989.”

The payroll follows that rule to within a cent. Each year's allowance is the year before's, raised by how much New York-area prices rose in the year before that, rounded to a hundredth of a percent. The elected officials' allowance, the Supervisor's and the managers' all move together:

Salaries did not move the same way. A Council Member was paid $48,955 in 2018 and still $48,955 in 2024; the allowance on top rose from $4,629.89 to $5,551.86 over the same years. A resident made the point to the Board in 2012, and the Supervisor agreed (see November 20, 2012 below).

The managers' amount is the contracts' own. Two contracts adopted in January 2010 state “The cost of these policies to the Town for 2009 was $4,692.59.” Carried forward by the same rule, that is $5,345.02 for 2018; the payroll paid $5,342.90. Where it is in the budget: inside the salary lines

No budget line is called deferred compensation, annuity or life insurance. The allowance is paid through payroll, so it is charged to each office's salary line (“Personal Services”), and the published salary schedule shows only base salaries.

The Town Board's own line shows it. In 2025 the Board's Personal Services line (A01-1-1010-101) spent $273,900.65. The Board's payroll that year, less health-insurance buyouts (which have their own line), was $272,506.94 with the four Council Members' allowances in it, and $249,457.82 without them. Only the first matches.

In 2010 a resident asked why the judges' annuities appeared to be budgeted as “sick pay buy back” (see November 3, 2010 below). In 2025 the Justice Court's sick-buyback line spent $4,511.69, less than even one judge's allowance.

July 2026: left out of the newest contract

The draft contract for the new Recreation Superintendent, Ashley Schandel, in the July 16, 2026 work session packet carried the clause as its item 6, letting the Town “contribute to an independent life insurance policy, disability insurance policy, annuity or deferred compensation program of the employee’s choice.” The contract attached to Resolution 2026-695, adopted 5–0 on July 21, 2026 (Halpin, Rothwell, Kern, Merrifield, Waski), goes from item 5 straight to Article IX without it. Raymond Coyne, the Superintendent of Recreation in the 2025 payroll, was paid the managers' allowance.

How it got here

1989
It begins, under Supervisor Joseph Janoski

Asked in 2010 about the payments, Supervisor Sean Walter said they started as a life insurance policy or an annuity bought from one insurance company, and that under Supervisor Phil Cardinale officials were allowed to put the money into deferred compensation instead. The contracts that carry the benefit still index it from a 1989 base year.

“it started out as an insurance or an annuity, life insurance or an annuity”
“It started under Janoski in '89.”
“to put this into deferred comp so you didn't have to go get life insurance or an annuity policy through an insurance agent that you didn't use”
Town Board minutes, December 7, 2010
January 5, 2010
Two Supervisor’s Office appointees get the clause, with its 2009 cost

Resolutions 11 and 15, adopted 5–0, set terms for the Executive Assistant to the Supervisor and the Deputy Supervisor. Both offer a Universal Life policy, a disability policy or the State deferred compensation plan, and state what the Town paid for it the year before.

“The cost of these policies to the Town for 2009 was $4,692.59.”
“The cost will be adjusted yearly based on the Consumer Price Index for New York and Northeastern New Jersey area for all Urban Consumers”
“The Base Year to be used will be 1989.”
Town Board agenda packet, January 5, 2010, pp. 27 and 42 (scanned; read by OCR)
November 3, 2010
A resident asks why the budget doesn’t say “annuities”

At the hearing on the 2011 budget, Jan McKenna said she had been told the Justice Court’s sick-pay buyback line also paid the two judges’ annuities, and estimated that about 20 employees received annuities costing about $100,000. Councilman James Wooten said he did not believe they were annuities.

“the remainder of that portion is for the annuities for the two judges”
“why does it not say annuities”
“we felt there was like 20 employees that were getting annuities which seems to come to $100,000”
“I don't believe that they are annuities.”
Town Board minutes, November 3, 2010
December 7, 2010
The Supervisor: “there’s nothing that’s been hidden”

A resident asked about an article by former Supervisor Vinny Villella saying that $170,000 in deferred compensation for elected officials, appointees and department heads had never been disclosed. Supervisor Walter answered “That’s not accurate,” said the payments were mostly contractual, and said the Town Board and the other elected officials receive them. He did not say what they cost.

“Most of it is contractual.”
“the town board does receive them”
“the other elected officials do receive them so there's nothing that's been hidden”
Town Board minutes, December 7, 2010
November 20, 2012
Frozen salaries, rising annuity

When three Board members said they would not take a raise, resident Matt Hattorff asked whether they would take their annuity’s cost-of-living increase. Supervisor Walter agreed that a zero raise would still leave the annuity rising.

“Are you taking your annuity?”
“If you had a zero built into the budget, you would still get cost of living on your annuity.”
“Yes, that's accurate.”
Town Board minutes, November 20, 2012 (transcript, pp. 1468–1470)
June 20, 2017
The Roth option opens

Resolution 2017-495, adopted 5–0, opted the Town into the New York State Deferred Compensation Plan’s Roth 457 program, so employees could choose an after-tax Roth account as well as the regular one.

“Authorizes the Financial Administrator to Opt into the New York State Deferred Compensation Plan Roth 457 Program for Otherwise eligible Town Employees”
“this is just giving the employees the option to invest in either Deferred or a Roth 457.”
Town Board minutes, June 20, 2017
January 3, 2019
Extended to the Chief of Staff

Resolution 2019-37, adopted 4–0, gave the Supervisor’s Chief of Staff the benefit “to make his salary commensurate with the previous staff member.” Its fiscal impact statement says there is a fiscal impact, absorbed in the existing budget, and gives no amount. The payroll pays the managers’ sum in 2019, but not 2018, to John Marafino, whom the Board appointed Executive Assistant to the Supervisor in January 2018.

“RESOLVED, that the deferred comp., annuity, life insurance benefit be extended to the chief of staff”
“to make his salary commensurate with the previous staff member”
“To provide the Chief of Staff with deferred comp., annuity and life insurance benefit”
Town Board minutes, January 3, 2019
December 5, 2023

Questions worth asking

  • Which resolution sets the elected officials’ allowance, and why is the Supervisor’s one and two-thirds times the others’? This site has not found one in the Town’s online records.
  • Why is the allowance not a line of its own in the budget, so that residents can see what it costs? Today it is folded into each office’s salary line.
  • The Board left the benefit out of the newest department head’s contract in July 2026. Is it closed to new hires? Will it end for elected officials, who have no contracts, and if so, by what vote?
  • Where does the money go? The payroll report shows each payment but not whether it went to a Roth or regular deferred comp account, an insurance policy or an annuity.

What this page cannot tell you

  • The counts are people whose payroll shows exactly the year’s sum. Someone who joined or left during a year, or whose allowance was paid together with other pay, is not counted, so each total is a floor. In 2024, for example, the Police Chief, the Chief Accountant and a Police Captain retired with large final payments that make their allowance impossible to separate.
  • The payroll data this site keeps does not carry the Town’s pay-code names. The allowance is identified by its amount: the same sum, to the cent, paid to a dozen or more people each year, growing by the rule the contracts state to within a cent.
  • The Town’s 2026 payroll report has not been published. The 2026 figures are the rule applied to BLS’s 2025 index.
  • Whatever each person chooses, the Town’s report counts the payment in gross pay.
