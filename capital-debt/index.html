Capital & Debt

What Riverhead actually owes, from its most recent financial report — plus a calculator for the two ways a town finances a capital project: bond immediately, or borrow short-term with a Bond Anticipation Note first.

💡In plain English
As of December 31, 2025, Riverhead had $38,423,858 in bonded debt outstanding and $21,975,000 in Bond Anticipation Notes — plus $57,059,509 the Board had authorized but not yet issued as long-term bonds as of December 31, 2023, which is exactly the kind of balance a BAN typically carries in the interim.
  • ›BAN: a Bond Anticipation Note — short-term borrowing a town uses while a project is underway, later paid off or replaced with a long-term bond once the final cost is known.
  • ›Serial bond: the long-term borrowing that eventually replaces a BAN — principal repaid in equal installments over the bond’s term, with interest on the declining balance.
  • ›Why not always bond now: issuing a 20-year bond for a project that isn’t finished yet means paying interest on money that may sit unspent, and locks in a rate before the final price tag is known.

At these assumptions, BAN-then-bond costs $375,000 more in total interest than bonding immediately — the price of 2 years of interest-only carrying cost on top of the eventual bond. Towns use BANs anyway when a project's final cost isn't settled yet, to avoid borrowing — and paying interest on — money that isn't ready to be spent, and to keep the option open on when to lock in a long-term rate.
