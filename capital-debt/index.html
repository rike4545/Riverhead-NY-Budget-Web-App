Capital & Debt

Every bond and note the Town owes, itemised — $60,398,858 outstanding across 10 issues as of December 31, 2025 — plus the repayment schedule through 2053 and a calculator for the two ways a town finances a capital project.

💡In plain English
As of December 31, 2025, Riverhead owed $38,423,858 on 8 bonds and $21,975,000 on 2 bond anticipation notes — $60,398,858 in all. The Town issued no new debt during 2025 and retired $6,361,040 of principal. Every issue is listed below.
  • ›BAN: a Bond Anticipation Note — short-term borrowing a town uses while a project is underway, later paid off or replaced with a long-term bond once the final cost is known.
  • ›Serial bond: the long-term borrowing that eventually replaces a BAN — principal repaid in installments over the bond’s term, with interest on the declining balance.
  • ›EFC bond: a bond sold through the NYS Environmental Facilities Corporation for clean-water work. They carry subsidised rates — one of Riverhead’s is interest-free.
  • ›Why the debt limit ignores water and sewer: those districts charge their own users, so State law leaves their debt out of the constitutional limit on town borrowing.

What has happened since

The balances above are a photograph taken on December 31, 2025. Both bond anticipation notes reached their maturity dates during 2026, and the Board has voted twice since to pay debt down — so here is what the Town's own record shows after the shutter closed.

  1. March 18, 2025
    Bond resolution 2025-273 — appropriating $2,350,000
    A new borrowing authorisation. Authorised is not issued; it joins the authorised-but-unissued balance until bonds are actually sold.
    Town Board meeting of March 18, 2025 — adopted unanimously.
  2. October 7, 2025
    Bond resolution 2025-856 — authorising issuance and appropriating $6,500,000
    A second, larger authorisation in the same year.
    Town Board meeting of October 7, 2025 — adopted unanimously.
  3. February 20, 2026
    The $19,250,000 Town Hall BAN reached its maturity date
    A note that matures is either paid off or renewed. The Town has until February 2028 — five years from the original 2023 issue — to convert it into long-term bonds.
    2025 Annual Financial Report, Statement of Indebtedness Debt Records.
  4. July 7, 2026
    Resolution 2026-641 — budget adjustment to pay down the Town Square BAN
    The Board voted unanimously to use fund balance to pay the note down rather than roll it over again.
    Town Board meeting of July 7, 2026 — adopted unanimously.
  5. July 7, 2026
    Resolution 2026-642 — budget adjustment to pay down the 2018 Series B bond refunding
    Booked as present-value debt-service savings — retiring principal early to avoid the interest that would have accrued on it.
    Town Board meeting of July 7, 2026 — adopted unanimously.
  6. August 14, 2026
    The Town Square BAN reached its maturity date
    Five years to the day after the original August 2021 issue — but five was not the deadline. Chapter 157 of the Laws of 2020 lets a capital BAN originally issued in calendar years 2015 through 2021 be renewed for up to seven years rather than five, and this note was issued in August 2021, inside that window. The Town could have rolled it to 2028. It chose to retire it instead, using the proceeds of the Petrocelli parcel sale.
    2025 Annual Financial Report, Statement of Indebtedness Debt Records.

The amounts of the two July 2026 pay-downs are not in the resolution titles, and no financial report covering 2026 has been filed yet. So the balances on this page are the audited December 31, 2025 figures — the true figures today are lower by whatever those pay-downs came to.

Bonds and notes are less than a third of it

Everything above this point is money the Town borrowed. It is the part that gets talked about, and at $60,398,858 it is also the part the Town has been paying down. But borrowing is not the largest promise on the books. The Comptroller's Schedule of Non-Current Government Liabilities adds up everything the Town owes beyond this year, and the total is $204,164,853 — with retiree health insurance alone larger than all the bonds, notes, pension and accrued leave put together.

Why the retiree-health number jumps around

This liability is an actuarial estimate, not a bill. Most of the year-to-year movement comes from the discount rate GASB 75 requires an unfunded plan to use — the S&P Municipal Bond 20-Year High Grade index. It rose from 4.00% at the 2023 valuation to 4.28% at the 2024 one, which is the bulk of why the reported liability dropped that year. Nothing was pre-funded and no benefit was reduced. Read the direction of this number as a rate story first and a policy story second.

At these assumptions, BAN-then-bond costs $375,000 more in total interest than bonding immediately — the price of 2 years of interest-only carrying cost on top of the eventual bond. Towns use BANs anyway when a project's final cost isn't settled yet, to avoid borrowing — and paying interest on — money that isn't ready to be spent, and to keep the option open on when to lock in a long-term rate.

Where these figures come from

Balances, issue and maturity dates, and the repayment schedule: Town of Riverhead 2025 Annual Financial Report, Statement of Indebtedness, p.142-143; Bond Repayment schedule, p.144-146. Interest rates, formal issue names, the governmental/water-sewer split, the authorized-but-unissued balance and the debt-limit percentage: Town of Riverhead 2024 Audited Basic Financial Statements, Note 3.E Indebtedness, p.60-62 — the newest independent audit, one year older than the balances.

Only governmental debt counts toward the constitutional limit; water and sewer district debt is excluded by statute. The Town reported $39,081,792 subject to the limit at December 31, 2024 — 6.74% of it. The prior audit reported 3.78% at December 31, 2023, but the two are not comparable: the earlier figure counted bonds only, while the later one counts the two BANs as well. Almost none of the difference is new borrowing. The constitutional limit itself is not printed as a dollar figure in the 2024 audit — the ~$579,848,545 shown here is implied by dividing the debt subject to the limit by the percentage the audit reports.
