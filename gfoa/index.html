Measured against the national standard

GFOA's Distinguished Budget Presentation Award criteria, revised for 2026: nine content categories worth 150 points and five material-type categories worth 50. On this site's own reading it scores 123 of 200, against the 100 points GFOA requires — but read the two caveats before the number.

💡In plain English
This page is a public scorecard of this site against the national standard for budget presentation — written to be useful where it falls short.
  • ›What GFOA is: the Government Finance Officers Association. Its Distinguished Budget Presentation Award is the recognized standard for how a government should present a budget.
  • ›What changed in 2026: mandatory criteria were replaced by a 200-point scale, the content criteria became questions a member of the public would actually ask, and websites and dashboards became eligible submission material alongside the budget document.
  • ›Why bother: the criteria are a checklist for whether budget information has been presented honestly and completely — including the parts this site gets wrong.

Two things to know before reading the score

This site cannot win this award. GFOA grants it to governments that submit their own budget communications. The 2026 revision widened what counts as a submission — a budget website or dashboard is now eligible material alongside the document — but the applicant still has to be the government. These criteria are used here as a yardstick, not as an application.

The points are GFOA's; the scores are this site's own. GFOA publishes what each category is worth, not a rubric for partial credit inside it, so “12 of 20” means the coverage was judged about three-fifths complete. It is a self-assessment — the reading most likely to flatter itself — so the verdicts and the named gaps matter more than the arithmetic.

Content — 150 points

What a member of the public wants to know. GFOA frames each category as a question the government should answer; the question under each heading is theirs.

Community Priorities and Organizational Challenges / Opportunities
12 / 20Partly covered

GFOA asks: What are the major challenges facing the organization and the community, how does this budget address them, and are results expected to improve within the budget period?

The pressures are documented in detail: the 2027 tax-cap gap, the retirement buyout, five years of cap overrides, the OPEB liability, and the community housing fund the Town declined. Candidate platforms and Board voting records show where the disagreements are. 2027 pressures →

What is missing: The challenges are well covered; the priorities are not. The Town publishes no strategic goals in a form anyone can extract, so this site cannot report what it has not adopted — and says so rather than substituting its own.

Value
10 / 20Partly covered

GFOA asks: What is the public getting from the government, and how much does the government cost — per person, per household, for specific services?

Road spending per maintained mile against the other nine Suffolk towns, the credit rating against peer towns, and reserve levels against peer policy are all real value comparisons rather than raw totals. Spending per mile →

What is missing: Service levels and outcomes — what the money actually buys, measured — are largely absent, because the Town publishes no performance measures. This is the weakest of the high-value categories.

Long-Term Outlook
15 / 20Well covered

GFOA asks: How do current budget decisions affect the long-term outlook, reserve levels, deferred costs, and future obligations?

A 2027 projection built from signed contract terms, the cap gap it implies, a debt schedule running to 2053, the unfunded OPEB liability, and twenty years of General Fund history behind it all. 2027 outlook →

What is missing: No multi-year forecast beyond 2027, and the Town publishes no long-range financial plan to check against.

Revenue Budget
17 / 20Well covered

GFOA asks: How much revenue is anticipated, how diverse and controllable is it, what is restricted, and how is the burden distributed across the community?

The levy and its growth against the cap, twenty years of revenue history, the CPF transfer tax, a personal tax-bill estimator built on the real assessment ratio, and the tax base the levy is spread across. Levy and the cap →

Personnel Budget
14 / 15Well covered

GFOA asks: How many staff are budgeted, how did personnel costs change, where did staffing rise or fall, and what is driving the cost?

Actual pay by employee and year, authorized salary schedules, raises, overtime against staffing levels, union step schedules, separation pay, and the retirement incentive — the deepest area on the site. Payroll explorer →

Department Budget
12 / 15Well covered

GFOA asks: What services does each department provide, what do they cost, and how is the department held accountable for results?

Every operating fund drills to department and then to individual account line items, reconciled to the Town’s own published summary. Funds and departments →

What is missing: Departmental narrative — what each department does and how it is organised — is thin next to the numbers.

Program Budget
3 / 15Not covered

GFOA asks: What are the major programs and services, what does each cost, what are their service-level goals, and do they generate revenue?

Not presented. The Town budgets by fund and department rather than by program, and this site has not constructed a program view on top of that.

What is missing: The clearest remaining content gap. Building it would mean mapping line items to services — real work, and partly a judgement call this site would have to make and defend.

Capital Budget
8 / 15Partly covered

GFOA asks: How is capital spending defined and prioritized, how is it funded, and what are the major projects?

The financing side is covered well: outstanding bonds and notes, the amortization schedule, debt limit headroom, the credit rating, and a bond-versus-note calculator. Capital and debt →

What is missing: There is no project-level capital plan here — what is being built, when, and at what cost — because the Town publishes no extractable capital improvement plan.

Budget Process
10 / 10Well covered

GFOA asks: How is the budget developed, who is involved, how are community priorities considered, and when can the public engage?

The statutory calendar, the tax-cap override mechanics, every Board vote on record with who voted how, fiscal-impact reads on resolutions, and a plain-English guide to the vocabulary. The Board’s own rules of procedure are laid out too — the two public-comment windows, the five-minute limit on resolutions, and the fact that a public hearing carries no time limit at all. Material type — 50 points

The tools used to communicate it: how they are organized and laid out, and whether they meet generally accepted accessibility standards.

Budget Document
0 / 10Not covered

GFOA asks: Is the budget document findable, organized, clearly messaged, well illustrated, and accessibility-compliant?

Not applicable. The budget document is the Town’s to publish; this site reads it rather than replacing it, and links to the source PDFs. Source library →

Budget-In-Brief / Newsletter
7 / 10Well covered

GFOA asks: Is it actually brief, free of non-budget material, distributed to residents, and attractively designed?

The guided tour is exactly this — an eleven-stop walkthrough from "what is a budget" to the raw data — backed by a dashboard that leads with plain-English figures and a glossary. The guided tour →

What is missing: No printable or mailable version, which is what "newsletter" implies for a resident without reliable internet.

Budget Website or Dashboard
9 / 10Well covered

GFOA asks: Is the budget site easy to reach, interactive with drill-down, accessible on mobile and to disabled users, and updated more than once a year?

This is the whole site: grouped navigation, on-page anchors, a search index across line items and documents, and charts that carry text labels and tooltips rather than relying on colour. Audited for heading order, landmarks, labelled controls and contrast. Dashboard →

Videos
0 / 10Not covered

GFOA asks: Do videos carry the major budget messages and explain key terms for a general audience?

None. Nothing on this site is video, narrated, or captioned.

What is missing: A short walkthrough of the tour would be the cheapest ten points available here, and would reach readers who will not read a page of numbers.
