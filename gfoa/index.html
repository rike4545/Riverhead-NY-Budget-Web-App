💡In plain English
This page is a public scorecard of this site against the national standard for budget presentation — written to be useful where it falls short.
  • ›What GFOA is: the Government Finance Officers Association. Its Distinguished Budget Presentation Award is the recognized standard for how a government should present a budget.
  • ›What the revision changes: mandatory criteria give way to a 200-point scale, the content criteria become questions a member of the public would ask, and the award takes in every kind of budget communication, from websites and dashboards to videos, not just the budget document.
  • ›Why bother: the criteria are a checklist for whether budget information has been presented honestly and completely — including the parts this site gets wrong.

Which criteria apply, and when

The criteria on this page are GFOA's revised set. They take effect on January 1, 2027. In GFOA's words: “Between January 1, 2027 and December 31, 2027, governments may submit to either the existing criteria or the new criteria. Starting January 1, 2028, all governments must submit using the new criteria.”

The existing criteria are the ones GFOA's award page still links. They are written for “Budgets with a Fiscal Year Beginning 1/1/25 or later”, and they mark 15 of their 25 criteria “Mandatory”. The revised set drops that: the first change GFOA lists is “Eliminate mandatory criteria”, and its criteria page says “Submission materials are not required for every category.” A category left empty scores nothing but does not disqualify.

What that means for the Town. The Town's budget year is the calendar year. Under the existing rules a budget must reach GFOA “within 90 days of the date when the budget was approved/adopted”, and the Board adopted the 2026 budget on November 18, 2025. So a 2026 budget submitted on time would have been judged against the existing criteria, not the ones on this page. The 2027 budget could be judged against either set, depending on when it is submitted.

This page uses the revised set because every submission will be judged against it from January 1, 2028, and because it scores a budget website, which is what this site is.

Two things to know before reading the score

This site cannot win this award. Only governments may apply: under the revised rules, state and local governments and their sub-units “that prepare and adopt a budget”. The revised criteria take in “all budget communications including the budget document, websites, dashboards, multimedia, and other forms of communications”, but the applicant still has to be the government. These criteria are used here as a yardstick, not as an application.

The points are GFOA's; the scores are this site's own. GFOA's reviewers score completeness from 0 to 2 and quality from 0 to 3, so “each category will be assigned a score between 0 and 5”. Then “The score will be divided by 5 and then multiplied by the total points possible for the category to calculate the total points earned.” This site set its scores by its own judgment, not on that scale, and 9 of the 14 could not come from a whole-number score out of 5. It is a self-assessment — the reading most likely to flatter itself — so the verdicts and the named gaps matter more than the arithmetic.

Content — 150 points

What a member of the public wants to know. GFOA words each category as “questions that a public stakeholder would have about the budget”. The questions under each heading are GFOA's, word for word.

Community Priorities and Organizational Challenges / Opportunities
12 / 20Partly covered

GFOA asks: What are the major challenges facing the organization and/or the community? How does this budget address those challenges? Are results expected to improve within the budget period?

The pressures are documented in detail: the 2027 tax-cap gap, the retirement buyout, five years of cap overrides, the OPEB liability, and the community housing fund the Town declined. Candidate platforms and Board voting records show where the disagreements are. 2027 pressures →

What is missing: The challenges are well covered; the priorities are not. The Town publishes no strategic goals in a form anyone can extract, so this site cannot report what it has not adopted — and says so rather than substituting its own.

Value
15 / 20Well covered

GFOA asks: What is the public getting from the government? How much does the government cost?

Cost is answered per resident and per household, town-wide and for each of the seven services, with Census household counts and median income as the denominators. Road spending per maintained mile against the other nine Suffolk towns, the credit rating against peer towns, and reserve levels against peer policy add outside comparison. Cost per resident and household →

What is missing: The cost half of the question is fully answered. The other half — what residents actually get, measured in response times, permits, tonnage or participation — is still descriptive rather than measured, because the Town publishes no performance data.

Long-Term Outlook
15 / 20Well covered

GFOA asks: How do current budget decisions impact the long-term fiscal outlook for the government? How does the current budget impact reserve levels? Are any expenses deferred to future budgets (operating or capital)? Does the budget create any long-term spending obligations? What were trends from prior years and their impact on the future outlook?

A 2027 projection built from signed contract terms, the cap gap it implies, a debt schedule running to 2053, the unfunded OPEB liability, and twenty years of General Fund history behind it all. 2027 outlook →

What is missing: No multi-year forecast beyond 2027, and the Town publishes no long-range financial plan to check against.

Revenue Budget
17 / 20Well covered

GFOA asks: How much revenue is anticipated? Where does revenue come from? How much control or discretion does the government have over revenue? How is the revenue burden distributed among the community?

The levy and its growth against the cap, twenty years of revenue history, the CPF transfer tax, a personal tax-bill estimator built on the real assessment ratio, and the tax base the levy is spread across. Levy and the cap →

Personnel Budget
14 / 15Well covered

GFOA asks: How many total staff are budgeted? How have personnel costs changed from the prior year? What are the major drivers impacting personnel costs?

Actual pay by employee and year, authorized salary schedules, raises, overtime against staffing levels, union step schedules, separation pay, and the retirement incentive — the deepest area on the site. Payroll explorer →

Department Budget
13 / 15Well covered

GFOA asks: What are the major programs or services? What are the costs? What are the goals? How much revenue does each generate? (GFOA’s criteria page lists the same questions here as under Program / Services Budget.)

Every operating fund drills to department and then to individual account line items, reconciled to the Town’s own published summary. Funds and departments →

What is missing: Written narrative now exists for each of the seven service functions, and every department is listed under the one it belongs to — but there is still no prose for each of the 176 individual departments, and no accountability-for-results reporting, because the Town publishes none.

Program / Services Budget
11 / 15Well covered

GFOA asks: What are the major programs or services? What are the costs? What are the goals? How much revenue does each generate?

The whole budget regrouped into the seven services New York’s Uniform System of Accounts says the Town performs, with the full cost of each — including the pension and health insurance of the staff who deliver it — the fee revenue it earns back, its cost-recovery rate, and its net cost per resident and per household. The classification is the State’s and the revenue tagging is the Town’s own, so this is a regrouping rather than an invention. Program budget →

What is missing: Three of GFOA’s four questions are now answered — the programs, their full cost, and whether they earn revenue. The fourth is not: service-level goals require performance measures the Town does not publish, so no amount of rearranging the budget will produce them.

Capital Budget
8 / 15Partly covered

GFOA asks: How is capital spending defined? What is the level of capital spending? How are projects prioritized? How is the capital budget funded? What are the major projects?

The financing side is covered well: outstanding bonds and notes, the amortization schedule, debt limit headroom, the credit rating, and a bond-versus-note calculator. Capital and debt →

What is missing: There is no project-level capital plan here — what is being built, when, and at what cost — because the Town publishes no extractable capital improvement plan.

Budget Process
10 / 10Well covered

GFOA asks: How is the budget developed? Who is involved? How are community priorities considered? When are decisions made? When can the public participate?

The statutory calendar, the tax-cap override mechanics, every Board vote on record with who voted how, fiscal-impact reads on resolutions, and a plain-English guide to the vocabulary. The Board’s own rules of procedure are laid out too — the two public-comment windows, the five-minute limit on resolutions, and the fact that a public hearing carries no time limit at all. Not applicable. The budget document is the Town’s to publish; this site reads it rather than replacing it, and links to the source PDFs. Source library →

Budget in Brief / Newsletter
7 / 10Well covered

GFOA asks: Does the budget in brief provide a concise overview? Is it free from unnecessary information? Was it distributed? Is it attractive?

The guided tour is exactly this — an eleven-stop walkthrough from "what is a budget" to the raw data — backed by a dashboard that leads with plain-English figures and a glossary. The guided tour →

What is missing: No printable or mailable version, which is what "newsletter" implies for a resident without reliable internet.

Budget Website
9 / 10Well covered

GFOA asks: Is the budget website easy to access? Is it interactive? Is it updated?

This is the whole site: grouped navigation, on-page anchors, a search index across line items and documents, and charts that carry text labels and tooltips rather than relying on colour. Audited for heading order, landmarks, labeled controls and contrast. Dashboard →

Videos
0 / 10Not covered

GFOA asks: Does the budget video highlight major budget messages? Does it explain key concepts? Does it reflect community priorities?

None. Nothing on this site is video, narrated, or captioned.

What is missing: A short walkthrough of the tour would be the cheapest ten points available here, and would reach readers who will not read a page of numbers.
