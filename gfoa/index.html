💡In plain English
This page is our public scorecard against the national standard for budget presentation — including where this site doesn't measure up yet.
  • ›What GFOA is: the Government Finance Officers Association, whose Distinguished Budget Presentation Award defines how a budget should be presented: as a policy document, a financial plan, an operations guide, and a communication device.
  • ›Why it applies here: the award rates a government’s own budget document — but its criteria are the recognized yardstick for budget presentation, so we hold this site to every criterion that can apply and say plainly where we fall short.
  • ›Honest statuses: "Not yet" usually means the Town has not published the underlying information (goals, performance measures, debt schedules) in a form anyone can extract.

As a Communication Device

Can an average reader actually find, read, and understand the information?

C1Table of ContentsMandatory
Met

GFOA asks: A comprehensive table of contents that makes information easy to find.

Persistent navigation on every page, a Start Here guide that maps every tool, on-page section anchors, and a full sitemap. Start Here guide →

C2Budget OverviewMandatory
Met

GFOA asks: An overview of significant budgetary items and trends (a "budget in brief").

The dashboard leads with plain-English KPIs and insights (levy growth, surplus, reserve use), and the Annual Report page gives the plan-vs-actual story in brief. Dashboard →

C3Statistical / Supplemental Section
Partial

GFOA asks: Statistical and supplemental data about the organization, its community, and population.

Deep fiscal statistics exist (20-year General Fund history, payroll statistics, per-fund histories), but community context — population, tax base, demographics — is not yet presented. General Fund history →

To close the gap: Add community/tax-base statistics (population, assessed valuation, largest taxpayers).

C4Glossary
Met

GFOA asks: A glossary defining terminology a lay reader would not know.

A full plain-English glossary on the Start Here page, plus tappable definitions on jargon terms and "what do these columns mean?" guides on data tables. Glossary →

C5Charts and Graphs
Met

GFOA asks: Charts and graphs highlighting financial information, with narrative interpretation where needed.

Twenty-year trend charts, per-line sparklines, category bars, and vote visualizations — each paired with a written explanation of what it shows. Budget Compare →

C6Understandability and Usability
Met

GFOA asks: Formatting oriented to the average reader: attractive, consistent, understandable.

Every tool opens with an "In plain English" intro; consistent design across all pages; per-page titles; mobile-friendly layout; the entire site is written for readers with no finance background. Start Here guide →

As a Policy Document

Does it explain what the government is trying to achieve and the rules it follows?

P1Strategic Goals and StrategiesMandatory
Not yet

GFOA asks: A coherent statement of organization-wide strategic goals addressing long-term concerns.

The Town has not published machine-readable strategic goals; nothing to extract yet.

To close the gap: Extract and present strategic goals if the Town publishes them in a budget message or plan.

P2Priorities and IssuesMandatory
Partial

GFOA asks: A budget message articulating priorities, issues, and significant changes for the year.

Resident Insights on the dashboard and the Annual Report narrative explain what changed and why it matters — an independent stand-in for a transmittal letter, not the Town’s own message. 2025 Annual Report →

To close the gap: Extract the Supervisor’s budget message from the adopted budget when one is included.

P3Financial PoliciesMandatory
Partial

GFOA asks: Entity-wide long-term financial policies, including the definition of a balanced budget.

Reserve-use and fund-balance context is explained where it appears, but the Town’s formal financial policies are not yet extracted and presented.

To close the gap: Extract the Town’s fund-balance and reserve policies into a dedicated policies section.

P4Budget ProcessMandatory
Met

GFOA asks: A description of how the budget is prepared, reviewed, adopted, and amended.

The Start Here guide explains the tentative → preliminary → adopted process in plain English, and post-adoption amendments are visible as Town Board resolutions in the voting record. How the budget gets made →

As a Financial Plan

Does it show all the money — where it comes from, where it goes, and what remains?

F1Fund Descriptions and Fund Structure
Met

GFOA asks: Describe all appropriated funds and the fund structure.

All 19 operating funds are described in plain English with their codes, purposes, and financing mix. Funds Explorer →

F2Basis of Budgeting
Not yet

GFOA asks: Explain the basis of budgeting (cash, modified accrual, or other) for all funds.

The Town’s budget documents do not state a basis of budgeting for us to extract; we do not assert one.

To close the gap: Present the basis of budgeting if stated in future Town documents.

F3Consolidated Financial ScheduleMandatory
Met

GFOA asks: A consolidated summary of revenues, expenditures, and other sources/uses.

Town-wide totals with a category rollup of every account line, plus each fund’s revenues by source — reconciled to the dollar against the official Summary page. Funds Explorer →

F4Three-Year Consolidated and Fund Schedules
Met

GFOA asks: Revenues and expenditures for prior actual, current, and proposed years, consolidated and by fund.

Every budget line carries 2024–2026 adopted figures (and a trend back to 2020); the Annual Report shows three years of actuals (2023–2025) for every fund. Annual Report →

F5Fund BalanceMandatory
Met

GFOA asks: Projected changes in fund balance for appropriated funds, with definitions.

Fund-balance use appears on every fund page; the Annual Report breaks the General Fund’s $33.4M balance into classifications with plain-English definitions; the glossary defines the terms. Fund balance breakdown →

F6RevenuesMandatory
Partial

GFOA asks: Describe major revenue sources, estimate assumptions, and trends.

Revenue line items for every fund, actual revenue categories with three-year trends, and 20 years of General Fund revenue history — but the Town’s estimating assumptions are not published for extraction. Revenue trends →

To close the gap: Present revenue assumptions if the Town publishes them.

F7Long-Range Operating Financial Plans
Not yet

GFOA asks: Long-range financial plans extending at least two years beyond the budget year.

The Town does not publish a long-range operating plan; the Scenario Lab explores assumptions but is not a plan.

To close the gap: Present multi-year projections if the Town adopts a long-range plan.

F8Capital ProgramMandatory
Partial

GFOA asks: Budgeted capital expenditures and their impact on operations.

Capital-project budget adoptions are trackable in the Town Board voting record, and capital-fund actuals appear in the Annual Report — but there is no consolidated capital schedule yet. Capital resolutions →

To close the gap: Build a consolidated capital-projects view from budget-adoption resolutions and the capital fund.

F9DebtMandatory
Partial

GFOA asks: Current debt obligations, legal debt limits, and effects on operations.

Debt Service Fund appropriations and actual debt-service spending are shown; principal/interest schedules, BAN exposure, and debt limits are not yet extracted. Debt Service Fund →

To close the gap: Extract debt schedules and legal-limit calculations from the audit and AFR.

As an Operations Guide

Does it show who does the work — departments, people, and what they aim to accomplish?

O1Organization ChartMandatory
Not yet

GFOA asks: A legible organization chart for the entire entity.

Not yet presented; the department structure exists in our data and could be rendered as a chart.

To close the gap: Render an organization view from the fund → department structure.

O2Department / Fund Relationship
Met

GFOA asks: Show the relationship between departments and funds.

The Funds Explorer maps exactly this: every fund drills into its departments, and every department shows its fund. Fund drilldowns →

O3Position Summary ScheduleMandatory
Partial

GFOA asks: Position counts for prior, current, and budgeted years, entity-wide.

Authorized positions with salaries for 2025 and 2026 (by department and group) and actual headcounts 2018–2025 exist — but not yet as a single position-count table across years. Payroll Explorer →

To close the gap: Publish a consolidated headcount-by-department table across years.

O4Departmental DescriptionsMandatory
Partial

GFOA asks: Descriptions of each department or program.

Departments are clearly presented with their spending detail and categories; narrative descriptions of what each department does are not yet written. Department drilldowns →

To close the gap: Add one-line plain-English descriptions per department.

O5Departmental Goals and Objectives
Not yet

GFOA asks: Department goals linked to the entity’s strategic goals.

The Town does not publish departmental goals in its budget documents.

To close the gap: Present departmental goals if the Town publishes them.

O6Performance MeasuresMandatory
Not yet

GFOA asks: Objective measures of progress for departments and programs.

The Town does not publish performance measures; the closest available signals (spending vs. plan, overtime trends) are shown but are not outcome measures.

To close the gap: Present performance data if the Town adopts measures.

Criteria summarized from the GFOA Distinguished Budget Presentation Award program (gfoa.org/budget-award). This is an independent self-assessment, not a GFOA review; the award itself is earned by governments for their official budget documents. "Mandatory" marks criteria GFOA requires for the award.
