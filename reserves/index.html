Reserves & fund balance policy

How the Town's savings stack up against its own reserve rules, what a disciplined one-time deployment plan could look like, and how Riverhead's posture compares to nearby towns.

Color an increase:
💡In plain English
The General Fund ended FY2025 with $29,671,084 in unassigned reserves — 42.9% of the $69,113,159 2026 General Fund budget, well above the Town's 15% policy floor.
  • ›Unassigned fund balance: the "rainy-day" savings with no strings attached — the actual FY2025 audited figure, not a mid-year estimate.
  • ›Policy floor: Riverhead's own policy sets a 15% minimum and 20% upper target of General Fund appropriations.
  • ›One-time vs. recurring: anything above the operating target is one-time money — good for debt paydown or capital, not for permanent new spending.

How 28.8% compares nearby

Riverhead's target lands below what Brookhaven and Smithtown are doing today, but above Southampton's official policy.

Riverhead target28.8%

Modeled target for this plan: 28.8% of the General Fund budget after one-time deployment.

Brookhaven38.8%

Brookhaven's 2026 adopted General Town Wide unreserved fund balance is about $60.0M against about $154.6M of budgeted expenditures, or roughly 38.8%.

Smithtown39.9%

Smithtown's 2026 tentative General Fund projected fund balance is about $24.1M against roughly $60.4M of projected annual scale, or about 39.9%.

East Hampton56.2%

East Hampton's 2026 adopted General Fund projection totals about $48.7M across whole-town and part-town balances against roughly $86.8M of General Fund appropriations, or about 56.2%.

Southampton policy17.0%

Southampton's 2026 adopted financial policy sets a general-fund reserve structure of 10% restricted plus at least 7% unallocated, for a 17% benchmark.

Benchmark note: GFOA guidance commonly points to at least two months of regular operating spending or revenue in unrestricted fund balance, about 16.7% to 17% — which is why Southampton's 17% policy reads more like a minimum floor than a default target.
