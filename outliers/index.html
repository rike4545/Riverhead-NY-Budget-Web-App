Outlier Watch

Riverhead's 19 town funds, each checked against its own budget the year before, from 2020 to the 2027 Tentative. A flag marks a swing large enough to be worth a second look. It is not proof of a problem, but a place to start asking questions.

In plain English

Across 133 fund-year comparisons from 2020 to the 2027 Tentative, 7 clear the bar for a flag, one of them in the 2027 Tentative.
  • What counts as an outlier: a fund whose appropriation moved at least 20% and at least $100,000 from one budget to the next: each adopted budget against the one before it, and the 2027 Tentative against the 2026 adopted budget.
  • Why two thresholds: percent alone flags small funds swinging on a tiny base (a $15,000 fund going to $25,000 is "+67%" but immaterial); dollars alone would flag the General Fund almost every year simply because it is the biggest fund.
  • The 2027 Tentative is a proposal: it is the Supervisor's recommended budget. The Board can change it before it adopts the 2027 budget, by November 20, and this page moves to the Board's version when it is published.
  • What it does not mean: a flag is not an accusation. Big swings often have ordinary explanations — a bond payoff, a new contract, a one-time grant. Treat this as a starting point for a question at a Town Board meeting, not a conclusion.

What the 2027 Tentative flags

Against the 2026 adopted budget, one fund moves past both bars: the Debt Service Fund, from $6,888,150 to $2,594,028 (−$4,294,122, −62.3%).

Five other funds change by $100,000 or more but by less than 20%, so they are not flagged: General Fund (up $2,901,203, 4.2%), Water District (up $382,713, 3.5%), Highway Fund (up $366,792, 4.6%), Riverhead Sewer District (up $264,016, 3.2%) and Riverhead Scavenger Waste (up $173,109, 7.8%).

One fund changes by 20% or more but by less than $100,000, so it is not flagged either: Calverton Parks Community Development Agency (up $31,161, 70.7%).

Why the Debt Service Fund falls

The fund makes bond and note payments for the General Fund and other funds, with money they send it, so a fall here is not a cut to a service. Two votes the Board took in 2026 paid debt down, mostly with savings:

  • Resolution 2026-642 (July 7, 2026, unanimous) pays down the 2018 Series B refunding bonds with $7,212,941 of Community Preservation Fund balance and $92,059 from the sale of Town property.
  • Resolution 2026-762 (August 4, 2026, unanimous) pays down the Town Square bond anticipation note with $1,874,218 of General Fund balance and $122,500 of rent from the Town Square properties.

In the 2027 Tentative, the Community Preservation Fund sends the Debt Service Fund nothing; the 2026 budget had it send $2,959,300. That fund's own budget does not shrink: a new line, “Fund Balance Contribution”, adds the same $2,959,300 to its savings instead, which is why the Community Preservation Fund is not flagged.
