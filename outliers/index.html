Outlier Watch

Riverhead's 19 town funds, each checked against its own budget the year before, from 2020 to the 2027 Preliminary. A flag marks a swing large enough to be worth a second look. It is not proof of a problem, but a place to start asking questions.

Independent, unofficial project·Data last refreshed November 8, 2026·Recently refreshed·See data update details →
The line figures on this page are the Supervisor’s Tentative.

The Town Board’s six October 20, 2026 amendments changed 32 account lines in the Preliminary Budget. This page measures the Tentative as it was filed, so those changes are not in its figures. The funds, revenue and Budget Accuracy pages use the Preliminary.

Read the six amendments and their sources

In plain English

Across 133 fund-year comparisons from 2020 to the 2027 Preliminary, 7 clear the bar for a flag, one of them in the 2027 Preliminary.
  • What counts as an outlier: a fund whose appropriation moved at least 20% and at least $100,000 from one budget to the next: each adopted budget against the one before it, and the 2027 Preliminary against the 2026 adopted budget.
  • Why two thresholds: percent alone flags small funds swinging on a tiny base (a $15,000 fund going to $25,000 is "+67%" but immaterial); dollars alone would flag the General Fund almost every year simply because it is the biggest fund.
  • The 2027 Preliminary is a proposal: it is the Tentative with the Board's changes, the version the public hearing is held on. The Board adopts the 2027 budget by November 20.
  • What it does not mean: a flag is not an accusation. Big swings often have ordinary explanations — a bond payoff, a new contract, a one-time grant. Treat this as a starting point for a question at a Town Board meeting, not a conclusion.

What the 2027 Preliminary flags

Against the 2026 adopted budget, one fund moves past both bars: the Debt Service Fund, from $6,888,150 to $2,594,028 (−$4,294,122, −62.3%).

Five other funds change by $100,000 or more but by less than 20%, so they are not flagged: General Fund (up $2,818,911, 4.1%), Water District (up $393,684, 3.6%), Highway Fund (up $366,792, 4.6%), Riverhead Sewer District (up $268,816, 3.3%) and Riverhead Scavenger Waste (up $175,166, 7.8%).

One fund changes by 20% or more but by less than $100,000, so it is not flagged either: Calverton Parks Community Development Agency (up $31,161, 70.7%).

Why the Debt Service Fund falls

The fund makes bond and note payments for the General Fund and other funds, with money they send it, so a fall here is not a cut to a service. Two votes the Board took in 2026 paid debt down, mostly with savings:

  • Resolution 2026-642 (July 7, 2026, unanimous) pays down the 2018 Series B refunding bonds with $7,212,941 of Community Preservation Fund balance and $92,059 from the sale of Town property.
  • Resolution 2026-762 (August 4, 2026, unanimous) pays down the Town Square bond anticipation note with $1,874,218 of General Fund balance and $122,500 of rent from the Town Square properties.

In the 2027 Preliminary, the Community Preservation Fund sends the Debt Service Fund nothing; the 2026 budget had it send $2,959,300. That fund's own budget does not shrink: a new line, “Fund Balance Contribution”, adds the same $2,959,300 to its savings instead, which is why the Community Preservation Fund is not flagged.
