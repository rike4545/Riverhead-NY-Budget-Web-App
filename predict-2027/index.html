Does the 2027 budget pierce the tax cap?

Yes — by about $2,619,382

On current trends the 2027 levy grows about 6.0% — well above the roughly 2% the cap allows — so the budget would pierce the cap by about $2,619,382. To stay under the cap the Town would have to close that ~$2,619,382 gap. (The real ceiling is a bit higher than a flat 2% once the tax-base-growth factor and exclusions are added, which would shrink the gap somewhat.)

How the gap gets closed → The 2027 Spending Reduction page lays out the plan — the retirement incentive plus sourced trims cover essentially the whole gap. Prefer to try the trade-offs yourself? Use the What-if scenarios tool.
Or stay under it another way — the full menu of leversOpen ▾
Refill fewer / lower-cost positions (the retirement buyout)
Realized salary savings from the 2026 buyout flow straight into Personal Services. Our buyout model puts this at roughly $0.7M on the positions with a clear entry step, and up to ~$1.8M if the eligible police fully turn over — a large share of the gap on its own.
Trim or slow spending
Every 1% cut to the $126,152,504 spending plan is about $1,261,525. Closing the whole gap this way means roughly a 2.1% cut — e.g. holding posts vacant, deferring equipment and capital, or trimming contractual lines.
Grow non-property-tax revenue
State aid, mortgage tax, fees, and interest earnings offset the levy dollar-for-dollar. Every extra $1M of non-tax revenue is $1M less that has to come from the cap-busting levy.
Use reserves (one-time)
Appropriating about $2,619,382 more of the $33,407,251 General Fund balance would erase the gap outright — but it's roughly 7.8% of the cushion, spends one-time money on recurring cost, and can't be repeated forever.
Claim the cap's legal exclusions
The cap formula already excludes pension-contribution growth above two percentage points and voter-approved capital. Booking those correctly raises the legal ceiling — the opposite of the 2018–2022 error, when the ceiling was miscalculated the other way.
Or override it — but on purpose
If the Board decides the services are worth it, it can pierce the cap the right way: adopt the override local law first, in public, with the 60% vote on the record — as it did in 2023, 2024, and 2026. The cap can be exceeded legally; it just has to be a deliberate, disclosed choice.

How this projection works — and the assumptions you can argue withOpen ▾

The spending side is projected line by line: each 2026 amount grows by the rate for its category. Add it up and 2027 spending lands near $126,152,504, up 4.16%. The tax-levy figure is a separate, illustrative estimate — illustrative only. Assumes non-property-tax revenue (state aid, fees, grants, reserves) grows 2% and the property-tax levy balances the rest. The real levy also depends on revenue and reserve choices the Board makes.

Predicted 2027 = 2026 Adopted × (1 + the growth rate for that line's category). Rates are calibrated to each category's actual 2024–2026 trend and the Town's stated cost drivers, then rounded to assumptions you can second-guess.
