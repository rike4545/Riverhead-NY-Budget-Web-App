The 2027 Tentative Budget

This site’s independent 2027 projection against the Town’s own Tentative, fund by fund — and what the record says about whether a Tentative changes before it is adopted.

Independent project·Updated September 26, 2026·● Current·Check data freshness →
💡In plain English
The Town’s 2027 Tentative has been published and is compared below with the projection this site made before it existed.
  • ›A Tentative is a proposal: it appropriates nothing. Only the budget the Board adopts by Nov 20 creates the authority to spend (Town Law §109).
  • ›Who prepares it: the budget officer — the Supervisor, or someone the Supervisor appoints to serve at the Supervisor’s pleasure (Town Law §103).
  • ›Where to be heard: the Sept 24 presentation has no public comment period. The public hearing is on the Preliminary budget, by Nov 5.

Against the projection

The Tentative appropriates $120,958,878, −$4,890,500 against the projection, and levies $67,159,863, −$1,807,211 against it. The General Fund plans to use $950,000 of fund balance against $1,250,000 in the 2026 adopted budget.

Not counting transfers between funds, it spends $116,864,850, up 3.7% from 2026 (+$4,142,096). Counting all 19 funds, it appropriates $120,958,878, down 0.1% from 2026. The Debt Service Fund, paid for by transfers from the other funds, falls $4,294,122. Counting that fund makes a smaller debt payment look like a spending cut, so the first figure is the one that says how much more the Town plans to spend.

What the Supervisor’s letter says

The Tentative opens with a letter from Supervisor Jerry Halpin, dated September 24, 2026. Its pages are scanned images, so this site read them by hand; the quotes are exact. Each claim is set beside what the budget’s own Summary shows.

“The goal of this Tentative Budget is clear – provide financial relief for tax payers while bolstering opportunities for increased employee retention at the lower end of the pay scale.”
“There is joy in presenting this budget as all taxing districts are budgeted within the tax cap limit of 2.79%.”

What the budget shows: None of the 9 levies in the Summary rises by more than 2.79%. The town-wide levy rises 2.79% and the total with the special districts 2.78%; the Business Improvement District levy is unchanged. How the Town reached 2.79% isn’t published. The State’s growth factor for 2027 is 2%; the formula also adjusts for tax-base growth, PILOT payments, carryover and exclusions, and the Town files the result with the State Comptroller. If the letter’s limit is right, the proposal as written needs no override vote. The Board would need one only to adopt a levy above it. How the tax cap works →

“Total appropriations for the town operating fund in 2027 are $116,864,850, a stable growth of $4,142,096 over 2026.”

What the budget shows: Matches the Summary to the dollar, a rise of 3.7%. It is every fund except the Debt Service, Workers’ Compensation and Risk Retention funds, which the other funds pay for. Counting those too, the total is $120,958,878, down 0.1%.

“The 2027 General Fund Tentative Budget adds $2.9 million in expenditures, which equals approximately $0.28 per day for property valued at $800,000.”

What the budget shows: General Fund appropriations rise $2,901,203 (+4.2%), to $72,014,362. At the 7.44% residential assessment ratio on the Town’s 2025–26 tax rate sheet, an $800,000 home is assessed at about $59,520. The General Fund rate rises $1.399 per $1,000, about $83 a year or $0.23 a day; the whole town-wide rate rises $1.626, about $97 a year or $0.27 a day. The letter doesn’t say what ratio it used.

“The 2027 Tentative Budget also includes a $300,000 reduction of Fund Balance contribution to the General Fund.”

What the budget shows: Matches: the General Fund uses $950,000 of its savings (fund balance), against $1,250,000 for 2026.

“This year, we were able to offer a retirement incentive that yielded a $819,000 savings directly to the General Fund for 2027. Three CSEA members and seven PBA members chose to take the retirement incentive …”

What the budget shows: The Summary shows funds, not salary lines, so it can’t confirm this. By the letter’s own figures the saving before retiree health insurance is $1,087,000: $746,000 in salaries and payroll taxes and $341,000 in State retirement contributions. The difference, $268,000, is the added retiree health insurance, the figure Financial Administrator Jeannette DiPaola gave at the September 24 meeting; she said replacements for the retirees are also budgeted for 2027 (Riverhead News-Review, Sept. 24, 2026). The 10 who took it were among 53 eligible employees, and the Town’s July estimate of the saving was $500,000 to $800,000. The retirement incentive →

“In total, seventy-six merit raises (including the sixty-four above), nine promotional raises, six new full-time positions, as well as two new part-time positions, are budgeted for while remaining under the 2.79% tax cap.”

What the budget shows: The Summary shows funds, not salary lines, so it can’t confirm this. As the Riverhead News-Review, Sept. 24, 2026 reported the meeting, 64 of the 76 merit raises are two extra steps on the salary schedule, on top of contractual raises, and the eight new positions are a maintenance mechanic, two buildings and grounds staff, an automotive equipment operator, a building inspector, a purchasing clerk, an office assistant and a fire marshal.

Source: 2027 Tentative Budget (PDF), pp. 2–3 (the letter) and the Summary page.

Will the Tentative change before adoption?

Rarely, on the record so far. In 3 of the 5 years where the Town published both, the adopted budget matched its Tentative at every fund: nothing moved in the Board’s review, and nothing moved after the public hearing. So the document presented on Sept 24 has, in practice, been very close to the budget the Town ends up with. The full record back to 2005, including the years the Board never voted to adopt, is on How Riverhead adopts its budget.

What the line-by-line Supplement shows

The Budget Supplement prints every line of the Tentative beside what it cost in 2025, the 2026 budget, and spending through June 30, 2026. Read against the seven Supplements before it, it shows things the totals do not.

The General Fund’s debt payments fall about $1 million

Its transfer for debt payments drops from $3,054,850 to $2,048,119, $1,006,731 less. Leave debt out and the rest of the General Fund rises $3,907,934, or 5.9%, where the headline increase is $2,901,203. The drop made room for other spending; the General Fund levy still rises the full amount the cap allows.

Where 2025’s surplus came from

Pay came within $4,351 of its 2025 budget. Pensions, health insurance, workers’ compensation, Social Security and other benefits came in $1,642,746 under their budgets. Revenue from outside sources — everything but the tax levy and money moved between the Town’s own funds — came in $5,385,176 over its estimate; interest alone was budgeted at $50,000 and brought in $1,491,222. The 2027 Tentative raises the interest estimate to $750,000. Every revenue stream →

Health insurance: $1,838,221 above 2025 spending

The General Fund budgets $11,075,232 for health, dental and optical insurance, 9.6% above the 2026 budget. 2025 came in $430,653 under its budget, and the first half of 2026 cost $3,989,834 against $4,293,684 in the first half of 2025. Part of the increase is retiree coverage for the ten employees who took the retirement incentive. Mid-year figures can shift with billing dates, so the question is what premium increase the Tentative assumes.

3 lines over budget three years running, budgeted low again

“Mach -Equipment” in the Highway Fund averaged $992,240 a year and gets $236,500 in the Tentative; “Atty - Prof Svcs - Legal” in the General Fund averaged $665,429 a year and gets $400,000 in the Tentative; “Police - Pers Svcs Uniform OT” in the General Fund averaged $1,195,715 a year and gets $1,037,500 in the Tentative. A line can only spend past its budget if money is moved to it during the year, so the adopted budget never shows what these cost. Budget accuracy →

22 lines budgeted every year, never used

Nothing has been spent from them in the last three years or the first half of 2026, yet the Tentative carries $338,060 on them, $265,430 in the General Fund. Revenue from an agreement the Board tabled

The Tentative counts $200,000 of reimbursement for school resource officers, $40,000 more than was requested, from the agreement with the Riverhead Central School District the Board tabled on September 15. The Town received $30,871 in 2024 and $52,696 in 2025, and $57,322 through June 30, 2026. School resource officers →

Employee benefits are a third of the General Fund

Pensions, health insurance, Social Security, workers’ compensation and unemployment come to $23,864,241 in the 2027 Tentative, 33% of the General Fund, up from $22,232,369 budgeted for 2026 and $18,742,966 spent in 2025. The Tentative’s pension figure is $1,116,744 below what departments requested; the State set its 2027–28 rates on September 8.

Figures are the Town's own, from its Budget Supplements (2027 Budget Supplement (PDF)). Actual spending and collections are the Town's books before each year's audit.

The 2027 calendar

  • Sept 20 — Department estimates due to the budget officer (Town Law §104)
  • Sept 24 — Town Clerk presents the Tentative at a special meeting — no public comment period (Town notice, Sept 2026)
  • Sept 30 — Deadline to file the Tentative with the Town Clerk (Town Law §106(2))
  • Oct 5 — Deadline to present it to the Town Board (Town Law §106(3))
  • Nov 3 — General election
  • Nov 5 — Deadline for the public hearing on the Preliminary budget (Town Law §108)
  • Nov 20 — Deadline to adopt; if the Board does not, the Preliminary becomes the budget (Town Law §109)

What this page cannot tell you

  • The 2.79% limit is the Town’s own figure, from the Supervisor’s letter. No table in the budget prints it, and the letter gives neither the dollar amount nor how it was reached. New York’s formula starts from the State’s 2% growth factor and adjusts the prior levy for tax-base growth, PILOT payments, carryover and exclusions; the Town files the result with the State Comptroller. This site’s 2% line is a reference, not that limit.
  • Totals of all 19 funds include the Debt Service, Workers’ Compensation and Risk Retention funds, which are paid for entirely by transfers from the other funds, so those dollars are counted twice. “Not counting transfers between funds” leaves the three out, as the Supervisor’s letter does. Either way, a total compares one budget with another; it does not state what the Town spends.
  • A Tentative is a proposal. It appropriates nothing (Town Law §109). The Board may change it before the hearing on the Preliminary and again before adoption. In the years on record it rarely has, but it can.
  • The projection grows each 2026 line by a rate for its category. It knows nothing about decisions made since, so a gap between it and the Tentative is a question about what changed, not a finding that either is wrong.
  • Department requests come from the Budget Supplement, which accompanies the Tentative. A year is shown only when its lines add up to its own Tentative; 2022 and 2023 extract with pages missing and are withheld.
