Workforce by Title

Who runs what: the offices residents elect, the departments the Town Board oversees, and every job title inside each. Then how many people hold each title and work in each department, 2022 to 2025.

Independent project·Updated October 10, 2026·Current·Check data freshness →

In plain English

This is the staffing side of the payroll data: not what people are paid, but who does which job and whether that job is growing or shrinking. It starts with how the Town is organized, then counts the same staff by job title and by department. For individual pay, use the Payroll Explorer.
  • What a count shows: the number of distinct employees paid under that title or in that department during the year.
  • Seasonal spikes: part-time and seasonal roles, such as lifeguards, recreation aides and beach attendants, run high because everyone paid during the summer counts for that year.
  • Why it starts in 2022: the Town’s gross-earnings exports only carry job titles from 2022 onward; earlier years have pay but no title.
  • Hired vs. moved: a net change hides its two sides. “Hired” and “left” mean the person was not on the Town payroll at all in the other year; “moved” means they stayed and were retitled or reassigned.

How the Town is organized

Residents elect the Supervisor, four Council Members, the Town Clerk, the Superintendent of Highways, the Receiver of Taxes, the Assessors and two Town Justices. The Supervisor and the four Council Members make up the Town Board, which adopts the budget and oversees every other department. Below is every office and department, with every job title the Town paid in 2025 and how many people held it: 574 people, 571 of them under 158 job titles and 3 paid with no title.

The Town’s report lists each person once a year, under one department and one title, so nobody is counted twice. A count takes in everyone paid at any point in the year, including people who joined or left partway through and the summer’s seasonal staff. Open a card to see its titles.

The Town publishes no organization chart, so this one is put together from its own records. Which offices are elected comes from the payroll’s department codes, which mark them. The departments and their names come from the Town’s list of departments (read October 8, 2026). The people and titles come from the Town of Riverhead Gross Earnings reports. Which of the payroll’s 65 department codes belong to which department is this site’s reading of their names and of the budget’s departments; each card lists its codes. A payroll code is the department the payroll report lists a person under, and the budget can group the same people differently: the payroll lists the code enforcement officers under the Town Attorney, while the budget has a Code Enforcement department. A head is named only where the payroll pays a title that names the role.

Distinct employees paid under each civil-service title, by year. Titles are available 2022 onward; seasonal and part-time roles (lifeguards, recreation aides, beach attendants) inflate summer headcounts. Arrivals and departures behind each net change, counted for regular staff only. "Hired" and "left" mean the person was not on the Town payroll at all in the other year; "moved" means they kept working for the Town under a different title or department, which a net count would otherwise read as turnover. People are matched on payroll file number where the roster supplies one, so the three employees who appear under two surnames are not double-counted. Only years the Town reports are compared. Staff the Town places in its NON-TIME, Part Time or Seasonal pay classes are excluded from these flows and counted separately: lifeguards and recreation aides returning each summer are not a workforce turning over, and including them reported Recreation as hiring 123 people and losing 104 when its six permanent staff saw one of each. That group is not only seasonal — appointed board members paid a stipend file no time card either, so they fall in the same classes. Those arrival and departure figures cover 2022–2025, the years the Town reports a title. They count regular staff only and the counts include seasonal and part-time staff, so the two need not add up. Counts are distinct employees paid under each title that year. The teal “2026 authorized rate” line is what the Town Board's January 2026 salary resolutions actually print for that title, available for 143 of the titles. Those rosters have an ANNUAL SALARY column and an HOURLY column, but the Town fills the hourly one in only for part-time and per-hour staff — and for the Water District, the one department that publishes both. For every other full-time title, no hourly rate is published, so the grey ≈ line brackets it instead: the annual over 2,088 hours (a 40-hour week) at the low end and over 1,827 hours (a 35-hour week) at the high end. Those are the two regular workweeks in the CSEA agreement, on Riverhead's 261-workday year; all 16 of the Water District's published hourly rates land on exactly one or the other. (The Town pays biweekly, but the rate is struck on that 261-day year, not on 26 × 80 hours — the published rates match 2,088 and miss 2,080.) Police Officers and Detectives are bracketed on their own contract instead: the PBA agreement sets an eight-hour tour with a duty chart of 238 work days a year, or 260 during an officer's first 30 months. The rosters don't say which schedule each title is on, which is why the figure is a range and not a single number — and why it is arithmetic by this site, not a rate the Board voted on. No hourly figure at all is shown for elected officials, board members (paid a stipend, not a wage), or sergeants and above, who are a separate Superior Officers unit whose duty chart we don't hold. Source: Town of Riverhead Gross Earnings reports

Staff by department

The same employees, counted by where they work rather than by what they are called. Open a department to see the titles inside it. These are the payroll's own department codes, which the organization chart above gathers into the Town's departments. Here the Police Department arrives as its squads, COPE, Detectives, K-9 and Headquarters rather than as one line, so read them as cost centers. A title can appear in several departments at once: in 2025 a Police Officer is paid out of nine of them, and an Account Clerk out of ten.

A net change is not a hiring figure, so each row also shows the flows underneath it. Two distinctions do the work. The first is left versus moved: when Heavy Equipment Operator fell from 15 to 6, nobody was let go and nobody was hired. Nine of those people were retitled, mostly to Construction Equip Operator, and kept working for the Town. A net column alone reads that as staff losses.

The second is who counts as staff at all. Riverhead employs a large casual workforce: 199 people in 2025 in the pay classes it marks NON-TIME, Part Time or Seasonal, at a median of about $3,900 against roughly $79,000 for regular staff. Recreation is the extreme case: 132 people, of whom 126 are seasonal or part-time and 6 are permanent. Counting a lifeguard's summer as a hire and their absence next June as a departure would have shown Recreation hiring 123 people and losing 104, when its permanent staff saw one of each. So the arrival and departure figures count regular staff only, and the seasonal headcount is shown beside them instead of being folded in. That group is not purely seasonal: appointed board members paid a stipend file no time card either, and land in the same classes.

Staff counted in each department the Town's payroll export names, by year. These are the payroll's own department codes rather than an organization chart: the Police Department appears as its squads, COPE, Detectives, K-9 and Headquarters instead of as one line. A title can sit in several departments, so the department counts are what add up to the workforce, not the sum of the titles listed under them. Departments start in 2022 because every earlier department value in this dataset was carried back from a later year rather than reported. Arrivals and departures behind each net change, counted for regular staff only. "Hired" and "left" mean the person was not on the Town payroll at all in the other year; "moved" means they kept working for the Town under a different title or department, which a net count would otherwise read as turnover. People are matched on payroll file number where the roster supplies one, so the three employees who appear under two surnames are not double-counted. Only years the Town reports are compared. Staff the Town places in its NON-TIME, Part Time or Seasonal pay classes are excluded from these flows and counted separately: lifeguards and recreation aides returning each summer are not a workforce turning over, and including them reported Recreation as hiring 123 people and losing 104 when its six permanent staff saw one of each. That group is not only seasonal — appointed board members paid a stipend file no time card either, so they fall in the same classes. Counts are distinct employees paid in that department that year, so they add up to the Town's payroll headcount — 574 in 2025. The titles listed under a department do not, for two reasons: the same title can sit in several departments, and a few people each year carry a department but no title at all. Where that happens the shortfall is shown on its own no title recorded line rather than left to look like an arithmetic error. Source: Town of Riverhead Gross Earnings reports
