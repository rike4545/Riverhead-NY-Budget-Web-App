This is the staffing side of the payroll data: not what people are paid, but how many hold each job and whether that job is growing or shrinking. For individual pay, use the Payroll Explorer.
  • ›What a row shows: the number of distinct employees paid under that title each year, and the net change from 2022 to 2025.
  • ›Seasonal spikes: part-time and seasonal roles — lifeguards, recreation aides, beach attendants — run high because everyone paid during the summer counts for that year.
  • ›Why it starts in 2022: the Town’s gross-earnings exports only carry job titles from 2022 onward; earlier years have pay but no title.

Distinct employees paid under each civil-service title, by year. Titles are available 2022 onward; seasonal and part-time roles (lifeguards, recreation aides, beach attendants) inflate summer headcounts. Counts are distinct employees paid under each title that year. The teal “2026 authorized rate” line is what the Town Board's January 2026 salary resolutions actually print for that title, available for 143 of the titles. Those rosters have an ANNUAL SALARY column and an HOURLY column, but the Town fills the hourly one in only for part-time and per-hour staff — and for the Water District, the one department that publishes both. For every other full-time title, no hourly rate is published, so the grey ≈ line brackets it instead: the annual over 2,088 hours (a 40-hour week) at the low end and over 1,827 hours (a 35-hour week) at the high end. Those are the two regular workweeks in the CSEA agreement, on Riverhead's 261-workday year; all 16 of the Water District's published hourly rates land on exactly one or the other. (The Town pays biweekly, but the rate is struck on that 261-day year, not on 26 × 80 hours — the published rates match 2,088 and miss 2,080.) Police Officers and Detectives are bracketed on their own contract instead: the PBA agreement sets an eight-hour tour with a duty chart of 238 work days a year, or 260 during an officer's first 30 months. The rosters don't say which schedule each title is on, which is why the figure is a range and not a single number — and why it is arithmetic by this site, not a rate the Board voted on. No hourly figure at all is shown for elected officials, board members (paid a stipend, not a wage), or sergeants and above, who are a separate Superior Officers unit whose duty chart we don't hold.
