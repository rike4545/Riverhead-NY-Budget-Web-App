Transfer of development rights

How Riverhead lets a builder buy extra building room by paying a farmer not to build: which land can sell credits, where they can be used, what one costs, and how the Walmart expansion on Route 58 uses them.

Independent, unofficial project·Data last refreshed November 10, 2026·Recently refreshed·See data update details →

In plain English

A credit sells for about $80,000, the Town's planners say. On Route 58 one credit lets a store build 3,000 more square feet, about $26.67 a square foot. The Walmart at 1890 Old Country Road has bought 45 credits since 2007. Its proposed 26,835 sq. ft. expansion needs 1.312 more, about $104,960 at that price. The Planning Board's public hearing on it is November 19, 2026.
  • Sending land: farmland in the Agricultural Protection Zone (APZ). Its owner can sell credits, one credit per 43,560 square feet of real property (one acre), and the land is then preserved for good.
  • Receiving land: places the Town wants growth, such as Route 58, Calverton and some residential districts. A builder there can exceed the normal limit by buying credits.
  • Preservation credit: the Code’s name for one development right once it has been separated from the farm and put on a certificate.

How it works

  1. A farm owner in the Agricultural Protection Zone (APZ) asks the Planning Board how many credits the land holds. The rule is one (1) right per 43,560 square feet of real property, which is one credit an acre (Town of Riverhead, Proposed Transfer of Development Rights Map and Text (2004), § 108-304 and § 108-307, pages 2 and 4).
  2. The owner records an easement that ends the land's development rights and receives a credit certificate. The farm can still be farmed and sold; it can never be built on. It then joins the preserved land.
  3. The owner sells the certificate privately. The Town does not set the price, and there is no TDR bank to buy from (see the program so far).
  4. A builder in a receiving district buys credits and redeems them with the Planning Board for more floor area or more homes than the zoning otherwise allows.

No credits are allocated for land whose development rights have already been sold or transferred; land under easements, rights of way or other recorded encumbrances; land already built on with homes, farm buildings or other improvements, including golf courses (Town of Riverhead, Proposed Transfer of Development Rights Map and Text (2004), § 108-308, page 4). The Town's 2026 environmental statement on golf cottages still counts one credit as one acre: “If the six (6) proposed golf courses ... at a TDR redemption rate of 1,200 square feet per TDR, the result would be 54 acres.” (Town Board agenda packet, October 20, 2026 (Golf Course zoning Draft Generic Environmental Impact Statement, with § 301-208 in Appendix 1.2.C), page 179).

The program so far, and is there a bank?

The Planning Department's count for the 2024 Comprehensive Plan Update: since 2003, over 300 acres preserved and about 265 credits redeemed. About 43 credits had been issued to farm owners but not yet used: those are the credits a builder could buy (2024 Adopted Town of Riverhead Comprehensive Plan Update, Chapter 6, Agricultural Lands, page 93 of the PDF).

There is no TDR bank. The plan's recommendation “2.1. Establish a TDR Bank to facilitate transactions.” describes one: “A TDR bank would serve as a central, trusted repository for developers and landowners to deposit, purchase, and trade development rights ... Establishing a TDR Bank does come with administrative considerations; significant resources and funding would be needed for the bank’s establishment and ongoing long-term management.” It adds: “The Town does not currently have a list of interested farmers (sellers).” (2024 Adopted Town of Riverhead Comprehensive Plan Update, Chapter 6, recommendations 2.1 and 2.2, page 100 of the PDF). This site found no Town Board resolution creating a bank in the meeting records it holds (every regular meeting since January 2025). So buyers and sellers find each other privately, and the Town publishes no current count of the credits still available; the 2023 figure above is the latest.

Where credits can be used, and what one buys

From § 301-208 of the Town Code as amended December 17, 2024 (Town Board agenda packet, October 20, 2026 (Golf Course zoning Draft Generic Environmental Impact Statement, with § 301-208 in Appendix 1.2.C), Appendix 1.2.C, pages 732 to 734, downloaded by the Planning Department from eCode360 on 2026-04-22). In the commercial and industrial districts a credit buys floor area, up to a cap on the floor-area ratio (building floor area divided by lot area).

The rate has changed

Until December 2024 a credit bought 1,500 square feet of commercial floor area; since then it buys 3,000. That halves the number of credits a store needs for the same building, so each addition preserves half as much farmland. A draft rule for golf cottages was also cut in half, from one credit per cottage to one per 1,200 square feet, after the Town Board worried the cost would deter golf courses (Town Board agenda packet, October 20, 2026 (Golf Course zoning Draft Generic Environmental Impact Statement, with § 301-208 in Appendix 1.2.C), pages 178 and 179).

Case study: the Walmart on Route 58

The site is 20.722 acres (902,668 sq. ft.) in the Destination Retail Center (DRC) district. Without credits a building may cover 15% of the lot, 135,400 sq. ft. With credits it may reach a 0.30 floor-area ratio, 270,795 sq. ft. Everything above the first figure has to be paid for in credits.

What it costs, and what it preserves

  • The 1.312 credits still needed would cost about $104,960 at $80,000 a credit. That is this site's estimate: the price is the planners' figure from a different sale, and Walmart buys its credits privately.
  • The 45 credits bought in 2007 to 2020 stand for about 45 acres of farmland preserved, at one credit an acre. The Town's records do not give what was paid for them.
  • The room left under the 0.30 cap, 61,403 sq. ft., would take about 20.5 more credits at today's rate, roughly $1,637,405.
  • The 26,835 sq. ft. figure is the size of the addition. The building grows by 9,345 sq. ft. over what was approved because the addition goes where the outdoor garden center is.

The Planning Board's October 15, 2026 agenda carried two Walmart resolutions: No. 2026-067, taking the lead in the environmental review and issuing a negative declaration (no significant adverse environmental impact expected), and No. 2026-068, scheduling a public hearing on the site plan for Thursday, November 19, 2026 at 3:00 p.m. at Town Hall. The Board's minutes are not yet posted; RiverheadLOCAL reported that both were adopted. The RiverheadLOCAL, October 21, 2026: Walmart expansion set for Nov. 19 public hearing is a news report, not a Town record.

What residents may want to ask

  • The 2024 rate change halved the farmland each commercial addition preserves. Is 3,000 square feet a credit the right balance? The 2024 Comprehensive Plan Update itself recommends revisiting the formulas from time to time.
  • Prices are set privately, case by case. The Town's figure rests on one recent sale. The Town publishes no list of trades or prices, so residents cannot see what preservation is costing builders.
  • Credits fill a gap between what zoning allows and what a site can hold. On Route 58, what traffic, sewer and water capacity do the receiving districts have for the extra floor area?
