What is this website?

The Town publishes budgets, audits, payroll records, and meeting documents. They contain useful information, but the details can be difficult to find in long reports. This independent site organizes those public records into searchable pages with plain-language explanations. You can follow a number back to the document it came from. This is not the Town’s official website.

A 30-second budget primer

A town budget is a plan for what the Town expects to spend and how it expects to pay for it. Appropriations are the amounts set aside for services, salaries, supplies, and projects. The money comes from revenue such as fees, state aid, and charges, plus the property-tax levy—the total amount the Town plans to collect from property owners. If those sources do not cover the plan, the Town may use fund balance, which is money saved from earlier years. The Town keeps separate funds for different purposes, such as general services, highways, water, and sewer. Each fund has its own budget.

How the budget gets made

Each fall, Riverhead follows a budget calendar set by New York State law. The names can be confusing, so here is what each stage means:

  1. Department requests: Town departments submit estimates of what they think they will need. They are due to the budget officer by September 20.
  2. Tentative Budget: The Supervisor, acting as budget officer, puts the requests together into the first complete proposal. It must be filed with the Town Clerk by September 30 and presented to the Town Board by October 5.
  3. Preliminary Budget: The Town Board reviews and can change the Tentative Budget. This is the version the Board presents at the public hearing. Notice must be published at least five days beforehand; the hearing is held on the Thursday after the general election and cannot be postponed beyond November 15.
  4. Adopted Budget: The Town Board’s final budget for the year. It must be adopted by November 20 and is the plan the Town uses to authorize spending.

Important: If the Board does not vote to adopt a budget by November 20, the Preliminary Budget takes effect automatically under State law. In other words, no final vote does not stop the budget from taking effect.

The budget can still change during the year, but changes must be approved through formal Town Board action. These changes can include transfers between accounts, added spending for projects, or salary adjustments. They appear as resolutions in the Town Board Decisions record, so you can follow changes during the year. When the year ends, the Annual Report shows what actually happened compared with the plan.
