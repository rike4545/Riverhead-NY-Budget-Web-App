Industrial Development Agency tax breaks

What the Riverhead IDA’s projects were spared in taxes, what they paid back, and the jobs they reported, 2017 to 2024.

Independent, unofficial project·Data last refreshed November 14, 2026·Recently refreshed·See data update details →

In plain English

The Riverhead Industrial Development Agency is a public benefit corporation, separate from the Town Board, that can give businesses tax breaks to build or expand here. Every year it reports each project to the State’s Authorities Budget Office: the taxes the project did not pay, the PILOTs it paid instead, and the jobs the company says it has. This page adds up those reports. The figures are the agency’s, not estimates of ours.
  • Tax break: taxes a project did not have to pay because of its IDA deal: property tax, sales tax on construction and equipment, and mortgage recording tax.
  • PILOT: a “payment in lieu of taxes”: what a project pays instead of full property tax while its deal lasts.
  • Net tax break: the tax break minus the PILOTs paid. This is the part that went uncollected.

Eight years in total

From 2017 to 2024, the agency reported 46 projects. Together they were spared $45.8 million in taxes and paid $14.4 million in PILOTs, so $31.4 million went uncollected. In 2024 alone the net tax break was $4.5 million, across 21 active projects.

Two projects account for 43% of the total: Atlantis Holding Company, LLC ($7.2 million) and Calverton Addiction and Treatment Center ($6.3 million). Projects named for Atlantis, the downtown aquarium and hotel, add up to $8.1 million.

A tax break does not take money out of the Town’s budget. The Town sets its tax levy first, so property tax a project does not pay is spread across everyone else’s bills, or it is tax on a new building that might not exist without the deal. Which of the two applies is the question behind every IDA vote.

The school districts carry the largest share: $16.2 million of the $31.4 million. The State’s data calls the Town’s share “local”. Sales and mortgage recording tax breaks are one-time savings on construction and financing, with no PILOT against them.

Has it been a net gain or a net loss?

The records cannot settle that, because the real question is what would have happened without each deal, and no record answers it. Here is what the records do show.

  • On the cost side: $31.4 million in taxes went uncollected over eight years, about $3.9 million a year. Just over half of it, $16.2 million, was school tax.
  • On the jobs side: the 37 projects that got tax breaks reported 655 jobs before their deals and 2,007 in their latest reports, a gain of 1,352. Together they had promised 1,063 new jobs.
  • Promises: 18 of the 37 reported reaching the number of new jobs they promised; 19 did not.
  • 2024: the active projects reported 1,240 more jobs than before their deals, for a net tax break of $4.5 million, about $3,642 a year for each of those jobs.

Read the job figures with care. Each company reports its own count, and the State publishes it as reported, not audited. A job counted here may have moved from somewhere else on Long Island. The 9 projects with no tax break are left out of the job figures: for those, the agency only issued bonds. Peconic Bay Medical Center’s bonds alone came with 1,314 reported jobs that no tax break paid for.

The agency’s own money

The IDA pays its way mostly with fees it charges when a project closes. Its audited statements show:

In 2024, $251,489 of the revenue came from one project that closed that year. 2023 included a $135,875 transfer from the Riverhead IDA Economic Job Development Corporation, a related agency. Most of the spending is staff pay ($173,748 in 2024).

Oversight

In December 2024 the State’s Authorities Budget Office reviewed the agency’s website. RiverheadLOCAL reported that it found the website “does not meet the requirements of the State Public Authorities Law” and that committee meetings held by video had not followed the Open Meetings Law. The agency’s executive director answered that “almost all those corrective actions have been taken.” This is a news report: the review letter’s address on the State’s site no longer opens.
