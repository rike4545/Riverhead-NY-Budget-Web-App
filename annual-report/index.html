2025 Annual Financial Report

What actually happened with the Town's money in 2025 — the independent audit's General Fund result, and the year-end results for every fund filed with the New York State Comptroller, compared with the prior two years and with the 2025 budget plan.

Independent project·Updated September 25, 2026·● Current·Check data freshness →
💡In plain English
This page shows the Town's actual 2025 results. The headline, from the independent audit the Town Board accepted on September 1, 2026: the General Fund added $4,899,127 to its savings, which reached $33,303,051. Revenue grew 7.5% on 2024 and spending 5.6%.
  • ›Budget vs. actual: The budget is the plan made before the year starts. This report is the scorecard afterward — what was really collected and spent.
  • ›Surplus: When a fund takes in more than it spends in a year, the extra adds to its savings (fund balance). A deficit draws savings down.
  • ›Two sources: The General Fund figures at the top are the independent audit's. Everything else comes from the Town's own Annual Financial Report, which is not audited. Columns show 2025, 2024, and 2023 so you can see the direction of travel.

The audit and the Town's own report

The Town files its Annual Financial Report with the State Comptroller each spring, before the audit is done, and the report is not audited. For 2025 the two end the General Fund's year $104,200 apart, and they sort the balance into its five tiers differently. The report counts less as Assigned, so its unassigned balance, the figure every reserve test measures, runs higher. The site uses the audit's wherever there is one.

What the audit counts as Assigned: $1,250,000 to help balance the next year's budget, $596,960 of purchase orders still open at year-end, and $553,685 of designations, most of it for a Teen Center ($25,136), the Senior Day Care Center ($48,129), improvement and maintenance of Stotzky Park ($68,574) and community benefit funds ($342,821). The report has $1,663,273 in that tier. It is not a one-year quirk: for 2024 the report's unassigned balance was $24,464,613 and the audit's $23,887,952. The General Fund's balance is also net of year-end deficits in two programs reported inside it: the Recreation Program Fund ($692,688) and the Police Athletic League ($73,185).

The Town planned to spend $64,852,829. By its own report it actually spent $66,007,499 but took in $71,010,826, so revenues came in well above plan — which is what produced the report's $5,003,327 surplus. These totals include transfers between funds and are counted differently from the audit's, which is why they run higher than the audited figures above.

What's in the General Fund's $33.3M savings?

Fund balance is split into categories by how freely it can be spent. These are the audit's; the Town's unaudited report is shown under each.

Nonspendable$2,012,534 (6%)

Money that is not available to spend (for example, prepaid items or inventory).

Restricted$17,924 (0%)

Money that can only be spent on a legally required purpose.

Committed$42,435 (0%)

Savings the Town Board has formally set aside for a specific use.

Assigned$2,400,645 (7%)

Savings the Town intends to use for a particular purpose but has not formally locked in. Town’s unaudited report: $1,663,273.

Unassigned$28,829,513 (87%)

The true "rainy-day" savings — money with no strings attached that the Town can use for any purpose. Town’s unaudited report: $29,671,084.
