💡In plain English
This page shows the Town's actual 2025 results. The headline: the General Fund took in about $5.0 million more than it spent, growing its savings to $33,407,251.
  • ›Budget vs. actual: The budget is the plan made before the year starts. This report is the scorecard afterward — what was really collected and spent.
  • ›Surplus: When a fund takes in more than it spends in a year, the extra adds to its savings (fund balance). A deficit draws savings down.
  • ›All figures: come straight from the Town's official filing. Columns show 2025, 2024, and 2023 so you can see the direction of travel.

The Town planned to spend $64,852,829. It actually spent $66,007,499 but took in $71,010,826, so revenues came in well above plan — which is what produced the $5,003,327 surplus.

What's in the General Fund's $33.4M savings?

Fund balance is split into categories by how freely it can be spent.

Nonspendable$2,012,534 (6%)

Money that is not available to spend (for example, prepaid items or inventory).

Restricted$17,924 (0%)

Money that can only be spent on a legally required purpose.

Committed$42,435 (0%)

Savings the Town Board has formally set aside for a specific use.

Assigned$1,663,273 (5%)

Savings the Town intends to use for a particular purpose but has not formally locked in.

Unassigned$29,671,084 (89%)

The true "rainy-day" savings — money with no strings attached that the Town can use for any purpose.
