What Changed in Riverhead’s Budget?

Start with the big picture, then see which funds changed the most. Compare adopted appropriations from 2020 through 2026, and the 2027 Tentative as proposed, and follow the evidence into the underlying records.

Independent project·Updated October 8, 2026·Current·Check data freshness →

The 2027 Tentative proposes $116,864,850 of spending, $4,142,096 more than 2026’s adopted budget (+3.7%).

That leaves out the three funds the others pay for: debt service, workers’ compensation and risk retention. Counting every fund, as the budget’s Summary page does, the total goes from $121,110,904 to $120,958,878, down $152,026, because debt service falls $4,294,122. The Supervisor’s letter gives the same figure: “Total appropriations for the town operating fund in 2027 are $116,864,850, a stable growth of $4,142,096 over 2026.” The comparison below ranks the funds responsible for the largest moves.

In plain English

This page turns a large budget into a simpler question: what changed, where did it change, and how much?
  • Appropriations: means planned spending. An appropriation increase does not automatically equal the same increase in your property-tax bill.
  • Start here: use “Biggest $ change” to find the largest budget movements; use “Biggest % change” to spot smaller funds with unusually large swings.
  • Follow the evidence: click a fund name to move from the comparison into that fund’s detailed records and multi-year history.

Without the funds the others pay for (debt service, workers’ compensation and risk retention, whose only revenue is transfers from the other funds), appropriations go from $112,722,754 to $116,864,850, a change of $4,142,096 (+3.7%). Adding those three funds counts the same dollars twice, so a fall in debt payments can look like a spending cut.
