Records to request

Some of what residents need to follow the Town’s money is not published. Here are the records this site looked for and did not find, with a ready request for each under the Freedom of Information Law, and the deadlines the Town has to meet.

Independent, unofficial project·Data last refreshed November 8, 2026·Recently refreshed·See data update details →

The records

The 2027 fund balance breakdown

Town Law §107 requires the preliminary budget to show each fund’s estimated balance with a breakdown, and the balance left after the budget “shall not exceed a reasonable amount.” The 2027 Tentative’s fund balance page lists the General Fund at $33,303,051 at the end of 2026, the same as the audited balance a year earlier, and has no column for the balance left after the 2027 budget.

The request asks for: The statement of estimated fund balances prepared for the 2027 Preliminary Budget under Town Law §107, showing for each fund the estimated balance at December 31, 2026, the amounts encumbered, reserved and appropriated for 2027, and the estimated unappropriated unreserved balance remaining, together with any worksheet the Accounting Department used to prepare it.

Town Law §107 · Town of Riverhead, 2027 Tentative Budget, Fund Balances · Reserves and fund balance on this site

The 2025 federal single audit and corrective action plan

RiverheadLOCAL reported on September 24, 2026 that the auditors found failures in the Town’s handling of a $3.575 million EPA grant. The report on federal awards that carries such findings, and the Town’s plan to correct them, are separate from the financial statements the Board accepted on September 1. This site has not found them posted.

The request asks for: The Town’s single audit report on federal awards for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, including the schedule of expenditures of federal awards, the schedule of findings and questioned costs, and the Town’s corrective action plan responding to those findings.

RiverheadLOCAL, September 24, 2026: Riverhead audit flags failures handling $3.57M EPA grant · Annual report on this site

The current payroll record

Every agency must keep “a record setting forth the name, public office address, title and salary of every officer or employee of the agency” (Public Officers Law §87(3)(b)). This site’s payroll runs through 2025, and some Board resolutions name employees only by payroll file number, which a current record would let residents match.

The request asks for: The record required by Public Officers Law §87(3)(b) setting forth the name, public office address, title and salary of every officer or employee of the Town, as of the date of this request, including each employee’s payroll file number where the Town’s payroll system records it.

Public Officers Law §87 · Payroll explorer on this site

The subject matter list and records retention schedule

Every agency must keep a “reasonably detailed current list by subject matter” of all its records (Public Officers Law §87(3)(c)), and the Town’s own policy requires it to be updated each year. With it, a resident can ask for a record by name. This site has not found it posted.

The request asks for: The Town’s current subject matter list of records maintained under Public Officers Law §87(3)(c), showing the date of its most recent update, and the Town Board resolution adopting the records retention and disposition schedule the Town follows.

Public Officers Law §87 · Town of Riverhead, 2023 Public Access to Records Policy, §6

How to send it

Under the Town’s public access policy, requests go in writing to the Town Clerk’s Office, 4 West Second Street, Riverhead, New York 11901. The policy says “Applicants are strongly encouraged to request records via the Online FOIL Submission Form”, reached through the “F.O.I.L. Electronic Request” link on the Town’s home page at townofriverheadny.gov. The Clerk logs each request and sends it to the department that holds the records. State law also requires an agency that has email to “accept requests for records submitted in the form of electronic mail” (Public Officers Law §89(3)(b)).

There is no fee to search for or inspect records. Paper copies are twenty-five (.25) cents per page; electronic copies of electronic records usually cost nothing. Keep a dated copy of what you send.

The deadlines

  • 5 business days for the Town to provide the records, deny them in writing, or acknowledge the request with a date: “within five business days of the receipt of a written request for a record reasonably described” (Public Officers Law §89(3)(a)).
  • 20 business days after acknowledging it, as a rule, to grant or deny it: “within twenty business days from the date of the acknowledgement of the receipt of the request”. More time needs a written reason and a date certain.
  • No answer counts as a denial. “Failure by an agency to conform to the provisions of subdivision three of this section shall constitute a denial.” (Public Officers Law §89(4)(a)).
  • 30 days to appeal a denial: “within thirty days appeal in writing such denial to the head, chief executive or governing body of the entity” (Public Officers Law §89(4)(a)). In Riverhead, appeals go to the Town Attorney, as FOIL Records Appeals Officer, 4 West Second Street, Riverhead, New York 11901.
  • 10 business days for the Town to decide the appeal: the agency “shall within ten business days of the receipt of such appeal fully explain in writing” (Public Officers Law §89(4)(a)). The Town’s 2023 policy still says “A failure to determine an appeal within fifteen business days of its receipt”. That looks out of date against the statute, which the Town follows; it is a question for the Town Attorney, not a legal finding.

Business days skip weekends and the State’s public holidays (General Construction Law §24).

Tips

  • Ask for records that exist, not for answers or new analysis. Name the document, the office and the dates.
  • You don’t have to say why you want them.
  • Ask for electronic copies by email.
  • If the Town says no record exists, you may ask it to certify that it searched and could not find one.
  • The State’s Committee on Open Government publishes advisory opinions you can cite in an appeal. The New York Coalition for Open Government’s FOIL toolkit has more guidance and templates.
