Assessment roll

What the Town's property is worth, by kind, from the Assessor's 2026 tentative and final assessment rolls. Totals only: no owner, address or single property.

Independent, unofficial project·Data last refreshed November 7, 2026·Recently refreshed·See data update details →

In plain English

The 2026 final roll lists 16,562 parcels assessed at $1,136,208,305, which is a market value of about $14.70 billion. Homes make up 52.7% of that value and commercial property 18.6%. After exemptions, $854,846,090 of the assessed value is taxable for the Town's tax: 75.2% of the roll.
  • Assessed value: Riverhead assesses property at 7.73% of its market value (the State calls this the uniform percentage). An assessed value of $46,300 means a market value of about $598,965.
  • Taxable value: the assessed value less exemptions. Tax rates are applied to it, so an exemption on one property shifts its share of the levy onto everyone else.
  • Two rolls: the tentative roll comes out in May; owners can grieve their assessment; the final roll in September is what the 2027 tax bills use.

What the Town's property is worth, by kind

Community services (class 600) are schools, churches, hospitals, libraries and the like; public services (800) are utilities and pipelines; class 900 is mostly parkland and preserved open space.

What a one-family home is worth

The roll's market values for the Town's 10,941 taxable one-family homes. The median is $598,965: half are worth less and half more. The middle half fall between $425,614 and $783,959.

A roll's market value is the assessment divided by 7.73%, valued as of July 1, 2025. It is not a sale price or an appraisal of any one house.

What is exempt, and why

1,371 parcels are wholly exempt: $182,897,036 assessed, about $2.37 billion of market value. Most are owned by governments, schools and nonprofits. Partial exemptions such as veterans', seniors' and farm exemptions lower the taxable value of thousands more. The chart shows each group's reduction to the value the Town's tax is levied on.

STAR is a school-tax exemption only: it takes $29,908,825 off the school districts' taxable value on 3,499 homes and nothing off the Town's. IDA exemptions are for businesses that receive a tax break from the Town's Industrial Development Agency, which usually pay a PILOT (payment in lieu of taxes) instead; see the budget flow.

What changed between the tentative and final rolls

The final roll's assessed value is $2,613,659 lower than the tentative roll's, about $33.8 million of market value. Changes between the two rolls include the Board of Assessment Review's decisions on grievances, corrections and the State's values for utility property; the rolls do not say which caused each change.

Sources and method

  • The 2026/27 Final Assessment Roll, revised 9/17/26 and the 2026/27 Tentative Assessment Roll, from the Assessor's page. Valuation date July 1, 2025; taxable status date March 1, 2026.
  • Every parcel record on each roll is added up by its property class. The sums match the roll's own totals for each roll section, parcel for parcel and dollar for dollar, or the page is not rebuilt.
  • Roll sections: taxable, State-owned land, special franchises (utility lines in streets, valued by the State), utilities and wholly exempt. Grand totals and the school and exemption summaries are the roll's own pages.
  • This page publishes totals only. The rolls themselves are public and list each owner; look up one property on the Assessor's roll, not here.
  • For what the Town's tax rate does with these values, see your tax bill.
