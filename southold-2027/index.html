Southold’s 2027 Tentative beside Riverhead’s

The Town of Southold’s 2027 Tentative Operating Budget, filed with its Town Clerk on September 30, 2026, set beside Riverhead’s: savings, the tax cap, police, revenue estimates, garbage, and what each budget book tells residents.

Independent project·Updated October 19, 2026·Current·Check data freshness →

In plain English

Southold, Riverhead’s neighbour on the North Fork, also runs its own police department, and its four main funds come to $65,936,738 for 2027, up 2.8%. Like Riverhead’s, its Tentative stays within the tax cap: its property tax for those funds rises 2.87%, Riverhead’s town-wide levy 2.79%. The biggest difference is savings. Southold’s General Fund counts $4,000,000 of them as income, 8.4% of its spending; Riverhead’s counts $950,000, 1.3%. Southold starts from a bigger cushion, and budgets its interest and mortgage tax closer to what it collects.
  • Two proposals: both are Tentative budgets, each Supervisor’s proposal. Each Town Board can change its own before it adopts a budget by November 20.
  • Savings: “appropriated fund balance”, which Southold’s Summary calls “unexpended balance”: money left from earlier years that a budget counts as income.
  • Read from a scan: Southold’s copy has no text layer, so its figures were read from the page images and checked by adding up its own tables.

At a glance

Southold’s four main funds are the ones its Tax Statistics page sets out (Southold PDF pp. 6–7). They include its solid waste district; Riverhead’s town-wide funds do not include its Refuse and Garbage District, so the garbage row shows both. Southold’s cushion is its budget’s own estimate of what will be left at the end of 2026 after its 2026 budget’s planned use; Riverhead’s is audited, a year earlier.

Three neighbours

With Brookhaven, whose 2027 Tentative this site set beside Riverhead’s as well, the three towns cover the range on savings:

The growth factors are the 2% allowable levy growth factor times each town’s 2027 tax base growth factor from the Department of Taxation and Finance (1.0025, 1.0087 and 1.0077). They are not the whole limit: each town also adjusts for payments in lieu of taxes, any carryover and exclusions, and files the result with the State Comptroller. Riverhead’s town-wide levy rises by what the two factors allow, to two decimals; Southold’s and Brookhaven’s rise by less.

Brookhaven balances its General Fund without savings. Riverhead uses a little. Southold uses the most, and holds the most. Each town’s “main funds” are its own budget’s grouping, so the per-resident tax is a rough guide: Brookhaven’s residents also pay the County police district, and Riverhead’s figure leaves out its refuse district.

Per resident

Counting garbage in both, the two towns raise about the same property tax per resident for their main funds: $1,905 in Southold and $1,874 in Riverhead. Riverhead spends more on police per resident. Southold budgets its dispatch centre separately, as Public Safety Communications ($1,437,650); Riverhead’s Budget Supplement has no such line, so its police lines may include that work.

A per-resident figure is not a tax bill. Businesses pay part of each levy, and so do owners of homes whose residents the Census counts elsewhere. Southold’s figure includes the Village of Greenport, whose residents pay the whole-town funds but not the funds outside the village.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, Annual Estimates of the Resident Population for Minor Civil Divisions in New York (SUB-EST2025).

What Riverhead residents can take from it

Southold leans on savings more than Riverhead, from a bigger cushion

Southold’s General Fund counts $4,000,000 of savings as income in 2027, the same as in 2026: 8.4% of its $47,340,762. Its four main funds count $5,050,000, 7.7% of their spending. Riverhead’s General Fund counts $950,000, 1.3%, and its town-wide funds 1.2%.

Southold can afford it for now. Its fund-balance page (Southold PDF p. 8) expects the General Fund to end 2026 with $24,180,345, 51.1% of its 2027 spending, after the $4,000,000 its 2026 budget plans to use. Its General Fund took in $1,081,941 more than it spent in 2025, by its fund pages’ 2025 actual columns. Riverhead’s audited General Fund grew $4,899,127 in 2025. In both towns, a planned draw is a budget’s assumption; whether it is needed is known only when the year closes. Riverhead’s reserves.

Both stay within the tax cap this year

Southold’s Supervisor writes of the cap: “Nevertheless, it serves as a benchmark we strive to remain under, as a measure of sound budgeting and responsible management.” and “This budget reflects that philosophy and does not pierce the cap.” Its property tax for the four funds rises 2.87%. The Suffolk Times reports that Southold’s 2026 budget also stayed within the state tax cap, after its 2025 budget raised the levy 7.48% and went above it.

Riverhead’s letters tell a longer story. 2025: “The Town-wide 2025 Budget is 4.14% over the tax cap, due in large part to increases to insurance premiums and retirement systems contributions.” 2026: “The 2026 town-wide budget is 4.63% over the tax cap.” 2027: “There is joy in presenting this budget as all taxing districts are budgeted within the tax cap limit of 2.79%.” Neither budget prints its limit in dollars, and Southold’s gives no figure at all.

Both run their own police, and both police budgets grow about 3.8%

Southold’s police department goes from $10,620,728 in its 2026 budget to $11,028,744, up 3.84% (Southold PDF p. 14). Riverhead’s goes from $22,388,906 to $23,244,090, up 3.82%. Southold’s Supervisor writes: “Previous budgets relied on federal ARPA funds to support recurring expenses, such as police vehicles. These critical vehicles are now included in this budget.” Brookhaven, by contrast, has no police department of its own.

Southold budgets its revenue closer to what it collects

Southold estimates its General Fund’s interest for 2027 at $925,000, 68% of the $1,369,072 it collected in 2025. Riverhead’s Tentative estimates $750,000, 50% of its $1,491,222; the amendments would make it $1,000,000, 67%. On mortgage tax, Southold estimates $1,500,000, 78% of the $1,920,134 it collected in 2025, and Riverhead $1,200,000, 73% of its $1,634,711.

Southold’s Supervisor gives a reason for caution: “Forecasted sales tax revenue has not increased, and mortgage tax revenue has significantly decreased.” Its sales tax estimate, $1,759,073, is the same figure in every column of its page. What closer estimates would do for Riverhead’s levy is on Zero or 1% for 2027.

Garbage: charges in Southold, property tax in Riverhead

Southold’s Solid Waste Management District spends $5,674,231 in 2027, up 7.41%. Of that, $3,376,092 comes from charges and other revenue, the largest part of it tipping fees, $250,000 from savings and $2,048,139 from property tax, up 5.44%. Riverhead’s Refuse and Garbage District spends $5,302,990, up 0.92%, all of it from property tax.

Southold’s budget book shows more of the picture

Its Tax Statistics pages (Southold PDF pp. 6–7) set out, for 2026 and 2027, each fund’s assessed value, spending, tax rate, levy, revenue and savings, with the change in each. That is where a resident can see why the tax rate rose 2.29% while the levy rose 2.87%: the whole town’s assessed value grew 0.59%. Its three-year capital plan (Southold PDF pp. 81–82) lists each project, from police vehicles to a screening plant, and how it would be paid for: $3,006,700 in 2027, $2,812,000 in 2028 and $2,188,000 in 2029. And its letter says how many people the Town employs: “All 217 full-time employees and 49 part-time employees are partners in this budget, and I appreciate their hard work.”

Riverhead’s Tentative has neither page. Its table of contents lists no capital plan, and it prints the town’s total assessed value only in the assessor’s exemption report at the back, not beside its rates. Both towns’ letters are scanned images: Southold’s whole budget is a scan.

How Riverhead’s budget could be improved

Ideas a resident could put to the Town Board, drawn from what Southold’s budget does. They are suggestions, not findings that Riverhead has done anything wrong. Several match what the Government Finance Officers Association asks of a budget document (Standards).

  1. Print a capital plan with the budget. Southold’s lists three years of projects and says how each year is paid for, including how much is borrowed: $1,355,000 of 2027’s $3,006,700.
  2. Show the assessed value behind each tax rate. Southold’s Tax Statistics page puts each fund’s assessed value beside its rate and levy, so a resident can see why the rate moves differently from the levy. Riverhead’s rate rises 2.27% against a levy up 2.79%, on a tax base about 0.50% larger, but its Summary doesn’t say so.
  3. Say how many people the Town employs. Southold’s letter does: 217 full-time and 49 part-time.
  4. Explain the revenue estimates against what came in. Southold’s interest estimate is 68% of its 2025 collections; Riverhead’s Tentative’s is 50%. A line saying why would help residents judge the levy.
  5. Ask whether some garbage costs could be charged by use. Southold covers 59% of its solid waste district from charges. Charging would move cost from the levy to users; it would not make it smaller. It would also move it outside the tax cap: the State Comptroller writes that “User fees are not taxes subject to the levy limit.”
  6. Publish the Supervisor’s letter as text. Riverhead’s 2025, 2026 and 2027 letters are scanned images, and Southold’s whole budget is a scan: a screen reader cannot read them and a search cannot find them.

Where the two towns differ

  • Size. Southold has 24,260 residents and Riverhead 36,394, by the Census Bureau’s estimates for July 1, 2025.
  • A village. The Village of Greenport is in Southold (Southold PDF p. 4), so Southold keeps General and Highway funds for the town outside the village. Riverhead’s budget has no such funds.
  • Fire districts. Southold’s Summary (Southold PDF p. 5) lists its six fire districts’ budgets, $12,591,640, and their taxes in its $138,222,897 grand total. Riverhead’s Summary lists no fire districts.
  • Fishers Island. Southold’s budget carries the island’s ferry district ($8,365,639), waste management and sewer districts.
  • Land and housing funds. Southold’s Community Preservation Fund ($39,321,985) and Community Housing Fund ($6,763,446) use $30,565,625 of their own savings in 2027. They build balances from their own revenue and then spend them, so that is not the kind of draw this page compares. Riverhead’s Community Preservation Fund budget is far smaller.
  • Water and sewer. Riverhead runs a water district and three sewer funds. Southold’s budget has only the Fishers Island sewer district, $66,100.
  • Tax rates. Southold’s total rate is 407.763 per $1,000 of assessed value and Riverhead’s town-wide rate 73.224, but each town assesses property at its own fraction of market value, so the rates can’t be compared. Their changes can: 2.29% and 2.27%.
  • Stage. Both are proposals. The Suffolk Times reports Southold’s public hearings on November 4. Either Town Board may change its budget before it adopts one.

How this page was made

Southold’s Tentative came to this site as an 82-page scan with no text, from the Town’s website. Each page was read with OCR, upright and turned a quarter-turn each way, and every figure and quotation on this page was then read from the page image. Each table’s rows add to its printed totals (the fund-balance totals to within a dollar, as the scan rounds), the Summary agrees with the Tax Statistics pages and the fund pages, and each quotation and line figure is checked against the OCR text of its page. A check runs on every build of this site and fails if any of that stops being true. The OCR text is kept in the site’s repository beside the scan.

Riverhead’s figures come from the records this site already checks: the Tentative’s Summary and pages, the 2027 Budget Supplement and the audited statements.
