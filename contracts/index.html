Contracts and bids

Who the Town Board agreed to pay in 2026: bid awards, bid extensions, purchases, change orders and service agreements, each with the vendor and amount in the resolution’s own words.

Independent project·Updated October 23, 2026·Current·Check data freshness →

At 19 meetings from January 1 through October 20, 2026, the Board voted on 90 resolutions that choose a vendor or set the terms for one. 81 of them name the vendor in a form this list can read. Only 16 state a dollar figure in the resolution itself. Most bid awards (13) say the prices are “on the attached pages”, so their amounts are left blank here rather than read from an attachment.

  • Sound Engineering, P.C.: 2 resolutions (2026-756, 2026-757)

A count of resolutions, not dollars. A renewal or an addendum counts as its own resolution.

The amounts that are stated

6 resolutions state an amount, together $1,109,771. Another 10 set a ceiling (“not to exceed”), together $131,719. A ceiling is the most the Town may pay, not what it paid.

These totals leave out every award whose prices are in an attachment, so they are far below what the Town committed. What it actually pays each vendor is in the bills the Board approves at each meeting, which this list does not read.

How this list is made

  • It is read from each 2026 Town Board agenda packet on CivicClerk, through the October 20, 2026 meeting, and rebuilt by running etl/build_contract_awards.py. The vote and result come from the meeting record.
  • A vendor is listed only where the resolution names it: in its title, in a RESOLVED clause, or in the block of vendors a bid award lists. The vendors’ e-mail addresses and phone numbers are left out.
  • Change orders name the engineer’s earlier agreement but not the contractor, so their vendor is left blank.
  • Left out: notices to bidders and requests for proposals (no one is chosen yet), rejected bids, licenses and leases of Town property, agreements with other governments, grants, labor agreements, and agreements setting a Town employee’s terms.
  • Names are set in ordinary case where the packet prints them in capitals. The packet’s spelling is kept.
  • This is an unofficial list. Check a figure against the packet before relying on it.
