Riverhead Payroll Explorer

A searchable, SeeThroughNY-style record of actual Town of Riverhead employee earnings 2018–2025 — base pay, overtime, and total gross pay by employee, title, department, and union.

Independent project·Updated October 21, 2026·Current·Check data freshness →

In plain English

Start with the public record. This page lets you search what Riverhead employees were actually paid, then move into salary, overtime, separation-pay, and police-step analysis. The analytical views are built on the underlying payroll data rather than replacing it.
  • Start with Employees & Pay: Search a name, title, or department to see what employees were actually paid. Click an employee name to follow that person across years.
  • Actual vs. authorized: Actual pay is what appeared in the payroll record. Authorized salary is the base pay the Board set; overtime, longevity, and buy-outs can make actual gross pay higher.
  • Analysis layers: Salaries & Raises, Overtime & Staffing, Separation Pay, and Police Pay Steps answer narrower questions after you understand the underlying payroll record.
How the payroll file numbers (GFZ numbers) work

The Town Board often names an employee by payroll file number instead of by name: Resolution 2026-888 (October 6, 2026), for example, sends “employee #GFZ000152” to a conference. GFZ is the Town’s payroll company code, and the six digits are the person’s file number. You can type one into the search box below, or into site search, in any form: “GFZ 000980”, “File # 000980” or just “980”.

The Town has not published how it assigns the numbers. Matching each of the 974 numbers on its roster to the hire year in the payroll exports shows four blocks:

So the numbering looks like it began around 2016. Everyone then working was numbered by seniority, then former employees still drawing pay, and since then each new hire has taken the next number. Carroll, Bryan, 000980, was hired in 2018; that year’s hires run from 000975 to 001072. A lower number means longer service only within a block: 000700 belongs to someone hired in 1973, not to someone senior to 000600.

Numbers stay with a person and are not reused: the 5 numbers that appear under two names each belong to one person whose name changed or was spelled two ways. The roster this site holds stops at 001308, people hired through 2021, so anyone hired since then has no file number here. The Board has cited numbers as high as GFZ001716 (Resolution 2026-951, October 20, 2026). The payroll exports carry no file number, so this site links each number to a person by name, and the hire years are years, not dates. One former employee’s hire year reads 1900 in the exports, an apparent placeholder left out of the ranges above.

Personnel actions the Board took by file number (7)

These Town Board resolutions take a disciplinary, termination, separation or similar action and name the employee only by payroll file number. The name beside each is the one the Town’s payroll roster gives that number; 4 of the 7 numbers are on it. The title and department are that person’s latest in the payroll exports.

A resolution is an action, not a finding. Disciplinary charges are allegations until they are decided, and a stipulation or separation agreement does not say what it settles. The match is by number on a roster that runs through file number 001308, so a higher number has no name here. No resolution in this site’s records names an employee by number in a grievance.

Agreements and stipulations with the unions (21)

A stipulation is a side agreement with a union that changes the contract’s terms for one employee or a group: a title the salary schedule lacks, a merit step, an insurance buyout. This site’s meeting records run in full from January 2025, and these are every agreement and stipulation with a union in them, the first on January 7, 2025: 14 with the CSEA, 3 with the Police Benevolent Association, 5 with the Superior Officers Association. One covers both police unions.

The clauses are the resolution’s own words, shortened where marked …; this site has the text of 8 of the 21. For the rest, the title is all the minutes give; the Town’s agenda packet for that meeting has the full resolution.

What do these columns mean?
Regular
Base salary or wages — the normal pay, not counting overtime.
Overtime
Extra pay for hours worked beyond the normal schedule.
Other pay
Everything on top of base and overtime: longevity, holiday and shift differentials, stipends, retroactive pay, and leave/termination buy-outs. That last group mixes sick and vacation buy-backs with severance and health-insurance opt-out buy-backs — only the buy-backs are accrued leave. Hover the column heading, or click a row, for the breakdown.
Gross Pay
Base pay + overtime + other pay, all added together — the total actually paid for the year.
Group
The union or bargaining group the employee belongs to (for example PBA for police, CSEA for many town workers). Elected, appointed, management and part-time staff are not union-covered.
Titles marked “·carried”
The Town’s payroll export only includes job titles and departments from 2022 onward. Where someone held one unchanging title across every year on record, it is carried back to their earlier years and marked — it is an inference, not something the Town reported for that year. Anyone whose title ever changed is left blank instead, so a later promotion is never written into an earlier year.
Tip: click any employee row to expand a full breakdown of how their gross pay is built — base + overtime + each addition (longevity, holiday, stipends, buy-outs, retro) adds up to the total.
