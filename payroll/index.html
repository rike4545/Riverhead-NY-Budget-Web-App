In plain English

Start with the public record. This page lets you search what Riverhead employees were actually paid, then move into salary, overtime, separation-pay, and police-step analysis. The analytical views are built on the underlying payroll data rather than replacing it.
  • Start with Employees & Pay: Search a name, title, or department to see what employees were actually paid. Click an employee name to follow that person across years.
  • Actual vs. authorized: Actual pay is what the payroll records show was paid. Authorized salary is the employee’s approved base rate. Overtime, longevity payments, and payouts for unused time can make total pay higher.
  • Analysis layers: The other sections look more closely at salary changes, overtime, payments when employees leave, and police pay steps.
Raises 2026 → 2027: what is already set

No one’s 2027 salary is official yet: the Board sets them in its salary resolution at the January 2027 organizational meeting. But contracts and the budget-hearing notice already fix much of it. Of the 331 salaried people on the 2026 authorized list, outside the elected officials:

WhoPeople2027
CSEA members161Set by contract. The 2026–2029 CSEA agreement (Article 15(2), Wages), which the Board approved December 16, 2025 (Resolution 2025-1001), raises every step of the salary schedule 2.5% and then adds $1,000. On their 2026 salaries that is $445,531 more in all, a median of $2,714 (4.0%), from 3.3% on the highest salaries to 4.7% on the lowest. Anyone not at the top step also moves up a step, so this is the least each one’s base should rise.
Police: PBA and Superior Officers80 PBA, 12 SOANot set. Both contracts expire December 31, 2026, and no successor has come before the Board. Until one is signed, the 2026 salary schedule stands, but step movement continues under the Taylor Law’s Triborough Amendment (Civil Service Law § 209-a(1)(e)). On the Police Officer ladder alone that is about $630,505 for the 39 officers still climbing it, this site’s estimate; the 35 at the top step get nothing from it, and detectives and sergeants move on their own schedules. The agreement that continues is the 2023–2026 PBA memorandum of agreement.
Managers, appointees and others78Not set until the January salary resolution. This includes 29 whose name could not be matched to a single union in the 2025 payroll export.

Elected officials: no raises proposed. The public notice of the November 5, 2026 budget hearing, attached to Resolution 2026-923 (October 6, 2026) lists each 2027 proposed salary at its 2026 amount: Town Supervisor $118,919; Town Council (4) $50,558; Town Justice (2) $96,872; Tax Receiver $96,085; Assessor Chair $110,663; Assessors (2) $96,085; Town Clerk $96,085; Town Clerk stipend, as Registrar of Vital Statistics $5,000; Superintendent of Highways $107,967. Raises already added to the Preliminary Budget. Amendment #2 (Resolution 2026-936) adds $1,603 of pay because “the Highway Superintendent requested an adjustment to the 2027 Preliminary Budget for an additional merit increase for an administrative employee in the General Fund”. Amendment #4 (Resolution 2026-938) adds $4,205 of pay because “the Highway Superintendent requested an adjustment to the 2027 Preliminary Budget for an additional promotion for one employee and an additional merit increase for one employee in the Highway Fund”. How the payroll file numbers (GFZ numbers) work

The Town Board often names an employee by payroll file number instead of by name: Resolution 2026-888 (October 6, 2026), for example, sends “employee #GFZ000152” to a conference. GFZ is the Town’s payroll company code, and the six digits are the person’s file number. You can type one into the search box below, or into site search, in any form: “GFZ 000980”, “File # 000980” or just “980”.

The Town has not published how it assigns the numbers. Matching each of the 974 numbers on its roster to the hire year in the payroll exports shows four blocks:

So the numbering looks like it began around 2016. Everyone then working was numbered by seniority, then former employees still drawing pay, and since then each new hire has taken the next number. Carroll, Bryan, 000980, was hired in 2018; that year’s hires run from 000975 to 001072. A lower number means longer service only within a block: 000700 belongs to someone hired in 1973, not to someone senior to 000600.

Numbers stay with a person and are not reused: the 5 numbers that appear under two names each belong to one person whose name changed or was spelled two ways. The roster this site holds stops at 001308, people hired through 2021, so anyone hired since then has no file number here. The Board has cited numbers as high as GFZ001716 (Resolution 2026-951, October 20, 2026). The payroll exports carry no file number, so this site links each number to a person by name, and the hire years are years, not dates. One former employee’s hire year reads 1900 in the exports, an apparent placeholder left out of the ranges above.

How this table handles privacy and employment law

This is context for residents, not legal advice. What New York law says, and what the table does about it:

  • Names, titles and pay are public. Public Officers Law § 87(3)(b) requires every agency to keep “a record setting forth the name, public office address, title and salary of every officer or employee of the agency.” That is all the table shows about a person.
  • What police records must leave out, this table never carries. Public Officers Law § 89(2-b): “a law enforcement agency shall redact the following information.” Before releasing police disciplinary records, an agency must remove medical history, home addresses, personal phone numbers and e-mail addresses, Social Security numbers, and use of employee assistance or counseling services. The same subdivision says it does not override the other laws that make “title, salary, and dates of employment” public. This table shows none of the redacted kinds of information. Each quotation is also checked for Social Security numbers, phone numbers and e-mail addresses, and any found are removed.
  • Charges are not findings. Civil Service Law § 75(2): “The burden of proving incompetency or misconduct shall be upon the person alleging the same.” Civil Service Law § 75(3) allows a suspension without pay pending a hearing “may be suspended without pay for a period not exceeding thirty days,” and under Town Law § 155 a town police officer who is not convicted “he shall be entitled to full pay from the date of suspension.”
  • Only the Town’s words are quoted; the name is reviewed. Civil Rights Law § 74 protects a fair and true report of an official proceeding, but “This section does not apply to a libel contained in any other matter added by any person concerned in the publication.” The resolution text here is the Town’s own; the name is this site’s match. So no name is shown until a person has checked it, and each check lapses after three to six months.

How each match is reviewed. Before a name is shown, a reviewer confirms that the file number is on the roster once; that the person’s payroll title and department fit the resolution; that the quotation is the resolution’s own words with nothing the law requires removed; that any later resolution about the same number has been read; and that the note on what the action means is right. A new match from the meeting records shows no name until then. When a later resolution, such as a determination of charges, cites the same number, it appears in that row.

Ask for a correction or a review. If an entry is wrong or out of date, or a person named here wants it reviewed, open a request. Requests are public, so give only the resolution number and what is wrong, with no personal details.

What do these columns mean?
Regular
Base salary or wages — the normal pay, not counting overtime.
Overtime
Extra pay for hours worked beyond the normal schedule.
Other pay
Everything on top of base and overtime: longevity, holiday and shift differentials, stipends, retroactive pay, and leave/termination buy-outs. That last group mixes sick and vacation buy-backs with severance and health-insurance opt-out buy-backs — only the buy-backs are accrued leave. Hover the column heading, or click a row, for the breakdown.
Gross Pay
Base pay + overtime + other pay, all added together — the total actually paid for the year.
Group
The union or bargaining group the employee belongs to (for example PBA for police, CSEA for many town workers). Elected, appointed, management and part-time staff are not union-covered.
Titles marked “·Jan. schedule”
The Town’s payroll export only includes job titles and departments from 2022 onward. For 2018 to 2021, a title marked this way comes from the Town Board’s January salary resolution for that year, which names every employee on a salary schedule with a title and pay group; the department is filled only where the pay group is a department (Police, Highway, Water, Sewer, Street Lighting). Seasonal and part-time workers are not on those schedules, so many of them stay blank.
Titles marked “·carried”
Where someone was not on that year’s salary schedule but held one unchanging title in every later year on record, it is carried back and marked: an inference, not something the Town reported for that year. Anyone whose title ever changed is left blank instead, so a later promotion is never written into an earlier year.
