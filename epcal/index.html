EPCAL: the Town’s land at Calverton

Where the failed sale and the lawsuit stand, what the land earns and costs today, and what the record suggests weighing before the next deal.

Independent, unofficial project·Data last refreshed November 14, 2026·Recently refreshed·See data update details →

In plain English

The Town has about 1,643 acres at Calverton that it has tried for years to sell for business use. The last attempt, a $40 million sale to Calverton Aviation & Technology, fell apart in 2023 and ended in a lawsuit. This page sets out where that stands, what the land brings in while it waits, and the choices ahead. The money figures are the Town’s own; the lawsuit and the candidates’ plans are from news reports, labelled as such.
  • EPCAL: the Enterprise Park at Calverton, the former Navy and Grumman aircraft plant, with its two runways.
  • CDA: the Town’s Community Development Agency, which owns and manages the land. The Town Board sits as its board.
  • Notice of pendency: a claim recorded against land during a lawsuit, which keeps it from being sold or leased cleanly.

How the last sale ended

  1. 2018. The Town and its Community Development Agency agree to sell 1,643 acres to Calverton Aviation & Technology (CAT) for $40 million. (RiverheadLOCAL, February 28, 2026)
  2. 2020. The Suffolk County Planning Commission approves the eight-lot subdivision the sale needed, 9–1. (RiverheadLOCAL, August 11, 2020)
  3. September 21, 2022. A preliminary agreement with the Riverhead IDA sets out the steps toward the sale. (RiverheadLOCAL, February 28, 2026)
  4. October 23, 2023. The Riverhead IDA declines CAT’s and the CDA’s joint application for financial assistance. (RiverheadLOCAL, February 28, 2026)
  5. October 24, 2023. The Town Board cancels the CAT contract at an emergency meeting. CAT sues. (RiverheadLOCAL, February 28, 2026)
  6. February 2026. Justice David Reilly dismisses 16 of CAT’s 17 claims, including all its breach-of-contract and fraud claims and its demand that the Town hand over the land. One claim survives: that the Town interfered with CAT’s contract with the IDA, which seeks money, not the land. (RiverheadLOCAL, February 28, 2026)
  7. March 2026. CAT appeals (March 26) and the Town cross-appeals the surviving claim (March 30). The judge orders CAT’s notice of pendency, its claim recorded against the land, cancelled. Whether the Town can market the land while the appeal runs depends on whether CAT wins a stay. (RiverheadLOCAL, March 31, 2026)

These events come from news reports of the court’s rulings and the Town’s statements, not the court papers themselves. No report found says whether CAT won a stay from the appeals court.

What the land earns and costs now

The land pays no property tax while the Town owns it. Its income is rent for short uses: runway licenses for a racing school and safe-driving programs, and parking for the 2026 U.S. Open. The Community Development Agency’s fund shows:

Rent from the land$97,800$125,550$209,125$44,100$75,261
Lawyers and consultants$23,986$226,876$7,450$12,000$12,000

From the 2027 and 2026 Budget Supplements (fund Z14). Lawyers and consultants add three lines: legal, “CAT legal” and consultants; 2024’s $226,876 was the year the lawsuit was being fought. The fund held about $1,050,888 at the end of 2024. Rent of $209,125 in 2025 is small next to the $40.0 million the land was to sell for.

The plans on the table

  • Supervisor Jerry Halpin: A rail-served manufacturing hub and “an economic engine”, keeping the site’s recreational and environmental assets; talks with Empire State Development and railroad representatives; no ash or trash facilities. (RiverheadLOCAL, March 12, 2026; RiverheadLOCAL, October 22, 2026)
  • Council Member Ken Rothwell: Well-paying jobs and a larger tax base, with recreation in a broader mixed-use plan; no taxpayer money for a rail spur without a committed user; developers should bear development costs; opposes state involvement that would reduce local control, and data centers. (RiverheadLOCAL, March 12, 2026; RiverheadLOCAL, October 22, 2026)

One piece of history weighs on the rail idea. The Town took $4.8 million in federal recovery money and paid a contractor more than $3.5 million to rehabilitate the rail spur, which can serve only the two adjacent business properties (RiverheadLOCAL, March 12, 2026).

What the record suggests weighing

What is best for EPCAL is a choice for the Town Board and residents, and reasonable people weigh jobs, taxes, open space and recreation differently. This is this site’s reading of the record, not a finding or the Town’s plan. The record points to a few things worth settling before the next deal:

  1. Know the legal position first. Until the appeal or a stay is decided, a buyer may not be able to take clean title. Marketing can start, but a transfer is likely safer after that.
  2. Check buyers’ money before signing, not after. The CAT sale ran five years before the IDA’s financial review ended it. A public request for proposals that asks for proof of financing up front would surface that sooner.
  3. Consider selling or leasing in parts. The eight-lot subdivision the County approved in 2020 already divides the land. Smaller deals would not hang the whole site on one buyer.
  4. Make the user pay for the infrastructure it needs. Both candidates said so for rail, and the spur shows what happens when the Town builds first.
  5. Count the tax base, not only the price. Land in private hands pays property tax every year, which a one-time sale price does not show; any IDA tax break asked for would reduce that.
  6. Keep the interim uses. The runway licenses earn money and cost little while the long-term plan is settled.
