The tax cap — the formula, the override, and Riverhead’s record

The familiar 2% figure is only one input. This page separates New York’s actual levy-limit formula from Riverhead’s historical compliance record and from simple levy-growth comparisons.

In plain English

For calendar-year local governments, OSC set the 2027 allowable levy growth factor at 2%. Riverhead’s final 2027 levy limit depends on the rest of the statutory formula. The Supervisor’s letter in the 2027 Tentative puts it at 2.79%; the calculation behind that figure comes with the Town’s filing with the State Comptroller.
  • Levy, not tax rate: the law limits the covered property-tax levy — not the percentage change on an individual tax bill.
  • 2% is one factor: the allowable levy growth factor is the lesser of inflation or 2%; it is not, by itself, Riverhead’s final legal levy limit.
  • Override means authority: a 60% local-law vote lets the Board adopt above the calculated limit, but does not require it to do so.
The Town’s 2027 Tentative Budget is out. It proposes a town-wide tax levy of $62,882,202, up 2.79% from 2026, for the General Fund, Highway and Street Lighting. With the special districts, the levy is $67,159,863, up 2.78% from 2026 and $1,807,211 less than this site’s forecast of $68,967,074. The Supervisor’s letter says every taxing district is within the tax cap limit of 2.79%, and no district’s levy rises by more than that. No table in the budget prints the limit, and the letter gives no dollar figure or calculation for it. Those are in the filing the Town makes with the State Comptroller. See how it compares →

The levy limit is a formula, not “last year × 1.02.”

The tax cap limits the property-tax levy, not a home's tax rate. The allowable levy growth factor — the lesser of inflation or 2%, but never below 1.00 — is only one input. The full local-government calculation also uses the prior levy, tax-base growth, PILOT adjustments, available carryover, transfers of function and statutory exclusions before arriving at the final levy limit.

Step 1
Start with the prior-year levy

OSC begins with the prior fiscal year levy, with specified reserve/tort adjustments where applicable.

Step 2
Apply tax-base growth

The Tax Department’s tax-base-growth factor reflects quantity change such as new construction or newly taxable property.

Step 3
Apply PILOT and growth-factor inputs

Prior-year and coming-year PILOT receivables enter the formula, together with the allowable levy growth factor.

Step 4
Add available carryover

Unused levy-limit capacity from the prior year can carry forward, subject to the statutory limit.

Step 5
Apply transfers and exclusions

Transfers of function and qualifying retirement or tort exclusions can change the final adjusted levy limit.

What an override does — and does not do

A town can lawfully adopt a levy above its calculated levy limit if it first enacts an override local law by at least 60% of the governing body's voting power. Authorizing an override does not itself require the final adopted levy to exceed the limit; it can be adopted as procedural protection while the budget is still being reduced.

Required vote
At least 60% of the governing body’s voting power. On a five-member Town Board, that is three votes.
Timing
The override local law must be enacted before adoption of a budget whose levy exceeds the calculated limit.
Not a mandate to exceed
The Board can authorize an override and still adopt a final levy at or below the calculated limit.

The auditor reported a multi-year compliance failure.

Riverhead's independent auditor reported that the Town exceeded its tax levy limit for 2018–2022 without adopting the required override local law. The auditor traced the issue to a tax-cap calculation error beginning with the 2018 fiscal year that carried forward.

“The Town exceeded the 2% property tax cap for the year ended December 31, 2022 without adopting a local law in accordance with the New York State enacted Chapter 97, Laws of 2011 Real Property Tax Levy Cap and Mandate Relief Provisions.”— Town of Riverhead 2022 Audited Basic Financial Statements

Later treatment. For 2023 and subsequent adopted budgets shown here, the Town used an override local law before adopting a levy above the calculated limit.

What this means for residents

The 2% figure is not the final Riverhead levy limit

For 2027, OSC has set the allowable levy growth factor at 2% for calendar-year local governments. Riverhead's limit also depends on the rest of the statutory formula, and the Supervisor's letter in the 2027 Tentative puts it at 2.79%.

An override is authorization, not an outcome

A 60% override local law preserves the Board's legal ability to adopt an above-limit levy. Officials can still reduce the budget and ultimately adopt a levy at or below the calculated limit.

Historical compliance should come from the filed/audited record

A General Fund growth percentage is useful context but cannot by itself prove whether the Town complied with the tax cap, because the statutory test is town-wide and includes formula adjustments and exclusions.

2027: the Town's figure is 2.79%, but the calculation isn't public

The Supervisor's letter gives Riverhead's 2027 limit as 2.79% and says every taxing district is within it. The dollar limit and the inputs behind it come with the Town's filing with the State Comptroller. Until that is public, 2.79% is the Town's own statement, and a 2% line is only a reference.

2027: what is known now

OSC set the 2027 allowable levy growth factor at 2% for calendar-year local governments because its inflation factor was 3.13%. That does not determine Riverhead’s final filed limit on its own.

The Town’s 2027 Tentative proposes a town-wide levy of $62,882,202, up 2.79%, and $67,159,863 with the special districts, up 2.78%. The Supervisor’s letter says every taxing district is within the tax cap limit of 2.79%, and no district’s levy in the budget rises by more than that. The letter doesn’t give the limit in dollars or show how it was reached; that is in the Town’s filing with the State Comptroller.
