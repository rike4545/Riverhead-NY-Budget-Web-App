Brookhaven’s 2027 Tentative beside Riverhead’s

The Town of Brookhaven’s 2027 Tentative Operating Budget, dated September 30, 2026, set beside Riverhead’s: how each balances, how fast each grows, what each tells residents, and what Riverhead could borrow.

Independent project·Updated October 19, 2026·Current·Check data freshness →

In plain English

Brookhaven, Suffolk’s largest town, proposes to spend $375,768,766 in 2027, about three times Riverhead’s $120,958,878. The biggest difference is not size. Brookhaven’s Tentative balances every fund but one sewer district without dipping into savings, and its Supervisor puts the levy increase at 1.70%. Riverhead’s uses $5,895,000 of savings, most of it in the water and sewer districts, and its town-wide levy rises 2.79%, the limit its Supervisor states. Per resident, Riverhead’s town government spends about four times what Brookhaven’s does, mostly because it runs its own police, water and sewer services.
  • Two proposals: both are Tentative budgets, each Supervisor’s proposal. Each Town Board can change its own before it adopts a budget by November 20.
  • Savings: “appropriated fund balance”: money left from earlier years that a budget counts as income. The Funds Explorer shows how Riverhead’s funds use it.
  • Read from a scan: Brookhaven’s copy has no text layer, so its figures were read from the page images and checked by adding up its own tables.

At a glance

Southold, Riverhead’s other neighbour, has its 2027 Tentative set beside Riverhead’s too, with Brookhaven alongside: Southold beside Riverhead.

Each total is the document’s own. Riverhead’s counts the Debt Service, Workers’ Compensation and Risk Retention funds, which the other funds pay into, so some dollars appear twice; Brookhaven’s table does not say how much of its total passes between funds.

Brookhaven’s rating and stress score are its Supervisor’s statements: “In 2026, both Moody’s Ratings and S&P Global Ratings reaffirmed the Town at AAA/Aaa—the highest credit ratings available—with stable outlooks.” and “The New York State Comptroller has also reported a 0.0 Fiscal Stress Score for Brookhaven, the best possible score and another independent measure of the Town’s strong financial condition.” Riverhead’s rating is as RiverheadLOCAL reported it in February 2024 (Credit Rating).

Per resident

Per resident, Riverhead’s town government spends about four times what Brookhaven’s does. The two governments do not do the same things:

  • Police. Riverhead pays for a police department: $23,244,090 in the 2027 Tentative, and $12,840,800 more in sworn officers’ pensions, health insurance and Social Security, $36,084,890 in all, or $992 a resident. Brookhaven’s police are the Suffolk County Police Department, outside its town budget.
  • Water and sewer. Riverhead runs a water district and three sewer funds, $23,740,533 or $652 a resident. Brookhaven’s town budget has two small sewer districts and one water district.

Leave those out, and the three funds the others pay into, and Riverhead’s spending comes to $57,039,427: about $1,567 a resident, still about twice Brookhaven’s $757. This page does not say why the rest differs. Residents of Brookhaven’s villages also get some services from their villages, which neither town budget counts, and a per-resident figure is not a tax bill: businesses pay part of each levy.

Sources: U.S. Census Bureau, Annual Estimates of the Resident Population for Minor Civil Divisions in New York (SUB-EST2025); Riverhead’s police lines from the 2027 Budget Supplement (PDF), pp. 11–13 (the department) and pp. 26–27 (sworn officers’ benefits).

What Riverhead residents can take from it

Brookhaven balances without savings. Riverhead does not.

Brookhaven’s fund-balance table (Brookhaven PDF pp. 17–18) shows savings used in only one of its 37 funds and districts: Brookhaven Sewer District #2, $150,000 of its $1,333,451 budget. Its Supervisor writes: “The 2027 Tentative Operating Budget is structurally balanced across all six major tax districts without using fund balance to support operations.”

Riverhead’s Tentative uses $5,895,000 of savings in five funds. Most of it, $4,945,000, is in the water and sewer districts, which Brookhaven does not run on anything like that scale. The General Fund uses $950,000. How each Riverhead fund uses its savings.

Riverhead’s main funds grow about twice as fast

Brookhaven’s general, highway and lighting funds go from $276,781,919 in its 2026 Adopted Budget to $282,826,232, up 2.2%. Its town-wide General Fund barely moves: $154,611,894 to $154,642,944. Riverhead’s town-wide funds go from $77,958,942 to $81,253,214, up 4.2%, and its General Fund up 4.2%. Part of the gap is what each General Fund pays for: Riverhead’s pays for a town police department. Brookhaven has none of its own; the Suffolk County Police Department serves it, and that cost is not in Brookhaven’s town budget.

Riverhead’s levy rises to its limit. Brookhaven’s Supervisor puts the town’s rise lower.

Riverhead’s Supervisor writes: “There is joy in presenting this budget as all taxing districts are budgeted within the tax cap limit of 2.79%.” The town-wide levy rises from $61,178,292 to $62,882,202, up 2.79%. Brookhaven’s Supervisor writes: “It remains well below the New York State Property Tax Levy Cap, with a 1.70% adjustment after accounting for the new LIPA PILOT agreement reflected in 2026 property taxes.” Suffolk’s supervisors asked Albany in October to change how the cap works; the supervisors’ tax-cap letter, checked.

Both hold large General Fund cushions

Brookhaven expects its town-wide General Fund to end 2026 with $61,558,409 unappropriated, 39.8% of its 2027 spending, and plans to use none of it. Riverhead’s General Fund ended 2025 with $28,829,513 unassigned, 41.7% of its 2026 budget, by its audit. The two measures are close but not the same: Brookhaven’s is its budget’s own estimate, Riverhead’s is audited, a year earlier. A cushion that size does not have to be spent to balance a budget. Riverhead’s audits show its General Fund ending 2024 and 2025 with more savings than it began, even with draws planned in both budgets. Reserves.

Brookhaven’s budget book tells residents more

Each of Brookhaven’s 16 departments and offices opens with a mission statement, and most list the past year’s accomplishments and their goals for 2027. Schedules of positions list each division’s job titles with their counts in the 2026 Adopted and 2027 Tentative budgets, and the change. Its fund-balance table shows every fund’s and district’s balance before and after 2027. And its message tells residents the town’s share of their tax bill: “In 2026, the Town’s share represented approximately 7.51% of the total local property tax bill for properties outside incorporated villages and approximately 4.0% within village areas, based upon the 2025–2026 Property Tax Warrant certified by the Suffolk County Legislature.”

Riverhead’s Tentative has a two-page letter, fund descriptions, a Summary, a Fund Balances page, elected officials’ salaries, its line items and an exemption report. Its text has no mission statements or position lists, and its Fund Balances page stops at an estimate for the end of 2026.

Garbage: a price per home, or a district total

Brookhaven’s message: “These expenses are identified through a dollar-for-dollar user fee so residents can see what these services cost and track those costs from year to year.” and “For a typical single-family home, the 2027 Garbage and Refuse User Fee is limited to a 1.5% adjustment, bringing the total to $395.52.” Riverhead pays for garbage collection through its Refuse and Garbage District, raised entirely by property taxes: $5,302,990 proposed for 2027, up $48,450 (0.9%). Its budget does not say what that comes to for a typical home.

Ambulance taxes fall in Brookhaven and rise in Riverhead

Brookhaven’s message: “Reduces property taxes across the Town’s nine Ambulance Districts by 2.05%.” Riverhead’s Ambulance District levy goes from $1,704,224 to $1,751,693, up 2.79%.

How Riverhead’s budget could be improved

Ideas a resident could put to the Town Board, drawn from what Brookhaven’s budget does. They are suggestions, not findings that Riverhead has done anything wrong. Several match what the Government Finance Officers Association asks of a budget document (Standards).

  1. Balance the General Fund without savings. Its planned draw has fallen from $2,000,000 for 2024 to $950,000 proposed for 2027. Brookhaven’s budget takes the last step. Savings are one-time money, best kept for one-time costs.
  2. Set a plan for the sewer funds. Savings would pay 42.0% of the Calverton Sewer District’s 2027 spending and 26.5% of the Riverhead Sewer District’s. The sewer funds’ audited unrestricted net position was a deficit of $4,493,308 at the end of 2025. A schedule of rates and draws over several years would show residents how the gap closes.
  3. Print a fund-balance table that looks ahead. Brookhaven’s shows each fund’s balance, the savings the budget uses and the balance expected after it. Riverhead’s stops at the end of 2026, subtracts a column labelled “2025 Application of Fund Balance”, and prints the water and sewer funds’ net position, mostly pipes and plants, as their fund balance.
  4. Open each department with its purpose and results. A mission, last year’s accomplishments and next year’s goals, as Brookhaven’s departments have.
  5. List positions by department. This year’s and next year’s count for each title, so a new or cut position shows in the budget itself.
  6. Show what garbage collection costs a home. Brookhaven prints a price for a typical single-family home. Riverhead prints only the district’s total.
  7. Tell residents the Town’s share of their tax bill. Brookhaven’s message gives its share, about 7.51% outside villages.
  8. Consider local limits on the tax rate and on debt. Brookhaven’s message lists among its highlights that it “maintains compliance with the Town’s local tax-rate and debt-management caps”.
  9. Publish the Supervisor’s letter as text. Riverhead’s 2025, 2026 and 2027 letters are scanned images: a screen reader cannot read them and a search cannot find them.

Where the two towns differ

  • Size. Brookhaven spends about three times what Riverhead does and has about 14 times its residents (496,410 against 36,394). It also has incorporated villages, whose residents pay some of its taxes and not others.
  • Police. Riverhead’s General Fund pays for a town police department. Brookhaven has no town police department: the Suffolk County Police Department serves it, outside the town budget. The Town’s own Department of Public Safety covers public safety administration, code enforcement, harbors and waterways, the animal shelter, the fire marshals and the Brookhaven Calabro Airport (table of contents, Brookhaven PDF p. 8).
  • Water and sewer. Riverhead runs a water district and three sewer funds, $23,740,533 of its 2027 spending. Brookhaven’s budget has three sewer and water districts, $2,356,074 together. Most of Riverhead’s savings use has no counterpart in Brookhaven’s budget.
  • Measures. Brookhaven’s balances are its budget’s estimates of “unappropriated, unreserved” fund balance; Riverhead’s General Fund figure is audited unassigned balance. Page 18 of Brookhaven’s table still carries the previous year’s 2025 and 2026 headings; its rows and totals continue page 17’s.
  • Totals. Brookhaven’s levy summary (Brookhaven PDF p. 16) totals $381,930,401. Its line for other special districts and the airport includes the $6,161,635 street-lighting district a second time, so this page uses the fund-balance table’s $375,768,766.
  • Stage. Both are proposals. Either Town Board may change its budget before it adopts one.

How this page was made

Brookhaven’s Tentative came to this site as a 350-page scan with no text. Each page was read with OCR, upright and turned a quarter-turn each way for the sideways tables, and every figure and quotation on this page was then read from the page image. Each table’s rows add to its printed totals (the balance columns to within a dollar or two, as the scan rounds to the dollar), and the levy summary agrees with the fund-balance table fund by fund. A check runs on every build of this site and fails if any of that stops being true. The OCR text is kept in the site’s repository beside the scan.

Riverhead’s figures come from the records this site already checks: the Tentative’s Summary and pages, the 2027 Budget Supplement and the audited statements.
