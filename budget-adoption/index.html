How Riverhead adopts its budget

Every budget from 2005 to 2026: what the Town Board changed in each Supervisor’s Tentative, whether it changed it before or after the public hearing, and whether it voted to adopt the result. Read from the Town’s own budget books and minutes.

Independent project·Updated September 25, 2026·● Current·Check data freshness →
💡In plain English
The law describes a sequence: the Board reviews the Supervisor’s Tentative, turns it into its own Preliminary, holds a hearing on that, and adopts a budget by vote. Riverhead followed it through the 2008 budget. Since then the Board has made every change after the public hearing, and for a decade it often did not vote to adopt at all.
  • ›The Supervisor proposes: the Tentative, by Sept 30. It appropriates nothing.
  • ›The Board decides: it can change the Tentative into a Preliminary before the hearing, change it again after, and must adopt by Nov 20.
  • ›If the Board never votes: the Preliminary becomes the budget on Nov 20 anyway (Town Law §109(3)). That has happened 6 times since 2009.

The sequence the law sets

  1. Sept 30 — The Supervisor, as budget officer, files the Tentative with the Town Clerk (Town Law §106(2))
  2. Oct 5 — The Board receives it and may change it; the Tentative plus the Board’s changes becomes the Preliminary (Town Law §106(3)–(4))
  3. By the Thursday after Election Day — Public hearing on the Preliminary, noticed with the elected officials’ proposed salaries (Town Law §108)
  4. After the hearing — The Board may change the Preliminary further (Town Law §109(1))
  5. Nov 20 — The Board adopts it by resolution. If it does not, the Preliminary becomes the budget anyway (Town Law §109(2)–(3))
  6. Before adopting a levy above the cap — A local law passed by 60% of the Board (from the 2012 budget on) (General Municipal Law §3-c)

2005–2008

The sequence as the law describes it

Cardinale filed lean Tentatives and the Board rebuilt them into a Preliminary before the hearing, then adopted by vote. Twice the result went through over the Supervisor’s own no.

4 budgets · 4 adopted by vote · 0 without one · 4 changed by the Board, net +$3,173,300
2009–2019

Changes after the hearing, and budgets nobody adopted

Every change moved to after the public hearing, and in six of these eleven years no adoption resolution passed. Board members described adoption as automatic, and under Town Law §109(3) it is.

11 budgets · 5 adopted by vote · 6 without one · 5 changed by the Board, net +$2,596,014
2020–2026

Votes every year, changes still late

Every budget has been adopted by vote, with an override law each year from 2023. The Board’s changes, in 2020, 2023 and 2025, all came after the hearing, and since the 2024 budget the hearing notice has left out the elected officials’ salaries.

7 budgets · 7 adopted by vote · 0 without one · 3 changed by the Board, net +$727,975

Why the Board stopped following the sequence — possible reasons

These are inferences from the record, not findings. Each lists the years it rests on; none is stated as a motive by anyone in the minutes.

  1. Adoption was treated as automatic. Town Law §109(3) makes an unadopted Preliminary the budget on Nov. 20, and with no changes the Preliminary is the Supervisor’s Tentative. Members said as much on the record, so a vote could be skipped, or cast against, at no cost.
    Rests on: 2011, 2015, 2019
  2. No one had to own a tax increase. The years without an adoption vote cluster in 2009–2015, when reserves were being spent down. The Nov. 18, 2014 minutes record a resident saying the $9 million reserve was now at zero, and Walter saying there had not been a structurally sound budget since 1999.
    Rests on: 2009, 2012, 2013, 2015
  3. Lean Tentatives invited additions; tight ones did not. Cardinale’s bare-bones Tentatives were rebuilt by the Board every year. Walter’s were described by his own Board as “bony” and as leaving nothing to give, and an all-Republican Board rarely amended them.
    Rests on: 2005, 2007, 2008, 2014, 2016
  4. Divided control produces amendments. The largest changes came when the Board majority overrode its Supervisor — Cardinale in 2007 and 2008, Walter in 2013, Aguiar in 2023 — or when the Supervisor was leaving, as in 2010 and 2020.
    Rests on: 2007, 2008, 2010, 2013, 2020, 2023
  5. The calendar pushes changes past the hearing. The Tentative arrives around Oct. 1 and the hearing notice goes out by mid-October. Since the 2009 budget, the Board has noticed the Tentative as filed and made any changes in November, after the public has spoken.
    Rests on: 2009, 2020, 2023, 2025
  6. The missed override was a calculation error. The auditor traced the 2018–2022 levies above the limit to a tax-cap calculation error that began in 2018, not to a decision to skip the law; members said in 2019 they were staying under the cap.
    Rests on: 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022

What it means for 2027

Jerry Halpin prepared the 2027 Tentative for a Board of four Republicans (Rothwell, Kern, Merrifield, Waski). The record has two stretches when the Board majority and the Supervisor were not aligned. From 2007 to 2009 the Board rewrote Cardinale’s Tentatives upward, adopted them over his no, and once let the budget take effect without a vote. In 2019 and 2020 a Board with a Republican majority left Jens-Smith’s first budget unadopted and amended her second after she lost. A 4–1 majority can do any of those things, and it can supply or withhold the three votes a tax-cap override needs.

  • Sept 24 — Town Clerk presents the Tentative at a special meeting (Town notice, Sept 2026)
  • Sept 30 — Deadline to file the Tentative (Town Law §106(2))
  • Oct 5 — Presented to the Board, which may change it into the Preliminary (Town Law §106(3)–(4))
  • Nov 3 — General election: Halpin against Councilman Rothwell
  • Nov 5 — Last day for the public hearing on the Preliminary (Town Law §108)
  • Nov 20 — Adopt by resolution — or the Preliminary becomes the budget (Town Law §109)

Precedents: 2007, 2008, 2009, 2019, 2020. The Tentative itself, against this site’s projection: 2027 Tentative Budget.

How this was built, and what it cannot tell you

Each adopted budget book prints its stages side by side — Department Requested, Tentative, Preliminary, Adopted — and the changes here are read from those columns, line by line, then checked against the same book’s fund recap or Summary page. The votes, dates and quotes are read from the Town Clerk’s minutes on CivicClerk; the files that are scanned images were read page by page and are marked. A date the minutes did not show is left blank.

  • The 2005 book’s line detail and fund recap disagree on several debt-service funds, so its totals do not reconcile; both show the same Tentative-to-Preliminary change.
  • No adopted book is posted for 2018. Its row compares the Tentative with what the 2019 book reports as the 2018 budget for the three town-wide funds.
  • The 2006 Preliminary column prints about $2 million of bond payments as zero and its Adopted column restores them; those lines are counted apart and are not a Board decision.
  • Whether the 2012–2015 levies were within the tax cap is not in this site’s data. The minutes for those falls record no override law.
  • Party labels are shown only where a source gives them. None is shown for council members before 2010.
  • A column’s label is kept as printed. The 2020 “Proposed Preliminary” column includes an amendment the Board rejected, so the record of votes comes from the minutes, not the column.
