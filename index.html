The handful of things that actually moved — what changed, why it matters to you, and what we're still double-checking.

watch

Town-wide tax levy growth exceeded appropriation growth

7.74% levy growth

The 2026 town-wide tax levy increased by $4,394,713, while town-wide appropriations increased by 6.02%.

Why it matters: When levy growth is faster than spending growth, residents may feel more pressure even if the budget increase looks moderate. It can signal changing revenue mix, fund balance choices, or taxable base dynamics.

information

General Fund is the largest operating driver

$69,113,159

The General Fund carries the largest 2026 appropriation total among the extracted operating funds.

Why it matters: Changes in the General Fund usually have the widest taxpayer and service impact because it supports core town operations.

watch

Appropriated fund balance is being used in several funds

$11,245,000

The extracted 2026 budget rows include appropriated fund balance as part of the financing plan.

Why it matters: Using fund balance can reduce near-term tax pressure, but recurring reliance may weaken reserves if not matched by recurring revenues or one-time needs.

positive

The General Fund ran a real surplus in 2025

$5,003,327 surplus

Actual 2025 results show the General Fund took in $71,010,826 and spent $66,007,499, growing fund balance to $33,407,251.

Why it matters: A surplus grows the Town’s savings and reduces pressure to raise taxes — but it can also mean revenues were under-forecast. These are management-reported AFR figures, not yet an audited statement.

The retirement buyout — and what to watch

Update — the buyout is now final. The Town has executed 2026 Voluntary Retirement Incentive agreements with all three unions (CSEA $12,500; PBA & SOA $1,000/yr of service + sick-day payout). See the final terms →

UNVALIDATED PROJECTED SAVINGS: The fiscal impact of the retirement incentive program cannot be validated without staffing assumptions, payroll tables, leave payout estimates, overtime trends, pension timing analysis, and replacement hiring plans.

High

Backfill risk

If critical employees retire and the Town refills those positions, projected savings may shrink substantially.

Fiscal impact: Actual savings may only equal the wage differential between the retiring employee and the replacement employee rather than the full salary removed from payroll.

Moderate

Overtime substitution risk

Reduced staffing levels may increase overtime requirements in highway, sewer, water, emergency response, inspections, and public works operations.

Fiscal impact: Overtime growth can offset projected payroll savings.

High

Leave payout exposure

Vacation, sick leave, and comp-time payouts can create large one-time expenditures during retirement waves.

Fiscal impact: The Town may need reserve usage or temporary financing to absorb payout spikes.

Moderate

Institutional knowledge loss

Senior personnel often hold operational, technical, and regulatory knowledge that is difficult to replace quickly.

Fiscal impact: Consulting costs, delays, or inefficiencies can reduce net savings.

Unvalidated

Pension timing mismatch

Pension contribution savings may not occur in the same fiscal period as the retirements themselves.

Fiscal impact: Near-term budget relief may be overstated.

Moderate

Structural versus temporary savings

Temporary vacancies do not always become permanent staffing reductions.

Fiscal impact: Projected long-term savings may not persist.

About Riverhead Budget Live

Riverhead Budget Live is an independent, non-partisan, non-profit project. Our mission is to make Riverhead a better place to live and work by promoting public-policy reforms grounded in the fiscally responsible ideals of effective and accountable government.

How to use this site

Use this site to share, analyze, and compare data from governmental entities throughout Riverhead. The information on this website comes from official government sources, but the author cannot guarantee data accuracy or completeness.

The fine print

This website is an independent public-information and fiscal-analysis project. It is not an official Town of Riverhead website and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by, or operated by the Town of Riverhead or any Town department, political subdivision, political party or political organization or candidate.

The figures are pulled automatically from public source documents, so the author cannot guarantee they are accurate or complete. A number can be misread, mislabeled, or lag behind the latest filing — a scanning glitch, a stray column, or a report that hadn't caught up yet.

Nothing here is legal, financial, tax, or investment advice. The explanations, projections, classifications, and “realistic” analyses are the author's own interpretation of public records — not the Town's position and not a professional opinion.

So before you rely on any figure, check it against the original official document; the source is linked on every page. And if you spot something wrong, please flag it — corrections are genuinely welcome and make this better for everyone.
