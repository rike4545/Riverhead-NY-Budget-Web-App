How each of Riverhead's 22 election districts voted, from the 2013 Supervisor race to 2026, with any two candidates compared district by district.

Independent, unofficial project·Data last refreshed November 14, 2026·Recently refreshed·See data update details →

In plain English

On November 3, 2026, Jerry Halpin led in 17 of the 22 districts and Ken Rothwell in five. Halpin took 52.4% of the Supervisor vote while Governor Hochul took 46.3% of the vote for Governor on the same ballots: Halpin ran ahead of her in 21 of the 22 districts.
  • Election districts: the County Board of Elections divides the Town into 22 districts. Each votes at an assigned polling place, and the Board counts each one separately.
  • Which counts: 2026 and 2025 are the counts on the Board's results site, which it labels unofficial; for 2025 that is the election-night count, and the certified totals are below. 2024 and the 2013, 2015 and 2017 Supervisor races are final counts, district by district.
  • Votes, not turnout: the results site does not give registered voters by district, so the map shows votes cast, not the share of voters who turned out.

What the districts show

  • 2026 Supervisor. Halpin 6,779, Rothwell 6,152. Rothwell's widest lead was in district 20 (Ridge), 775 to 457. Halpin's was in district 6 (Riverhead), 130 to 55.
  • Split tickets. Bruce A Blakeman and Todd Hood carried Riverhead for Governor, 7,018 to 6,074, yet Halpin won the Town for Supervisor. Choose “Compare” and “Halpin against Hochul, 2026” on the map to see where his vote ran ahead of hers.
  • 2024 President. Donald J Trump and JD Vance carried Riverhead 10,122 to 7,505, leading in 18 districts to four for Kamala D Harris and Tim Walz. Two years later Halpin ran ahead of Harris's share in 22 of the 22 districts: choose “Compare” and “Halpin in 2026 against Harris in 2024”.
  • Two Democratic wins, nine years apart. Laura Jens-Smith won in 2017 with 52.8% of the vote; Halpin won in 2026 with 52.4%. Halpin's share was higher in ten of the 22 districts. In Compare, choose any two candidates and years, back to 2013.
  • 2025 to 2026. In the 2025 election-night count Halpin led Tim Hubbard 3,891 to 3,870, ahead in eleven districts to Hubbard's ten. His share rose in 17 of the 22 districts in 2026. About 5,174 more votes were cast for Supervisor in 2026, a year with a race for Governor on the ballot.

Turnout, year by year

The share of Riverhead's registered voters who voted in each November election the County has a final Town page for. In the odd years, when only local offices are on the ballot, between 32.3% and 41.3% voted; with a President on the ballot, 63.8% and 71.6%. 2025's 32.3%, 7,879 of 24,429, was the lowest since 2013.

Each year's figures
  • 2013: 7,493 votes for Supervisor, of 21,001 registered (35.7%).
  • 2015: 7,513 votes for Supervisor, of 21,012 registered (35.8%).
  • 2016: 13,992 votes for Town Justice, of 21,935 registered (63.8%).
  • 2017: 9,011 votes for Supervisor, of 21,833 registered (41.3%).
  • 2019: 8,587 votes for Supervisor, of 21,798 registered (39.4%).
  • 2020: 16,686 votes for Town Justice, of 23,296 registered (71.6%).
  • 2021: 9,142 votes for Supervisor, of 23,133 registered (39.5%).
  • 2023: 8,753 votes for Supervisor, of 23,509 registered (37.2%).
  • 2024: 16,686 votes for Town Justice, of 24,462 registered (68.2%).
  • 2025: 7,879 votes for Supervisor, of 24,429 registered (32.3%).

2014, 2018 and 2022 had no Town race, so the County has no Riverhead page for them. In the presidential years the measure is the Town Justice race; in 2024 the race for President drew more, 17,754 votes (72.6%). For 2026, 13,108 votes were counted for Governor in Riverhead, but the County has not yet published the Town's registered voters or its final 2026 page; this chart adds 2026 when it does. The County's results site does not give registered voters by district, so there is no turnout map; the map's “Votes cast” view shows where the votes came from. For turnout by county across the State, see New York Focus's 2026 turnout map.

The certified 2025 totals

The map's 2025 figures are the results site's count as of November 6, 2025, which it still labels unofficial. The Board's certified totals, after absentee ballots and a full manual recount, were higher:

  • Supervisor: Jerome (Jerry) Halpin 3,958, Timothy C. Hubbard 3,921, a margin of 37 votes.
  • Town Council (two seats): Bob Kern 3,958, Kenneth Rothwell 3,882, Mark A. Woolley 3,824, Kevin M. Shea 3,515.

The certified count is not published by district, so the map cannot show it. The full record is on Board elections.

Sources and method

  • Results: Suffolk County Board of Elections, public election results, each contest's “results by election district”: the 2026 General Election as of November 4, 2026, and the 2025 General Election as of November 6, 2025. A candidate on more than one party line is counted once.
  • 2024 by district: Benjamin J. Rosenblatt and @cinyc9, New York 2024 general election results by election district, compiled from the State and County boards of elections. Its Suffolk totals for President and the 1st Congressional District equal the County Board's final 2024 results exactly, and its 2024 districts match today's to 99.6% or better.
  • 2013, 2015 and 2017 Supervisor races by district: OpenElections, transcribed from the County's precinct returns. Each candidate's Town total equals the County's final results page exactly. No County map of those years' districts is published, so the lines are checked against the Census Bureau's 2010 and 2020 voting-district maps, which match today's to 96.7% or better (the Census draws districts 4 and 14 as one).
  • Boundaries: Suffolk County GIS, Voting Precincts (Suffolk County Board of Elections), last updated March 27, 2026. The results site draws 2025 and 2026 on the same district map, and the 2024 districts match it, so every year's figures fit these lines. The place name beside each district is the County's.
  • Refreshed by etl/build_election_map.py; data last changed November 7, 2026. The map is drawn from the County's boundaries; no outside map service is used.
  • The idea follows the NYC Election Atlas from the CUNY Graduate Center's Center for Urban Research, which maps New York City's results by election district.
