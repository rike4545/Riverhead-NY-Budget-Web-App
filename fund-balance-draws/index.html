Where the surplus went

Every 2026 resolution that spent fund balance, what it funded, and how much of it the published record actually shows.

Independent, unofficial project·Data last refreshed November 14, 2026·Recently refreshed·See data update details →

In plain English

The Town uses surplus through two separate channels, and only the first appears in the budget a resident can read. The 2026 adopted budget planned to use $1,250,000 of General Fund balance. Resolutions adopted during the year have charged $3,195,711 more to that account. Other funds drew $9,658,903 from their own balances by resolution, listed separately below because that money does not pay for General Fund services. New to these terms? See appropriated fund balance in the glossary, or the five tiers of fund balance.
  • Fund balance: accumulated surplus: money left over from prior years. It is a balance, not an income stream, so spending it is a one-time act that cannot be repeated without rebuilding it first.
  • Appropriated, not spent: these votes create the authority to spend. Whether the money was then obligated or paid out is a separate question, and not one this site can answer.
  • Object code 9999: the Town numbers its Appropriated Fund Balance accounts like revenue lines (A01-9999 in the General Fund), but the money is not new revenue. The State Comptroller’s accounting manual keeps it as a budget account of its own, 599 Appropriated Fund Balance. A resolution that draws on it adds the same amount to the fund balance the budget uses and to what the budget may spend: a debit to 599 and a credit to 960 Appropriations.

What this page cannot tell you

  • These are appropriations, not expenditures. Adopting the resolution creates the authority to spend the money; whether it was then obligated or paid is a separate question this site does not track. An encumbrance in the accounting sense, a purchase order reserving part of an appropriation, happens later and is not published in a form this site reads.
  • Only 233 of the 998 resolutions with a parsed fiscal impact statement (23%) itemise their accounts. The rest name a funding source without the account detail that identifies a fund balance draw, so a draw among them cannot be counted here. Every total on this page is a floor.
  • 7 of the 10 General Fund draws name no GASB 54 tier on the account itself. The object code alone does not distinguish Unassigned from Assigned or Committed, so an unnamed draw is treated as unassigned. That is by far the usual case, but it is an assumption rather than a fact from the record.
  • A draw split across two tiers is held out whole. The Meals on Wheels truck (2026-645) charges A01-9999 twice: $48,128.53 of Assigned Fund Balance for Senior Day Care and $31,871.47 of Appropriated Fund Balance. The rule that keeps Assigned money out of the unassigned cushion applies to the resolution rather than to each account, so all $80,000 sits in the reported-not-netted section. The $31,871.47 genuinely is unassigned, so the cushion is described as very slightly larger than the record supports. Splitting the draw by account would be more exact; this site does not do that yet.
  • 3 General Fund draws are reported and deliberately not netted against the unassigned cushion, because the account itself names another GASB 54 tier: resolution 2026-361 ($113,613, Assigned fund balance), resolution 2026-645 ($80,000, Assigned fund balance), and resolution 2026-930 ($480, Assigned fund balance). They are money spent by Board votes, but they do not come out of the unassigned tier this site measures the cushion on.
  • Draws on other funds' balances are listed apart and never added to the General Fund figures. 8 other funds drew $9,658,903 from their own balances by resolution, and 2 votes name a fund balance account without an amount. Each fund keeps its balance for its own purposes, so that money does not pay for General Fund services and does not move the cushion either way.
  • Two kinds of entry are listed apart and never totalled together. A documented draw charged A01-9999 on the Town's own fiscal impact statement, so the figure is the Town's. A curated commitment was read from the record because the account codes do not cover it, and the largest of them was authorized by vote rather than booked against the fund balance account. That is firmer than a ceiling, but the record notes it could rise if the property owner litigates. Adding the two would assert of the curated entries something the record does not say.
  • A vote that amends next year's budget, such as the amendments to the 2027 Preliminary Budget, changes how much fund balance that budget plans to use. It does not draw on this year's balance, so it is left out of the 2026 figures and shows in the 2027 line instead.
  • A resolution that amends the funding source of spending already budgeted moves money between sources rather than appropriating new surplus. Adding it to the budgeted figure would overstate the year. The record does not mark which resolutions do this, so the combined total is presented as a sum of two channels rather than as a settled figure.
