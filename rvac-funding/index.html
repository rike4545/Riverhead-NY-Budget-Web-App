Riverhead Volunteer Ambulance Corps: where the funding comes from

A line-by-line look at the Town’s Ambulance District budget, the contract with RVAC, and the 2026 adjustments that corrected the original appropriation.

Independent project·Updated October 29, 2026·Current·Check data freshness →

In plain English

The key distinction: RVAC’s contract is one line inside a much larger Ambulance District budget. The original adopted 2026 budget set that line at $1,274,924. Resolution 2026-156 moved $100,000 out of the RVAC line to other Ambulance District lines; Resolution 2026-470 later added $99,322 back. The calculated line after both changes is $1,274,246—$678 below the original adopted amount. The May addition used $80,000 of fund balance and a $19,322 reduction to the vehicle line. These are budget authorizations, not a claim that this exact amount has already been paid.
  • RVAC: Riverhead Volunteer Ambulance Corps, the service provider paid under contract.
  • Ambulance District: A separate Town fund (SM1), not the Town General Fund. Its revenues and reserves are not interchangeable with General Fund money.
  • Budget vs. cash: An appropriation is permission to spend, and a revenue estimate is a plan. Neither proves how much was ultimately collected or paid.
  • Fund balance: Accumulated surplus from earlier years. Using it can fund a one-time shortfall, but it is not recurring revenue.

3. The two 2026 corrections to RVAC’s contract line

FEBRUARY 18, 2026 · RESOLUTION 2026-156

Move $100,000 out of RVAC to other ambulance lines

The resolution corrected several lines that the original adopted budget had left at different amounts. The Town’s 2027 Budget Supplement’s 2026 column shows vehicles at $105,000, other professional services at $30,000, equipment rent at $50,000, and RVAC at $1,174,924—a pattern consistent with moving $100,000 out of RVAC and into those three lines. The original 2026 adopted budget instead shows vehicles at $85,000, the other two lines at $0, and RVAC at $1,274,924. The supplement appears to incorporate this February adjustment even though it labels the column “2026 Adopted Budget”; that difference is important to disclose.

6. New headquarters: donations, a Town bond, and lease payments

The new ambulance headquarters is a separate capital project—not part of RVAC’s annual service-contract line above. The September 1, 2026 public-hearing notice put the project’s estimated maximum cost at $14.1 million. The stated financing plan was $6 million provided by RVAC from private fundraising and other available funds, with the balance—up to $8.1 million—financed through Town-issued bonds. At the hearing, the Supervisor said fundraising was then just under $6.5 million. Those are reported project-financing figures, not proof of the final construction cost, final donations received, or bonds ultimately issued.

  • Ownership: the new building is to be owned by the Ambulance District and leased to RVAC.
  • Repayment plan: the public notice and hearing record say the lease agreement requires payments from RVAC to the district sufficient to cover bond debt service. The stated plan relies on ambulance-billing revenue generated by services RVAC provides.
  • Risk distinction: “planned to be paid by RVAC” is not the same as proof that the Town has no financial exposure. The bond documents, executed lease, actual billing collections, debt-service schedule and any guarantee or backstop determine who bears the risk if collections fall short.
  • Timeline: while the existing headquarters is replaced, RVAC temporarily operates from the Town-owned Armory. Resolution 2026-842 appropriated $45,000 from Ambulance District fund balance for Armory building repairs. That is a separate operating-fund adjustment, not part of the $8.1 million bond ceiling.
  • Other Town facilities: 2026 resolutions also authorized temporary use of Town space for EMT instruction, medical billing / records storage and ambulance vehicle storage. The resolutions identify the uses, but do not provide a dollar value for that space or establish whether rent was charged.

How ambulance billing is supposed to repay the building bond

  1. RVAC responds and transports patients. Depending on the service and coverage, a bill may be submitted to Medicare, Medicaid, a private insurer, or the patient.
  2. Billing produces collections. The gross amount billed is not the same as cash collected: insurer allowed amounts, denials, write-offs, patient responsibility and timing all affect receipts.
  3. The lease is the repayment bridge. The Town’s bond hearing notice says the lease must require payments from RVAC to the Ambulance District sufficient to cover the bond’s debt service. Public statements describe ambulance-billing receipts as the expected source for those payments.
  4. The District pays the bond debt service. Principal and interest are paid according to the bond schedule. The key test is whether the lease payments actually received, supported by available billing cash, cover each scheduled payment when due.

Important accounting distinction: ambulance billing is the expected economic source of repayment; it is not proof that each dollar collected is automatically deposited into a legally restricted bond account. The public hearing notice establishes the lease-payment requirement, but the detailed executed lease, revenue pledge or assignment, debt-service schedule, collection reports and annual reconciliations are needed to verify the exact cash path.

What should be published each year

  • Gross ambulance charges, collections received, contractual adjustments, write-offs and accounts receivable.
  • Net billing cash available after the cost of billing and other required operating expenses.
  • Lease payments due and received from RVAC, with dates and amounts.
  • Bond principal, interest and fees paid; remaining principal; and any reserve or fund-balance support used.
  • Any shortfall between lease receipts and debt service, including which entity is legally responsible and what funding source covers it.

Sources: Town’s September 1 public-hearing notice (reproduced by RiverheadLOCAL); September 1 Town Board hearing and bond resolution; RiverheadLOCAL’s project and lease reporting; RVAC’s headquarters fundraising page; Town Board Resolution 2026-842 (September 15).

7. What this record does—and does not—prove

  • The original adopted RVAC line was $1,274,924. After the February reduction and May addition, the calculated line is $1,274,246, just $678 below the original amount.
  • Resolution 2026-156 moved $100,000 out of RVAC into other district lines; Resolution 2026-470 added $99,322 back, including $80,000 from fund balance. Resolution 2026-842 later added $45,000 from fund balance for temporary Armory repairs.
  • The Ambulance District is separate from the General Fund. These resolutions do not show a $450,000 General Fund shortfall; they document a specific correction to the district’s RVAC contract line.
  • The 2026 budget also contains $450,000 for a transfer to General Fund debt service and $190,000 for the RVAC & Improvements Reserve. Neither line is itself the RVAC service-contract payment.
  • The available budget and resolutions do not establish the final amount paid to RVAC, explain the negative YTD medical-billing figure, establish the current reserve balance after all transactions, or show whether further adjustments occurred after the records reviewed. Those require year-end ledger / audited actuals and the full contract-payment ledger.
