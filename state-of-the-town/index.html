State of the Town and inaugural addresses

Why these speeches matter, what the law asks of a Supervisor, and what each Riverhead Supervisor did from 2010 to November 7, 2026.

Independent, unofficial project·Data last refreshed November 11, 2026·Recently refreshed·See data update details →

In plain English

Riverhead had a State of the Town address in 13 of the 17 years from 2010 to 2026. No State law requires one, or an inaugural speech: both are customs. This site found no address in 2017, 2020, 2025, 2026. For 2026, that is as of November 7, 2026.
  • Inaugural address: the remarks a Supervisor gives when sworn in, usually on January 1, setting out what they mean to do.
  • State of the Town: a yearly speech in which the Supervisor reports how the Town is doing and what comes next.
  • Annual financial report: the report on the Town’s money that State law does require the Supervisor to file each year.

Why these speeches matter

An inaugural address is a Supervisor’s first public statement of goals. A State of the Town address is the moment, once a year, when the Supervisor tells residents in one place how the Town is doing and what they plan to do next. Together they give residents something to measure later votes and budgets against.

Neither speech is a financial statement, and neither is checked by anyone before it is given. A claim in a speech is a claim; the budget, the audited statements and the Town Board’s resolutions are the record. This site’s Supervisor’s Promises page checks the current Supervisor’s claims that way.

What the law requires, and what it does not

The Governor must give one

“The governor shall communicate by message to the legislature at every session the condition of the state, and recommend such matters to it as he or she shall judge expedient.”

New York State Constitution, Article IV, § 3

A town supervisor must file a report, not give a speech

Town Law § 29(10) requires the supervisor to file an annual financial report with the town clerk within thirty days after the fiscal year ends; under § 29(10-a) a town board may instead have the supervisor file a copy of the annual report sent to the State Comptroller.

New York Town Law § 29, subdivisions 10 and 10-a. The Town’s report is on this site’s Annual Report page.

This site found no State law that requires a town supervisor to give a 'State of the Town' address or an inaugural speech. In Riverhead both are customs.

How the custom changed

Before 2018 the address was given at a joint dinner meeting of the Riverhead Rotary, Kiwanis and Lions clubs, held at local restaurants.

Laura Jens-Smith moved it to Town Hall in 2018, free to attend, broadcast on the public-access channel and livestreamed.

What each Supervisor did

Jerome (Jerry) Halpin (D), 2026–

Sworn in: January 1, 2026, Town Hall

Sworn in as the 65th Supervisor at a packed Town Hall. His first salary resolution set the Supervisor's 2026 pay at $110,000, "reduced from budgeted amount of $118,919." At the January 6 meeting he twice pointed back to the speech, saying it would be "a little clunky at first" and that "we could look for the negative in everything."

  • Town record: Town Board Resolution 2026-1, January 6, 2026: Sets Salaries of Elected Officials for the Year 2026
  • Machine transcript: Machine transcript of the January 6, 2026 Town Board meeting (this site)
  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, January 2, 2026: Swearing-in ushers in new Riverhead Town administration on New Year's Day
  • No State of the Town address found in 2026, as of November 7.

Tim Hubbard (R), 2024–2025

Sworn in: January 1, 2024, the new Town Hall

Sworn in as the 64th Supervisor in the new Town Hall, pledging transparency, civility and action. At the next Board meeting he said the ceremony "is on channel 22 if you haven't seen it" and that he "felt it was appropriate to do it in the new building."

  • Town record: Town Board minutes, January 3, 2024 (announcements)
  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, January 2, 2024: A new day in Riverhead: Tim Hubbard, pledging transparency, civility and action, is sworn in
State of the Town: April 9, 2024, Town Hall, 11 a.m.

Given on his 100th day in office: the first three months and the work ahead.

  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, April 9, 2024: Supervisor Tim Hubbard, on his 100th day in office, delivers 'State of the Town' speech
  • No address found for 2025.

Yvette Aguiar (R), 2020–2023

Sworn in: January 1, 2020, Suffolk Theater

Sworn in as the 63rd Supervisor at the Suffolk Theater, the only official that day to give a speech, urging residents to work together rather than rely on government alone. At the next Board meeting she said "We had over two, three hundred and fifty people at the theater." Sworn in for a second term on January 2, 2022 at a scaled-back ceremony at Town Hall, with a seven-minute prepared address.

  • Town record: Town Board minutes, January 7, 2020 (announcements)
  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, January 1, 2020: Riverhead officials sworn into office in ceremonies at the Suffolk Theater
  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, January 2, 2022: Aguiar sworn in for second term as supervisor at Riverhead Town Hall
State of the Town: February 8, 2021, Town Hall, by invitation only because of the pandemic

Her first address, given at Town Hall to an invited audience because of the pandemic.

  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, February 1, 2021: Riverhead supervisor will give State of the Town address Feb. 8 at Town Hall
State of the Town: June 29, 2022, Town Hall, livestreamed

Planned for the service clubs' dinner on June 8 ($35 a ticket), then rescheduled and given at Town Hall. Two years of accomplishments, downtown revitalization and the sale of Town land at EPCAL.

  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL topic page 'State of the Town', listing the June 30, 2022 article: Aguiar sets forth accomplishments over past two years
State of the Town: December 4, 2023

Her final address, announced to "tout accomplishments" of her four years.

  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, November 24, 2023: Supervisor announces final 'State of the Town' speech
  • Town record: Town of Riverhead news release: Supervisor Yvette Aguiar to Deliver Final State of the Town Address

Laura Jens-Smith (D), 2018–2019

Sworn in: January 1, 2018

Sworn in as the Town's 62nd Supervisor and its first woman in the office; called for unity, cooperation and perseverance in Town government.

  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, January 1, 2018: History is made in Riverhead: Jens-Smith sworn in as first woman town supervisor
State of the Town: April 10, 2018, Town Hall meeting room, free and open to the public

Moved the address out of the service clubs' dinner to a free public forum; standing room only, broadcast live and streamed. Her message: "Riverhead can do better."

  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, April 11, 2018: Jens-Smith's first State of the Town: Riverhead can do better
State of the Town: April 8, 2019, Town Hall, 6:30 p.m.

Her second address, again at Town Hall.

  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, March 18, 2019: Jens-Smith announces 'State of the Town' address at town hall April 8

Sean Walter (R), 2010–2017

Sworn in: January 1, 2010

Took office on January 1, 2010, setting out three goals: fiscal health, downtown revitalization and redeveloping the Enterprise Park at Calverton (EPCAL).

  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, December 31, 2017: A look back at eight years of Sean Walter
State of the Town: February 2010, Joint dinner of the Rotary, Kiwanis and Lions clubs

His first address; he gave one every year through 2016, each restating his three goals.

  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, December 31, 2017: A look back at eight years of Sean Walter
State of the Town: February 25, 2015, Joint meeting of the Riverhead Kiwanis, Rotary and Lions clubs

Proposed merging the elected tax receiver into the Town Clerk's office and replacing the elected Board of Assessors with appointed assessors, which he said would save $150,000 a year, and one combined highway, garage and State DOT yard at EPCAL to save another $150,000. He predicted the first proposal would "be met with an uproar."

  • News coverage: RiverheadLOCAL, February 25, 2015: Walter in 'State of Town' to call for elimination of elected tax receiver, assessor positions
State of the Town: March 2, 2016

His last address, recorded on video.

  • Video: Riverhead State of the Town 2016 (YouTube)
  • No address in 2017, his final year.
  • Walter was re-elected three times; this site has not found coverage of his 2012, 2014 and 2016 swearing-ins, so the timeline marks only 2010.

What makes an address useful to residents

4 of the 4 Supervisors before the current one gave at least one State of the Town address. Nothing in State law says what one must contain. These are practices that would make any Supervisor’s address easier for residents to check and use. They are this site’s suggestions, not a rule.

  • Give it after the budget is adopted. The Town Board adopts the next year’s budget each November. An address early in the year can explain decisions that are final, not proposals that may still change.
  • State the hard numbers first. The tax rate, the levy, any use of savings and any new borrowing, each matching the adopted budget and the annual financial report.
  • Publish the full text, with sources. This site has not found the text of any address posted as a Town record. Posting it, with each figure tied to its document, lets residents check it without relying on a transcript or a news report.
  • Measure the year against the inaugural goals. Say which goals from the swearing-in were met, which slipped and why. Hubbard’s 2024 address, given on his 100th day, reviewed his first three months in office.
  • Keep it open. Since 2018 the address has been given at Town Hall, free to attend and livestreamed, rather than at a ticketed dinner.
  • Residents can ask for one. Any resident may ask the Supervisor, at a Town Board meeting or in writing, when the next address will be. The record above shows it is a custom every recent Supervisor has kept, not something the law requires.

Likely concerns for residents

  • No State of the Town address was found for 2025 or 2026. Residents have had no yearly account from the Supervisor in one place since April 2024. This site may have missed one; it would add any it is shown.
  • The Town does not publish the text of these speeches. Most of what is known about them comes from news coverage, which is not a Town record, and from meeting video and machine transcripts, which can mishear words.
  • A speech is not checked before it is given. Compare what a Supervisor says with the budget, the annual report and the Board’s votes.
