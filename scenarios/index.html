What-if scenarios

Play out choices the Town could make — starting with the big one for next year: can it fund services without blowing past the tax cap? These are illustrations for thinking, not Town policy.

Independent project·Updated October 10, 2026·Current·Check data freshness →
The Town’s 2027 Tentative Budget is out. It proposes a town-wide tax levy of $62,882,202, up 2.79% from 2026, for the General Fund, Highway and Street Lighting. With the special districts, the levy is $67,159,863, up 2.78% from 2026 and $1,807,211 less than this site’s forecast of $68,967,074. The Supervisor’s letter says every taxing district is within the tax cap limit of 2.79%, and no district’s levy rises by more than that. The sliders below still start from the forecast and a 2% line. At the 2.79% limit the letter gives, the ceiling would be about $67,167,035 and the forecast’s gap about $1,800,039. See how it compares →

Other things the Town could weigh

Using a one-time surplus

What should the Town do with a surplus?

The audit has the General Fund adding $4,899,127 to its balance in 2025, ending the year with about $33.3M. A windfall can hold taxes down for a year, top up the rainy-day fund, or fix up parks and replace vehicles — but one-time money spent on recurring costs just reopens the hole next year.

Workforce turnover

What does the retirement buyout really save?

The buyout looks like big salary savings, but the honest number is smaller: ranked police retirements trigger promotion chains (the real saving lands on a rookie at the bottom, ~$95k each), and every retiree keeps ~$17k/yr of lifetime health coverage. Net realistic savings land well below the headline.

Debt & big projects

How much is the Town borrowing against the future?

Riverhead has used budget adjustments to pay down the Town Square bond-anticipation note and a 2018 refunding bond, and its debt sits at 5.48% of the legal limit. Neither resolution states an amount, so the paydowns cannot be sized from the record. But debt service and big capital projects compete directly with the operating budget for the same tax dollars.
