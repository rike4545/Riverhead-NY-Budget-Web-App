Other things the Town could weigh

Using a one-time surplus

What should the Town do with a surplus?

The General Fund ended 2025 with a $5,003,327 surplus and about $33.4M in reserves. A windfall can hold taxes down for a year, top up the rainy-day fund, or fix up parks and replace vehicles — but one-time money spent on recurring costs just reopens the hole next year.

Workforce turnover

What does the retirement buyout really save?

The buyout looks like big salary savings, but the honest number is smaller: ranked police retirements trigger promotion chains (the real saving lands on a rookie at the bottom, ~$95k each), and every retiree keeps ~$17k/yr of lifetime health coverage. Net realistic savings land well below the headline.

Debt & big projects

How much is the Town borrowing against the future?

Riverhead has used budget adjustments to pay down the Town Square bond-anticipation note and a 2018 refunding bond, and its debt sits at 6.74% of the legal limit. Neither resolution states an amount, so the paydowns cannot be sized from the record. But debt service and big capital projects compete directly with the operating budget for the same tax dollars.
